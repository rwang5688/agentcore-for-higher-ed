## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced engineering course, designated in honour of K.S. Venktesh of IIT Kanpur, examines the theoretical foundations and applied methodologies central to contemporary engineering systems design and analysis. Students engage with complex problem-solving frameworks drawn from established research traditions, integrating principles of systems engineering with computational and analytical tools relevant to modern engineering challenges. The course serves as a rigorous capstone-level offering within the College of Engineering, preparing students for professional practice and advanced scholarly inquiry.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate mastery of advanced engineering systems principles and apply them to the modelling and analysis of complex, real-world engineering problems
- Critically evaluate existing research and design frameworks, identifying limitations and proposing evidence-based improvements
- Develop and defend original engineering solutions through structured methodological inquiry and quantitative reasoning
- Integrate interdisciplinary knowledge across engineering sub-disciplines to synthesise coherent and innovative design outcomes
- Communicate technical findings effectively through formal written reports and oral presentations consistent with professional engineering standards

### Prerequisites

Given the 935-level designation, students must have completed the following prior to enrolment:

- ENGI-6200: Advanced Systems Modelling and Simulation
- ENGI-6450: Engineering Analysis and Optimisation Methods
- MATH-5310: Graduate-Level Applied Mathematics
- Completion of at least 80 credit points at the postgraduate level within the College of Engineering
- Demonstrated research competency as assessed by the Department of Engineering, or approval of the course coordinator

### Assessment Methods

**Research Dissertation Component (40%)**
Students will produce an extended original research paper (8,000–12,000 words) addressing a substantive engineering problem, incorporating a comprehensive literature review, novel analytical contribution, and discussion of practical implications.

**Design Project and Presentation (30%)**
Working individually or in approved pairs, students will develop an advanced engineering design solution, culminating in a formal oral defence before a panel drawn from the course instructional team.

**Critical Review Assignments (20%)**
Two structured critical reviews of seminal or contemporary engineering research papers will be submitted across the teaching period, each requiring rigorous evaluation of methodology, assumptions, and findings.

**Participation and Seminar Engagement (10%)**
Active, informed participation in weekly seminars led by members of the instructional team is expected and assessed. Students are required to lead at least one seminar discussion during the semester.

*Note: A minimum passing grade of 50% must be achieved across all components. Students failing the dissertation component are not eligible for a passing grade regardless of aggregate score.*
