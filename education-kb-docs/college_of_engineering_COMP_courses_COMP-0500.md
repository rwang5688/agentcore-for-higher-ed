### Course Description

Building upon the foundational principles established in Algorithms Part I, this course provides an advanced and rigorous examination of algorithm design paradigms, computational complexity theory, and the analysis of sophisticated data structures. Students will explore advanced topics including approximation algorithms, randomised methods, network flow, and NP-completeness, developing the theoretical and practical tools necessary to tackle computationally challenging real-world problems. The course places particular emphasis on formal proof techniques and the ability to critically evaluate algorithmic efficiency across diverse problem domains.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **Analyse and classify** computational problems within the framework of complexity theory, including reductions between NP-complete and NP-hard problems, and articulate the implications of these classifications for practical problem-solving.
- **Design and implement** advanced algorithmic strategies, including dynamic programming over complex state spaces, network flow algorithms, and linear programming relaxations, selecting appropriate techniques based on problem structure and performance requirements.
- **Construct and evaluate** approximation algorithms and randomised algorithms for intractable problems, formally bounding approximation ratios and expected runtimes using probabilistic and analytical methods.
- **Apply** advanced graph algorithms — including those for matching, cuts, and connectivity — to model and solve problems arising in engineering, optimisation, and computer science contexts.
- **Critically assess** the correctness and efficiency of novel algorithmic solutions through formal proof and asymptotic analysis, communicating findings clearly in both written and oral forms appropriate to a professional or research setting.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following, or obtained instructor consent:

- **COMP-0300 Algorithms Part I** — a grade of B or higher is strongly recommended
- **COMP-0280 Data Structures and Complexity** or equivalent
- **MATH-0310 Discrete Mathematics and Proof Writing**
- **MATH-0320 Probability and Statistics for Computer Science** (may be taken concurrently with instructor approval)

Students are expected to have a working proficiency in at least one high-level programming language (Python, Java, or C++ preferred) and to be comfortable with formal mathematical reasoning, including induction, contradiction, and asymptotic notation. Students who have not completed COMP-0300 but hold equivalent background from transfer credit or prior study should consult the department registrar before enrolling.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Sets (×5) | 30% | Five individual written problem sets distributed throughout the semester, each requiring formal proofs, algorithm design, and complexity analysis |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | In-class closed-book examination covering Weeks 1–7 |
| Algorithm Design Project | 25% | A semester-long group project (groups of 2–3) requiring students to identify an open or applied algorithmic problem, design and implement a solution, and present findings in both a written report and a live presentation |
| Final Examination | 20% | Comprehensive closed-book examination covering all course material |
| Participation & Recitation | 5% | Active engagement in weekly recitation sections, assessed by the assistant and associate instructors |

*Problem sets must be typeset in LaTeX and submitted via the course portal by 11:59 PM on the stated due date. Late submissions will be penalised at a rate of 10% per 24-hour period unless an extension has been approved in advance by the supervising instructor.*

*The Algorithm Design Project requires an interim progress report submitted at the midpoint of the semester. Full rubrics and submission guidelines will be distributed during Week 1.*
