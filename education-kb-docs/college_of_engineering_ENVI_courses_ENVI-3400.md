### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive examination of the ecological relationships between wildlife species and their environments, with emphasis on population dynamics, habitat use, and community interactions across diverse ecosystems. Students will explore the theoretical foundations of wildlife ecology alongside applied methodologies used in field research, monitoring, and conservation management. Special attention is given to the impacts of anthropogenic activity on wildlife systems and the role of ecological science in informing resource management policy.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain and apply core ecological principles including population dynamics, trophic interactions, and species-habitat relationships to real-world wildlife systems
- Design and evaluate field-based methodologies for wildlife population monitoring, including mark-recapture, transect surveys, and remote sensing techniques
- Analyse the effects of habitat fragmentation, climate change, and human-wildlife conflict on biodiversity and ecosystem function
- Interpret and critically assess primary scientific literature in the field of wildlife ecology, communicating findings effectively in both written and oral formats
- Apply ecological data to develop evidence-based recommendations for wildlife conservation and natural resource management scenarios

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | In-class written examination covering core theoretical content from Weeks 1–7 |
| Field Methods Practical Report | 25% | Individual written report based on supervised field data collection exercises; approximately 2,500 words |
| Research Paper | 30% | A 3,500–4,500 word analytical paper examining a wildlife management case study selected in consultation with the supervising instructor |
| Final Examination | 20% | Cumulative written examination; includes short-answer and case-based analysis questions |
| Participation & Lab Engagement | 5% | Active engagement in weekly laboratory/field sessions and seminar discussions |

Students are expected to attend all scheduled field sessions. Absences from field activities must be approved in advance by the supervising instructor and may require alternative arrangements. Late submission penalties of 5% per business day apply to all written assessments unless a formal accommodation has been granted.
