### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to computational methods used to model and simulate materials at the atomic and molecular scale, with emphasis on the physical principles underpinning modern simulation techniques. Students will explore a range of methodologies—including molecular dynamics, Monte Carlo methods, and density functional theory—and apply these tools to investigate structural, thermodynamic, and mechanical properties of real materials systems. The course integrates theoretical foundations with substantial hands-on computational practice, preparing students to critically evaluate and contribute to materials research in both academic and industrial contexts.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **Explain and apply** the fundamental theoretical frameworks underlying atomistic simulation techniques, including classical force fields, statistical mechanics, and quantum mechanical approximations relevant to materials modeling.
- **Design and execute** molecular dynamics and Monte Carlo simulations using industry-standard computational software, selecting appropriate algorithms, boundary conditions, and ensemble parameters for a given materials problem.
- **Critically evaluate** interatomic potential models and assess their suitability, accuracy, and limitations when applied to metallic, ceramic, polymeric, and composite material systems.
- **Analyze and interpret** simulation output data—including radial distribution functions, mean square displacement, stress-strain curves, and phonon spectra—connecting computed results to experimentally observable material properties.
- **Communicate scientific findings** effectively through structured written reports and oral presentations, demonstrating the ability to contextualize atomistic modeling results within broader materials science and engineering literature.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-0300 must have successfully completed the following or have written instructor approval:

- **ENGI-0201: Introduction to Materials Science and Engineering** — foundational knowledge of crystal structures, bonding, and material properties is assumed throughout this course.
- **MATH-0202: Differential Equations and Linear Algebra** — proficiency in calculus-based mathematics and matrix operations is essential for understanding simulation algorithms and data analysis techniques.
- **PHYS-0211: Thermodynamics and Statistical Mechanics** — students must be comfortable with concepts such as ensembles, partition functions, and thermodynamic potentials, which form the theoretical basis of many atomistic methods.
- **COMP-0150: Introduction to Scientific Programming** (or demonstrated programming proficiency in Python, MATLAB, or equivalent) — all simulation work in this course requires the ability to write, modify, and debug scripts for data processing and visualization.

*Note: Students without the programming prerequisite are strongly encouraged to complete the College of Engineering's self-directed Python Readiness Module prior to the start of term.*

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Sets (×5) | 20% | Weekly computational assignments reinforcing lecture material and developing simulation skills |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written examination covering theoretical foundations, algorithm design, and interpretation of simulation data |
| Simulation Laboratory Reports (×3) | 25% | Structured lab reports documenting simulation design, methodology, results, and critical analysis for three prescribed materials systems |
| Final Research Project | 25% | An independent or paired project in which students select a materials problem, design and conduct an original atomistic simulation study, and submit a full technical report (minimum 3,000 words) |
| Final Oral Presentation | 10% | A 15-minute presentation of the final research project to peers and course instructors, including a question-and-answer period |

*All simulation laboratory work is conducted using the Peculiar U High-Performance Computing Cluster. Students will receive cluster access credentials during the first week of term. Late submission penalties of 5% per calendar day apply to all written components unless prior accommodation has been arranged.*
