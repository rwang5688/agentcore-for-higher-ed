## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-3900: Charles Harvey provides an advanced examination of the engineering principles, methodologies, and innovations attributed to the work and legacy of Charles Harvey, with particular emphasis on his contributions to urban infrastructure and elevated railway systems. Students will investigate the historical, mechanical, and structural dimensions of Harvey's engineering achievements, situating them within broader contexts of nineteenth-century industrial development and modern civil engineering practice. The course bridges historical case study analysis with contemporary engineering evaluation, equipping students to critically assess legacy infrastructure and apply lessons from pioneering engineering projects to modern design challenges.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the structural and mechanical engineering principles underlying elevated rail and urban transit systems pioneered during the industrial era
- Evaluate Charles Harvey's contributions to engineering history and assess their lasting influence on urban infrastructure design
- Apply critical engineering judgment to the assessment of legacy and heritage infrastructure systems
- Demonstrate the ability to synthesize historical engineering documentation with modern structural analysis techniques
- Communicate technical findings effectively through written reports and oral presentations consistent with professional engineering standards

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Structural Case Study Report | 20% |
| Group Infrastructure Analysis Project | 25% |
| Weekly Response Journals | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess students' comprehension of core engineering concepts, historical context, and analytical frameworks covered throughout the course. Examinations include both short-answer and extended technical response questions.

**Structural Case Study Report** requires each student to independently research and evaluate a selected historical infrastructure project, producing a formal engineering report of approximately 2,500–3,500 words that incorporates technical drawings, load analysis, and material assessment.

**Group Infrastructure Analysis Project** challenges student teams to apply course methodologies to a contemporary urban engineering scenario, drawing parallels between historical precedents and modern infrastructure challenges. Teams will submit a written report and deliver a formal oral presentation during the final two weeks of term.

**Weekly Response Journals** are short reflective submissions (300–500 words) in which students engage critically with assigned readings and lectures, demonstrating ongoing synthesis of course material.

### Required Materials

Students are expected to obtain the following texts prior to the first week of class:

- Vonderhaar, C. L. (2021). *Structural Legacies: Case Studies in Nineteenth-Century Civil Engineering*. Harmon & Bridle Academic Publishers. ISBN 978-0-5521-8847-9
- Imholt, T. & Salgeira, P. R. (2016). *Foundations of Infrastructure Analysis: Historical and Modern Perspectives* (3rd ed.). Queensbury Engineering Series. ISBN 978-1-4832-5563-0

A supplementary course reader, compiled by the instructional team and containing primary source documents, technical schematics, and selected journal articles, will be made available through the university's online course portal at no additional cost. Students are also expected to have access to standard structural analysis software (AutoCAD and SAP2000); licensed versions are available through the College of Engineering computing laboratories.
