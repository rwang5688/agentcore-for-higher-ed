## Course Description

### Course Description

Engineering Statics provides a rigorous introduction to the analysis of forces and moments acting on rigid bodies in equilibrium. Students will develop foundational skills in vector mechanics, free-body diagram construction, and structural analysis, with applications drawn from real-world civil, mechanical, and materials engineering contexts. This course serves as a critical gateway to advanced study in mechanics of materials, structural analysis, and dynamic systems.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of ENGI-3000, students will be able to:

- Apply the principles of vector mathematics and Newton's laws to analyze two- and three-dimensional force systems acting on rigid bodies in static equilibrium
- Construct accurate free-body diagrams and use equilibrium equations to determine unknown forces and reactions in structures, trusses, frames, and machines
- Calculate centroids, centers of gravity, and moments of inertia for composite bodies and cross-sectional areas relevant to engineering design
- Analyze internal forces, shear, and bending moment distributions in beams and structural members
- Demonstrate proficiency in applying systematic, methodical problem-solving approaches consistent with professional engineering standards and practice

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrollment in ENGI-3000:

- **MATH-1200: Calculus I** (minimum grade of C)
- **PHYS-1100: General Physics I: Mechanics** (minimum grade of C)
- **ENGI-1050: Introduction to Engineering Principles** or departmental consent

Students are strongly encouraged to be concurrently enrolled in **MATH-1250: Calculus II**, as familiarity with integration techniques significantly supports course material.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination I | 20% |
| Midterm Examination II | 20% |
| Final Examination | 30% |
| Problem Sets (weekly) | 20% |
| Truss and Frame Design Project | 10% |

**Examinations** are administered in-person and are closed-book, though students are permitted one hand-written reference sheet (both sides). Examinations emphasize quantitative problem-solving and conceptual application rather than rote recall.

**Weekly Problem Sets** are assigned each Monday and due the following Monday before class. Sets consist of 6–10 problems of graduated difficulty, drawing from both worked examples and novel applications. Collaboration is permitted on problem sets; however, submitted work must be independently written and clearly show all solution steps.

**Truss and Frame Design Project** is a semester-long individual assignment in which students design, analyze, and report on a simple structural system of their own specification. The project culminates in a formal technical report submitted during the final two weeks of the semester. Assessment criteria include accuracy of analysis, quality of engineering communication, and adherence to professional report formatting standards.

### Required Materials

Students are required to obtain the following materials prior to the first week of instruction:

- **Kopman, R.-R., & Frauenhofer, L. (2021).** *Foundations of Engineering Statics: Equilibrium, Structures, and Vector Mechanics* (4th ed.). Harbridge Engineering Press. — Primary textbook; all problem set assignments reference this edition.

- **Greenbank, L., & Reamey, S. (2023).** *Engineering Statics Laboratory and Design Workbook.* Peculiar University Press. — Required for problem sets and the design project; available through the Peculiar U Campus Bookstore and the College of Engineering resource portal.

Students will also require access to a scientific calculator approved by the College of Engineering (a current list of approved models is available on the ENGI-3000 course page on the university learning management system). Graph paper, a ruler, and a protractor are recommended for diagram work.
