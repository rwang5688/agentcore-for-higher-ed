## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-6500 provides an advanced treatment of the mathematical foundations essential to computer science, with emphasis on discrete structures, combinatorial reasoning, and probabilistic analysis. Students will explore topics including graph theory, formal logic, set theory, number theory, combinatorics, and both classical and Bayesian probability frameworks. The course is designed to develop rigorous mathematical thinking and equip students with analytical tools applicable to algorithm design, cryptography, data structures, and machine learning theory.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Construct and evaluate formal mathematical proofs using techniques such as induction, contradiction, and direct reasoning, and apply these methods to problems in discrete mathematics
- Analyse combinatorial structures and apply counting principles, generating functions, and recurrence relations to solve complex computational problems
- Model real-world systems using graph-theoretic frameworks, including trees, directed graphs, and network flows, and apply relevant algorithms to optimise and interpret these models
- Apply classical, conditional, and Bayesian probability theory to reason about uncertainty in computational systems, including Markov chains and probabilistic algorithms
- Integrate discrete mathematical and probabilistic concepts to evaluate the correctness, efficiency, and reliability of algorithms and computational processes

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrolment in COMP-6500:

- **COMP-4210: Data Structures and Algorithm Analysis** or equivalent, with a grade of C or above
- **MATH-4100: Linear Algebra for Computing** or equivalent
- **MATH-3050: Introductory Probability and Statistics** or equivalent
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or written instructor approval

Students who have not completed formal coursework in proof-based mathematics are strongly encouraged to consult with the course coordinator prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 total) | 30% | Weekly or biweekly written assignments covering lecture material; solutions must demonstrate formal proof-writing and analytical reasoning |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book, in-person examination covering discrete structures, logic, combinatorics, and introductory probability |

| Group Project | 15% | A collaborative computational project requiring students to implement and analyse a probabilistic or graph-theoretic algorithm; presented to the class in the final two weeks of term |
| Final Examination | 15% | A comprehensive, closed-book examination; students may bring one handwritten formula sheet (A4, double-sided) |

*A minimum aggregate score of 50% is required to pass the course. Students must attempt all assessment components to be eligible for a passing grade.*

### Required Materials

Students are expected to obtain the following texts prior to the first week of class:

- **Harwell, D. T. & Cressmore, A. J.** (2021). *Foundations of Discrete Mathematics for Computer Scientists* (4th ed.). Aldenmoor Academic Press. — Primary textbook; covers logic, set theory, graph theory, combinatorics, and number theory across the full course.

- **Pashkevich, R. M.** (2019). *Probability, Randomness, and Computation: A Graduate Introduction* (2nd ed.). Northgate University Press. — Supplementary text for the probability and Markov chain modules; chapters assigned will be listed in the course schedule.

- **Vrindley, C. & Samuels-Howe, T.** (2022). *Proof Techniques and Mathematical Reasoning in Computing* (1st ed.). Ironfield Publishing. — Reference text for proof methodology; particularly recommended for students seeking additional support in formal reasoning.

All required readings, lecture slides, and supplementary materials will be made available through the Peculiar U Learning Management System (LMS). Students are advised to bring a copy of Harwell & Cressmore to all in-person sessions.
