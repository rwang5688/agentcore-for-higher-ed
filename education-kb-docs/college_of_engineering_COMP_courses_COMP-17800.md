## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the principles, technologies, and applications of virtual reality (VR), exploring the foundational concepts of immersive environment design, human-computer interaction within three-dimensional spaces, and the underlying hardware and software systems that power modern VR platforms. Students will examine the perceptual and cognitive factors that contribute to presence and immersion, alongside the ethical and accessibility considerations inherent in VR development. Practical laboratory sessions complement theoretical instruction, enabling students to engage directly with industry-standard tools and development pipelines.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the core principles of virtual reality, including rendering pipelines, stereoscopic display, spatial audio, and motion tracking technologies.
- Design and prototype basic interactive virtual environments using contemporary VR development frameworks and engines.
- Analyze the human perceptual and physiological factors—such as cybersickness, field of view, and latency—that influence user experience in immersive systems.
- Evaluate existing VR applications across domains including healthcare, education, engineering simulation, and entertainment, critically assessing their design choices and effectiveness.
- Apply foundational programming and scripting techniques to implement interactive object behaviors and user interface elements within a VR context.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Laboratory Assignments (6 total) | 30% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Quizzes (weekly, lowest score dropped) | 10% |
| Group VR Prototype Project | 25% |
| Final Written Examination | 15% |

**Laboratory Assignments:** Six structured lab exercises guide students through progressively complex VR development tasks, from environment construction and asset integration to physics simulation and user interaction scripting. Labs are completed individually and submitted via the department's online portal.

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations assess theoretical understanding of course material, including hardware architecture, rendering concepts, perceptual psychology, and design principles. The final examination is cumulative.

**Weekly Quizzes:** Short formative quizzes administered at the beginning of each lecture session reinforce assigned readings and prior lecture content. The single lowest quiz score will be automatically dropped at semester's end.

**Group VR Prototype Project:** Working in teams of three to four, students will conceive, design, and develop an original VR application addressing a defined real-world problem or creative challenge. The project culminates in a live demonstration and a written technical report. Peer evaluation contributes a portion of each student's individual project grade.
