---

This course provides graduate-level students with a rigorous foundation in computational thinking principles and their direct application to real-world data science problems, emphasizing algorithmic reasoning, structured problem decomposition, and data-driven decision-making. Students will engage with core data science methodologies—including data acquisition, cleaning, visualization, and introductory machine learning concepts—through both theoretical frameworks and applied programming exercises. The course bridges the gap between abstract computational reasoning and practical data analysis, preparing students for advanced study in computer science, data engineering, and applied analytics.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a systematic understanding of computational thinking paradigms, including decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithmic design, and apply these paradigms to solve complex, open-ended data problems
- Design and implement data processing pipelines using industry-standard programming tools and libraries, performing data ingestion, transformation, exploratory analysis, and visualization with proficiency and critical awareness of methodological limitations
- Evaluate and apply foundational statistical and machine learning techniques—including regression, classification, and clustering—to structured and semi-structured datasets, interpreting results within appropriate ethical and contextual frameworks
- Critically assess the quality, provenance, and representational fairness of datasets, articulating the implications of data bias, missing values, and sampling methodology on downstream analytical outcomes
- Communicate the findings of computational and data-driven investigations clearly and persuasively to both technical and non-technical audiences through written reports, visual presentations, and live demonstrations

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (7835), students are expected to have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency at the time of enrolment:

- **COMP-4200: Foundations of Programming and Algorithmic Logic** or demonstrated proficiency in at least one high-level programming language (Python strongly recommended)
- **MATH-4150: Applied Statistics and Probability** or an equivalent undergraduate-level statistics course with a grade of B or higher
- **COMP-4500: Data Structures and Computational Complexity** or equivalent preparation in algorithmic analysis
- Admission to a graduate program within the College of Engineering or written departmental approval from one of the supervising instructors

Students who are uncertain about their preparation are encouraged to consult with the course coordinator prior to registration. A preparatory bridging module is available through the College of Engineering for students with identified gaps in programming background.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through a combination of individual and collaborative assessments designed to reflect both theoretical mastery and practical competence:

- **Problem Sets (20%)** — Five structured problem sets distributed throughout the semester, each targeting a specific computational or statistical concept covered in lecture. Problem sets are completed individually and are designed to reinforce and extend material from weekly sessions.
- **Midterm Examination (20%)** — A closed-book written examination assessing conceptual understanding of computational thinking frameworks, algorithmic logic, and foundational data science theory. The examination is held in week seven and covers all material from weeks one through six.
- **Data Analysis Project — Part I: Proposal and Exploratory Report (15%)** — Students form groups of two to three and select a publicly available dataset to investigate. This component requires a formal written proposal outlining the research question, dataset description, ethical considerations, and preliminary exploratory analysis with visualizations.
- **Data Analysis Project — Part II: Final Report and Presentation (30%)** — Continuation of the group project, culminating in a comprehensive written report detailing the full analytical workflow, methodology, results, limitations, and conclusions, accompanied by a 15-minute group presentation to the class and panel of instructors.
- **Final Examination (15%)** — A take-home examination distributed in the final week of instruction, requiring students to analyse a novel dataset and produce a structured analytical response demonstrating mastery of all course competencies. Submissions are individual and must reflect original work.

Late submissions will be penalised at a rate of 5% per business day unless an extension has been formally approved. Academic integrity policies of Peculiar University apply to all assessments without exception.
