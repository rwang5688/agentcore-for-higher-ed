### Course Description

This advanced course investigates the theoretical frameworks and applied methodologies underpinning education for sustainable living, with a focused emphasis on environmental systems, ecological responsibility, and the human dimensions of environmental change. Students engage critically with contemporary sustainability challenges — including resource depletion, climate adaptation, and biodiversity loss — through the lens of educational theory and community-based practice. Drawing on interdisciplinary perspectives, the course equips students to design, evaluate, and implement sustainability education initiatives within professional, institutional, and community contexts.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically analyse the relationship between environmental sustainability principles and educational theory, identifying how learning frameworks can drive systemic ecological change.
- Evaluate current environmental challenges — including climate change, land degradation, freshwater scarcity, and ecosystem disruption — using evidence-based and systems-thinking approaches.
- Design and assess sustainability education programmes suited to diverse audiences, including professional organisations, schools, and community groups.
- Synthesise research from environmental science, policy studies, and pedagogy to develop coherent, actionable sustainability strategies in real-world settings.
- Demonstrate ethical reasoning and cultural sensitivity when addressing global-local environmental tensions in educational and professional practice.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-0700 are expected to have completed:

- A minimum of one prior Level 500 or Level 600 course in environmental studies, natural resource management, sustainability, or a closely related discipline.
- Foundational knowledge of environmental science concepts (equivalent to ENVI-0500 or ENVI-0600, or instructor approval).
- Graduate-level standing or enrolment in a recognised postgraduate programme within the College of Engineering or a cognate faculty.

Students lacking formal prerequisites but possessing substantial professional experience in environmental management, education, or sustainability practice may seek enrolment by permission of the course coordinators.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weighting | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Sustainability Education Programme Design | 35% | A major project in which students design a comprehensive, evidence-based sustainability education initiative for a specified environmental context or community. Submitted in written report format with supporting materials. |
| Critical Literature Review | 25% | A 4,000–5,000 word scholarly review examining current literature on environmental education, sustainability pedagogy, and ecological behaviour change. |
| Case Study Analysis | 20% | Students select and critically evaluate a real-world sustainability education programme, assessing its effectiveness, scalability, and alignment with environmental objectives. Presented to the cohort in a structured seminar format. |
| Participation and Reflective Portfolio | 20% | An ongoing portfolio documenting students' critical reflections on course readings, field discussions, and guest lectures. Assessed for depth of engagement, intellectual development, and integration of interdisciplinary perspectives. |

All assessments must adhere to Peculiar University's academic integrity and referencing standards. Late submissions are subject to the University's standard penalty policy unless an extension is formally approved.
