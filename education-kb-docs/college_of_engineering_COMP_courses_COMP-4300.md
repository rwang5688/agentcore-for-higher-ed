This course provides a rigorous and comprehensive introduction to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and their application to computer vision, image recognition, and related domains within modern machine learning. Students will develop a deep theoretical and practical understanding of CNN architectures, training methodologies, and optimization strategies, with emphasis placed on real-world implementation using industry-standard frameworks. Topics include foundational deep learning principles, convolutional operations, pooling mechanisms, regularization, transfer learning, and contemporary architectural advances such as ResNets, Inception modules, and attention-augmented networks.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the mathematical foundations of convolutional operations, including kernel design, feature map generation, stride, padding, and the role of activation functions within multilayer CNN architectures.
- Design, implement, and train convolutional neural network models using deep learning frameworks, demonstrating proficiency in constructing pipelines for data preprocessing, augmentation, and evaluation.
- Critically analyze and compare established CNN architectures — including LeNet, AlexNet, VGGNet, ResNet, and EfficientNet — assessing the trade-offs between model complexity, computational cost, and performance.
- Apply transfer learning and fine-tuning techniques to adapt pre-trained CNN models to novel classification and detection tasks, particularly in resource-constrained or limited-data scenarios.
- Diagnose and address common challenges in CNN training, including overfitting, vanishing/exploding gradients, and class imbalance, employing appropriate regularization and optimization strategies.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment | Weight |
|---|---|
| Programming Assignments (4 total) | 35% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Research & Implementation Project | 15% |
| Participation & Lab Exercises | 5% |

**Programming Assignments** are completed individually and require students to implement CNN components and full architectures from scratch and using established frameworks, addressing tasks such as image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation.

**Midterm and Final Examinations** are closed-book assessments covering theoretical concepts, architectural design, mathematical derivations, and analytical problem-solving. A formula sheet will be provided.

**Research & Implementation Project** is a semester-long team project (groups of two to three students) in which students select a domain application, review relevant literature, design and train a CNN-based solution, and present findings in both a written technical report and a formal oral presentation during the final week of term. Projects are expected to demonstrate original problem framing and critical engagement with existing work.

**Lab Exercises** are completed during scheduled laboratory sessions and are assessed on a completion and engagement basis.
