## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-10300: Introduction to Cybersecurity provides students with a comprehensive foundation in the principles, technologies, and practices that underpin the protection of digital systems, networks, and data. Students will examine core concepts including threat modeling, cryptographic systems, network defense, and the ethical and legal frameworks governing information security. Through a combination of theoretical study and hands-on laboratory exercises, students develop the analytical and technical skills necessary to identify vulnerabilities, assess risk, and implement effective security countermeasures in real-world computing environments.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and classify common cybersecurity threats, attack vectors, and vulnerabilities across network, application, and operating system layers
- Apply fundamental cryptographic principles, including symmetric and asymmetric encryption, hashing, and public key infrastructure, to protect data integrity and confidentiality
- Design and evaluate basic network security architectures, including the use of firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and virtual private networks
- Conduct structured risk assessments and develop incident response plans aligned with industry-standard frameworks such as NIST and ISO/IEC 27001
- Articulate the ethical, legal, and societal responsibilities of cybersecurity professionals, including relevant legislation such as the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act and applicable data protection regulations

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in this course will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Assignments (6 total) | 25% |
| Security Audit Project | 20% |
| Participation & In-Class Exercises | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations are closed-book and will assess students' understanding of theoretical concepts, terminology, and applied problem-solving across all major course topics. The final examination is cumulative.

**Laboratory Assignments:** Six structured lab exercises conducted in the university's secure virtual computing environment will require students to perform tasks such as packet analysis using industry-standard tools, password auditing, firewall configuration, and basic penetration testing in sandboxed, legally controlled settings.

**Security Audit Project:** Working in groups of three to four, students will conduct a simulated security audit of a provided fictional organizational case study, culminating in a formal written report and a 15-minute oral presentation delivered to the class. The report must include a vulnerability assessment, threat model, risk matrix, and a set of actionable remediation recommendations.

**Participation and In-Class Exercises:** Students are expected to engage actively in discussions, case study analyses, and unannounced in-class exercises throughout the semester.
