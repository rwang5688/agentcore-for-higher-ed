## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-3300 Fluid Mechanics provides a rigorous introduction to the fundamental principles governing the behavior of fluids at rest and in motion, with applications to engineering systems and real-world design problems. Students will examine the physical and mathematical foundations of fluid statics, kinematics, and dynamics, including the analysis of internal and external flows. Emphasis is placed on applying governing equations — including continuity, momentum, and energy — to solve practical engineering problems in areas such as pipe flow, open-channel flow, and aerodynamics.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply the principles of fluid statics to calculate pressure distributions, buoyancy forces, and forces on submerged surfaces in both simple and complex geometries.
- Derive and utilize the continuity, Navier-Stokes, and Bernoulli equations to analyze steady and unsteady fluid flows in a variety of engineering contexts.
- Classify and characterize flow regimes using dimensionless parameters, including the Reynolds, Froude, and Mach numbers, and apply dimensional analysis and similitude to experimental and design problems.
- Analyze internal pipe flows and external boundary layer flows, including head loss calculations, friction factors, and the use of Moody diagrams for practical system design.
- Apply the principles of fluid mechanics to open-channel flow and introductory compressible flow scenarios, demonstrating the ability to integrate theoretical knowledge with engineering judgment.

---

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-3300 must have successfully completed the following courses with a grade of C or higher:

- **ENGI-2100: Statics and Dynamics** — foundational understanding of forces, equilibrium, and Newtonian mechanics is essential for analyzing fluid forces and momentum transfer.
- **MATH-2250: Differential Equations** — proficiency in ordinary and partial differential equations is required for derivation and application of governing flow equations.
- **MATH-2200: Calculus III (Multivariable Calculus)** — vector calculus concepts including divergence, gradient, and surface integrals are used extensively throughout the course.
- **PHYS-1120: University Physics II** — prior exposure to thermodynamic principles and wave phenomena supports understanding of compressible and viscous flow behavior.

Students who have not completed the above prerequisites must obtain written departmental approval prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-3300 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination I | 20% |
| Midterm Examination II | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Reports (4 reports) | 20% |
| Problem Sets (weekly) | 10% |
| Design Project | 5% |

**Examinations:** Three in-person, closed-book examinations will be administered over the course of the term. Students are permitted one double-sided formula reference sheet of their own preparation. Examinations will assess both theoretical understanding and quantitative problem-solving ability.

**Laboratory Reports:** Four structured laboratory sessions accompany the lecture curriculum, covering topics such as pipe flow resistance, flow measurement devices, Reynolds number visualization, and hydrostatic forces on submerged surfaces. Each lab requires a formal written report submitted within one week of the session date, adhering to departmental report formatting standards.

**Problem Sets:** Weekly problem sets reinforce lecture material and prepare students for examination conditions. Late submissions will be penalized 15% per day unless prior arrangements have been made with the supervising instructor.

**Design Project:** Working individually or in pairs (instructor-approved), students will complete a semester-long design project applying fluid mechanics principles to a practical engineering scenario, such as the design of a municipal water distribution network segment or a hydraulic turbine selection study. A final written report and brief oral presentation are required.
