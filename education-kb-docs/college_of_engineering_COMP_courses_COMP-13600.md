### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous examination of the principles, protocols, and practices underlying the security of modern computer networks, with emphasis on both theoretical foundations and applied defensive techniques. Students will explore a broad range of topics including cryptographic systems, intrusion detection, firewall architecture, secure network design, and contemporary threat landscapes. Through hands-on laboratory exercises and case-based analysis, students will develop the skills necessary to assess network vulnerabilities and implement effective security countermeasures in real-world environments.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and explain core concepts in network security, including authentication, authorization, confidentiality, integrity, and availability (the CIA triad), and articulate how these principles are applied in practical network environments.
- Analyze common network attack vectors — including man-in-the-middle attacks, denial-of-service, packet sniffing, and spoofing — and evaluate the effectiveness of appropriate defensive strategies and mitigation techniques.
- Design and evaluate secure network architectures incorporating firewalls, virtual private networks (VPNs), intrusion detection and prevention systems (IDS/IPS), and demilitarized zones (DMZs).
- Apply cryptographic principles, including symmetric and asymmetric encryption, public key infrastructure (PKI), and digital certificates, to the design and assessment of secure communication protocols.
- Conduct structured vulnerability assessments and basic penetration tests within controlled environments, producing professional-grade security reports with documented findings and remediation recommendations.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-13600 must have successfully completed the following courses or obtained instructor approval prior to registration:

- **COMP-11200: Introduction to Computer Networks** — foundational knowledge of network topologies, protocols (TCP/IP, UDP, DNS, HTTP), and the OSI model is assumed throughout this course.
- **COMP-10800: Principles of Operating Systems** — familiarity with operating system concepts, process management, and file system structures is required for laboratory components.
- **COMP-11850: Fundamentals of Cybersecurity** — students should have basic exposure to security concepts, including common threat categories and introductory risk assessment frameworks.

Students who have not completed the above prerequisites but possess equivalent demonstrated experience should consult with the course instructor before registering.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-13600 will be evaluated through a combination of written assessments, practical laboratory work, and a culminating project, as detailed below:

**Midterm Examination (20%)**
A written examination covering all lecture and assigned reading material from the first half of the course. The midterm will include multiple-choice questions, short-answer responses, and scenario-based analysis questions requiring students to apply theoretical concepts to realistic security situations.

**Final Examination (25%)**
A comprehensive written examination administered during the final examination period. The final exam will assess cumulative knowledge across all course modules, with particular emphasis on integrative problem-solving and the application of security frameworks discussed in the latter half of the course.

**Laboratory Assignments (25%)**
A series of six structured laboratory exercises completed individually or in pairs, using the university's dedicated secure network simulation environment. Labs will cover practical skills including packet analysis using network monitoring tools, firewall configuration, VPN setup, and introductory penetration testing in sandboxed environments. Each lab submission must include a written report documenting methodology, observations, and conclusions.

**Vulnerability Assessment Project (20%)**
A major group project (teams of 3–4 students) in which students conduct a full vulnerability assessment of a simulated enterprise network environment provided by the department. Teams will submit a formal written security report and deliver a 20-minute oral presentation of their findings, including prioritized remediation recommendations. Peer evaluation will account for 10% of the project grade.
