## Course Description

### Course Description

Principles of Digital Communications II is an advanced continuation of the foundational digital communications series, examining the design, analysis, and optimization of modern digital communication systems. Students will explore advanced modulation schemes, error control coding, channel capacity, and the theoretical underpinnings of wireless and broadband transmission. Emphasis is placed on applying mathematical models and simulation tools to evaluate system performance in real-world communication environments.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and compare advanced digital modulation techniques, including QAM, OFDM, and spread spectrum methods, and evaluate their performance under varying channel conditions
- Apply principles of error detection and correction coding, including convolutional and turbo codes, to design robust communication systems
- Demonstrate a working understanding of Shannon's channel capacity theorem and its implications for practical system design
- Utilize signal processing and simulation tools to model digital communication systems and interpret system performance metrics such as bit error rate (BER) and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
- Evaluate the trade-offs inherent in digital communication system design, including bandwidth efficiency, power consumption, and transmission reliability

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-10200: Principles of Digital Communications I** – foundational knowledge of digital signals, basic modulation, and transmission media is assumed
- **COMP-11150: Signals and Systems** – proficiency in Fourier analysis, linear time-invariant systems, and frequency-domain representation is required
- **MATH-20310: Probability and Stochastic Processes** – a working knowledge of probability theory, random variables, and statistical analysis is necessary
- Students are additionally expected to have completed **MATH-18200: Linear Algebra and Differential Equations** or an equivalent course

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Assignments (×4) | 25% |
| Design Project | 20% |
| Problem Sets / Homework | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess conceptual understanding of modulation theory, coding schemes, and channel analysis through a combination of analytical problems and short-answer questions.

**Laboratory Assignments** require students to implement and simulate communication system components using industry-standard software, with written reports submitted for each lab session.

**Design Project** is a semester-long individual or paired project in which students design, simulate, and critically evaluate a complete digital communications link. A formal written report and oral presentation are required at semester's end.

**Problem Sets** are assigned regularly throughout the term and serve as formative assessments aligned with weekly lecture content.
