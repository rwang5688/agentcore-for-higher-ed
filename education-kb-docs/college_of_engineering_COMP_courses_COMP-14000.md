## Course Description

### Course Description

Performance Engineering of Software Systems provides an in-depth examination of the principles, methodologies, and tools used to analyze, model, and optimize the performance of modern software systems. Students will explore the full performance engineering lifecycle, from requirements specification and system modeling through measurement, profiling, bottleneck identification, and iterative optimization. Emphasis is placed on quantitative approaches to system analysis, including workload characterization, capacity planning, and the application of performance engineering practices within real-world software development contexts.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply systematic measurement and profiling techniques to identify performance bottlenecks in software systems, including memory usage, CPU utilization, and I/O throughput constraints.
- Construct and interpret analytical and simulation-based performance models, including queuing theory models and workload characterization frameworks, to predict and evaluate system behavior under varying load conditions.
- Design and execute structured performance testing strategies, including load testing, stress testing, and scalability testing, using industry-standard tooling and instrumentation.
- Evaluate architectural and algorithmic trade-offs that impact system performance, and propose evidence-based optimizations grounded in empirical data collection and statistical analysis.
- Integrate performance engineering practices into the software development lifecycle, articulating the relationship between performance goals, service-level objectives, and broader system reliability and quality requirements.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-10200 – Data Structures and Algorithm Analysis** (minimum grade of C or better)
- **COMP-11400 – Systems Programming and Computer Organization**
- **COMP-12750 – Operating Systems Fundamentals**
- **MATH-10500 – Probability and Statistics for Engineers** or equivalent, as approved by the department

Students are expected to have proficiency in at least one systems-level programming language (e.g., C, C++, or Java) and familiarity with command-line environments and basic software development workflows. Students who have not completed the listed prerequisites may seek departmental consent for enrollment in exceptional circumstances.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Closed-book written exam covering analytical models, queuing theory, and performance measurement concepts covered in the first half of the course. |
| Final Examination | 25% | Comprehensive closed-book written examination assessing mastery of all course topics, with emphasis on integration of concepts across the performance engineering lifecycle. |
| Performance Profiling Lab Reports (×3) | 20% | Three structured lab assignments in which students profile, instrument, and analyze the performance of provided software systems, submitting formal written reports documenting methodology, findings, and recommended optimizations. |
| System Performance Optimization Project | 25% | A semester-long team project (groups of 2–3) in which students select or are assigned a non-trivial software system, establish performance baselines, apply engineering techniques to improve measurable performance metrics, and deliver a final written report and oral presentation to the class. |
| Weekly Problem Sets | 10% | Assigned problem sets reinforcing quantitative analytical methods, including workload modeling exercises and queuing network calculations. |

*All submitted work must adhere to the Academic Integrity Policy outlined in the Peculiar University Student Handbook. Collaboration policies specific to each assessment will be distributed at the start of the semester.*

### Required Materials and Textbooks

Students are required to obtain the following materials prior to the first week of class:
