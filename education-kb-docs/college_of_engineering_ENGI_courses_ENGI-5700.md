## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-5700 provides an advanced treatment of microelectronic devices and circuits, examining the physical principles, operational characteristics, and design applications of semiconductor devices including diodes, bipolar junction transistors, and metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistors. Students will develop rigorous analytical and simulation-based skills for the modeling, analysis, and design of integrated circuits operating at the microelectronic scale. Emphasis is placed on bridging foundational semiconductor physics with practical circuit design methodologies relevant to modern electronic systems engineering.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the physical mechanisms governing carrier transport, energy band structure, and junction behavior in semiconductor materials and devices
- Analyze and model the small-signal and large-signal behavior of BJT and MOSFET devices under various operating conditions and biasing configurations
- Design and evaluate analog and digital microelectronic circuits, including amplifiers, logic gates, and current mirrors, using both hand analysis and simulation tools
- Apply frequency-domain analysis techniques to characterize the bandwidth, gain, and stability of multi-stage microelectronic amplifier circuits
- Interpret and produce professional-level circuit schematics and simulation reports that communicate design intent, performance trade-offs, and validation results

---

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **ENGI-3210: Semiconductor Fundamentals** – foundational treatment of solid-state physics, energy bands, and carrier dynamics
- **ENGI-3450: Electronic Circuits I** – introductory analysis of passive and active circuits including basic transistor biasing
- **MATH-3310: Differential Equations and Linear Algebra** – mathematical methods required for circuit modeling and frequency-domain analysis
- Students are strongly encouraged to have working familiarity with a circuit simulation environment (e.g., SPICE-based platforms) prior to the first laboratory session

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Problem Sets (6–8 assignments) | 20% |
| Laboratory Reports (4 sessions) | 20% |
| Circuit Design Project | 15% |

**Examinations** are closed-book with an approved formula reference sheet and assess theoretical understanding, device modeling, and analytical circuit problem solving at a graduate-preparatory level.

**Problem Sets** are assigned bi-weekly and involve quantitative analysis, small-signal modeling exercises, and design calculations intended to reinforce lecture material.

**Laboratory Reports** document hands-on experimentation with discrete and integrated microelectronic devices, requiring students to compare measured results against theoretical predictions and simulation outputs.

**Circuit Design Project** is a semester-long individual or paired effort in which students design, simulate, and present a microelectronic subsystem of their choosing (e.g., a low-noise amplifier, a bandgap reference circuit, or a CMOS logic block), culminating in a written technical report and an oral presentation delivered during the final two weeks of term.
