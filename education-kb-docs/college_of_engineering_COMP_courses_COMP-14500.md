## Course Description

**COMP-14500 | Probabilistic Systems Analysis and Applied Probability**
*3 Credit Points | Department of Computer Science | College of Engineering*

---

This course provides a rigorous introduction to probability theory and stochastic modeling as applied to the analysis and design of computational and engineered systems. Students explore foundational probabilistic concepts including random variables, expectation, distributions, and limit theorems, with an emphasis on modeling uncertainty in real-world computing environments. The course bridges theoretical probability with applied methodologies, preparing students to reason about and quantify uncertainty in algorithm performance, network behavior, system reliability, and data-driven applications.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Construct and analyze probabilistic models for discrete and continuous systems, selecting appropriate distributions and parameters to represent real-world computational scenarios
- Apply core theorems of probability—including the Law of Large Numbers, the Central Limit Theorem, and Bayes' Theorem—to evaluate system behavior under uncertainty
- Analyze Markov chains, queuing models, and stochastic processes in the context of network design, operating systems, and resource allocation problems
- Design and interpret Monte Carlo simulations and other computational probabilistic methods to estimate system performance and quantify risk
- Critically evaluate the assumptions and limitations of probabilistic models, and communicate findings through formal written and verbal technical presentation

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-14500 must have successfully completed the following, or received explicit departmental approval:

- **COMP-2210: Discrete Structures for Computer Science** or equivalent — a minimum grade of C required
- **MATH-2310: Calculus II** or equivalent — a minimum grade of C required
- **MATH-3100: Linear Algebra** — strongly recommended
- Junior or Senior standing, or enrollment in a graduate program within the College of Engineering

Students without formal background in introductory statistics are strongly encouraged to complete **MATH-2050: Introduction to Statistical Reasoning** prior to enrollment, though this is not a strict requirement.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance is evaluated through a combination of examinations, applied problem sets, simulation projects, and written analysis. The following breakdown reflects the standard grading structure for this course:

- **Midterm Examination (20%)** — A closed-book in-class examination covering foundational probability theory, distributions, and introductory stochastic processes. Students may bring one hand-written reference sheet.
- **Final Examination (25%)** — A comprehensive closed-book examination administered during the official final examination period, covering all course material with emphasis on integration of concepts across units.
- **Problem Sets (25%)** — Approximately eight to ten problem sets assigned throughout the semester, requiring analytical solutions and, where specified, computational verification. Collaboration is permitted in accordance with the Academic Integrity Policy; submitted work must be individually written.
- **Simulation and Modeling Projects (20%)** — Two substantial applied projects in which students implement probabilistic models and stochastic simulations using approved programming environments. Projects include a written technical report and, for the second project, a brief in-class presentation.
- **Participation and In-Class Exercises (10%)** — Regular in-class activities, pop quizzes, and discussion contributions that assess ongoing engagement with course material.

*Late work policy, grading rubrics, and academic integrity expectations are detailed in the course syllabus distributed at the start of the semester.*
