## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced course provides a rigorous treatment of electromagnetic field theory, examining the fundamental principles governing electric and magnetic fields, wave propagation, and their engineering applications. Students will develop a deep mathematical understanding of Maxwell's equations and their implications for modern electrical and electronic systems. The course bridges theoretical electromagnetic principles with practical engineering problems encountered in communications, power systems, and device design.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Derive and apply Maxwell's equations in both differential and integral forms to analyse static and time-varying electromagnetic fields in complex media
- Model and solve boundary value problems involving electric and magnetic fields using advanced analytical and numerical techniques
- Evaluate the behaviour of electromagnetic waves in guided structures, free space, and at material interfaces, including reflection, transmission, and polarisation phenomena
- Apply electromagnetic field theory to engineering design challenges in areas such as antenna systems, transmission lines, and electromagnetic compatibility
- Critically assess and interpret experimental and computational results within the context of established electromagnetic theory and current research literature

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-1900 must have successfully completed the following:

- ENGI-1720: Advanced Circuit Analysis and Linear Systems (minimum grade: B)
- MATH-1680: Vector Calculus and Differential Equations (minimum grade: B)
- PHYS-1540: Classical Electrodynamics and Wave Mechanics
- Graduate standing in Engineering or a closely related discipline, or written permission from the supervising professor

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 30% |
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 20% |
| Computational Modelling Project | 20% |
| Research Paper and Oral Presentation | 10% |

**Problem Sets:** Six written assignments distributed throughout the semester, requiring analytical derivations, numerical calculations, and applied problem solving aligned with lecture content.

**Computational Modelling Project:** Students will employ finite element or finite difference time-domain (FDTD) software to model an electromagnetic system of engineering significance, submitting a formal technical report detailing methodology, results, and critical analysis.

**Research Paper and Oral Presentation:** A 4,000–5,000 word scholarly paper investigating a current research topic in electromagnetic engineering, presented to the class in a 20-minute seminar format. Topics must be approved by the supervising instructor by the end of Week 3.

**Examinations:** Both the midterm and final examinations are closed-book assessments requiring analytical solutions to multi-part problems. A single handwritten formula sheet (A4, double-sided) is permitted.
