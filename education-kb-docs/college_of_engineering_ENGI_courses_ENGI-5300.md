## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the fundamental principles governing microelectronic devices and their application in analog and digital circuit design. Students will examine the physical behavior of semiconductor materials, diodes, bipolar junction transistors (BJTs), and field-effect transistors (FETs), developing both theoretical understanding and practical analytical skills. Emphasis is placed on the design, analysis, and simulation of basic electronic circuits encountered in modern microelectronic systems.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of ENGI-5300, students will be able to:

- Explain the physical operating principles of key semiconductor devices, including p-n junction diodes, BJTs, and MOSFETs, and relate device behavior to underlying material properties
- Apply small-signal and large-signal models to analyze and design amplifier circuits operating under specified performance constraints
- Evaluate the DC and AC characteristics of common circuit configurations, including common-emitter, common-source, and differential amplifier topologies
- Utilize industry-standard circuit simulation tools to model, verify, and troubleshoot microelectronic circuit designs
- Interpret and apply device datasheet specifications to inform practical design decisions within real-world engineering contexts

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-5300 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 25% |
| Final Examination | 30% |
| Design Project | 20% |
| Problem Sets (8 assignments) | 15% |
| Circuit Simulation Labs (4 submissions) | 10% |

**Examinations:** Two formal written examinations will assess students' command of theoretical concepts, device modeling, and quantitative circuit analysis. Both exams are closed-book; one double-sided reference sheet prepared by the student is permitted.

**Design Project:** Completed in teams of two to three students, the design project requires the analysis, simulation, and documented design of a functional amplifier or signal-conditioning circuit meeting a specified set of performance criteria. A written technical report and a brief oral presentation to the instructional team are required components of the project submission.

**Problem Sets:** Weekly assignments reinforce lecture content through quantitative analysis problems, device modeling exercises, and short conceptual questions. Late submissions are subject to a 15% per-day penalty unless prior arrangements have been made with the course instructor.

**Circuit Simulation Labs:** Four structured simulation exercises using industry-standard software require students to model assigned circuits, interpret simulation outputs, and submit written analysis reports.
