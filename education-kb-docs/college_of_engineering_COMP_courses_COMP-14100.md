## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-14100: Principles of Computing introduces students to the foundational concepts underlying modern computational systems, including algorithmic thinking, data representation, and the logical structure of hardware and software. Students will develop an understanding of how computers process, store, and transmit information, as well as the theoretical underpinnings that govern computational problem-solving. This course serves as a cornerstone of the Computer Science curriculum, preparing students for advanced study in programming, systems design, and computational theory.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a working understanding of core computing concepts, including binary representation, Boolean logic, and basic data structures
- Apply algorithmic thinking to decompose real-world problems into logical, computable steps
- Identify and describe the functional components of a computing system, including CPU architecture, memory hierarchy, and input/output mechanisms
- Evaluate the efficiency of basic algorithms using introductory complexity analysis
- Communicate computational ideas clearly in both written and oral forms appropriate to a professional and academic audience

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Weekly Problem Sets (10 total) | 20% |
| Laboratory Assignments (5 total) | 20% |
| Computational Design Project | 15% |

**Examinations:** Both the midterm and final examinations are closed-book and assess students' understanding of theoretical and applied computing concepts covered in lecture. The final examination is cumulative.

**Problem Sets:** Weekly problem sets reinforce lecture content and require students to apply algorithmic and logical reasoning to structured computing problems.

**Laboratory Assignments:** Five hands-on lab sessions complement lecture material, providing practical experience with basic programming environments, binary arithmetic, and logic circuit simulation.

**Computational Design Project:** Students will complete a semester-long project in which they design, document, and present a solution to a defined computational problem. Projects are completed individually and culminate in a written technical report and a brief oral presentation during the final two weeks of the semester.
