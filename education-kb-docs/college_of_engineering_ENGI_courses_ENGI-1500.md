## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-1500: Dynamics of Machines provides a rigorous examination of the kinematic and kinetic principles governing mechanical systems, with emphasis on the analysis of motion, forces, and energy transfer in real-world machine components. Students will explore the behaviour of linkages, gears, cams, and rotating assemblies through both analytical and computational methods. This course bridges foundational engineering mechanics with applied machine design, preparing students for advanced study and professional practice in mechanical and systems engineering.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the kinematics of planar and spatial mechanisms, including velocity and acceleration of rigid bodies in constrained motion
- Apply Newton-Euler equations and Lagrangian mechanics to model the dynamic behaviour of multi-body machine systems
- Evaluate force transmission, mechanical advantage, and efficiency in gear trains, cam-follower systems, and linkage assemblies
- Perform vibration analysis of machine components, including natural frequency determination, resonance identification, and damping assessment
- Utilize computational tools and simulation software to model, validate, and optimize dynamic machine systems under realistic operating conditions

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 30% |
| Laboratory Reports (4 submissions) | 20% |
| Design & Analysis Project | 20% |
| Problem Sets / Assignments (6 sets) | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** are closed-book with an approved formula sheet permitted. Both examinations assess analytical problem-solving across all major course topics.

**Laboratory Reports** require students to document experimental procedures, record measured data from the machine dynamics laboratory, and compare empirical results against theoretical predictions. Reports must conform to departmental technical writing standards.

**Design and Analysis Project** is completed in teams of three to four students. Teams will select or be assigned a mechanical system, perform full kinematic and dynamic analysis, conduct computational simulation, and present findings in both a written technical report and an oral presentation during the final two weeks of the semester.

**Problem Sets** are submitted weekly and cover analytical techniques introduced in lectures. Collaboration is permitted; however, all submitted work must reflect individual understanding and be written independently.
