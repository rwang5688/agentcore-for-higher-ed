## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-1600: Free Course in Multiple Formats provides advanced computer science students with a comprehensive exploration of adaptive instructional methodologies, cross-platform content delivery systems, and the architectural principles underlying modern open-access educational technologies. Students will examine how freely distributed computational coursework is designed, structured, and disseminated across heterogeneous digital environments, with particular emphasis on accessibility standards, format interoperability, and scalable deployment. This course bridges theoretical frameworks in educational technology with applied software engineering practice, preparing students to design and critically evaluate open-format learning systems in professional and academic contexts.

### Learning Objectives

- Analyze and compare multiple digital content delivery formats, including web-based, offline, and hybrid platforms, evaluating their suitability for diverse computational learning environments
- Design and implement open-access course materials that conform to established accessibility and interoperability standards such as WCAG and IMS Global specifications
- Apply software engineering principles to develop scalable, format-agnostic content management pipelines capable of supporting varied instructional media
- Critically evaluate the pedagogical and technical trade-offs inherent in freely distributed educational resources, including licensing, versioning, and long-term maintainability
- Collaborate in multidisciplinary teams to produce a complete, deployable open courseware module demonstrating proficiency in content structuring, metadata tagging, and cross-format rendering

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 15% |
| Final Examination | 20% |
| Format Analysis Paper | 15% |
| Open Courseware Design Project (staged submissions) | 35% |
| Weekly Lab Exercises & Participation | 10% |
| Peer Review Portfolio | 5% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations assess theoretical knowledge of content delivery systems, accessibility standards, and software engineering principles covered in lectures. The final examination is cumulative with emphasis on the latter half of the course.

**Format Analysis Paper:** A 3,000–4,500 word scholarly paper in which students critically examine two or more content delivery formats, evaluating their technical and pedagogical merits within a defined educational context. Papers must adhere to IEEE citation standards.

**Open Courseware Design Project:** The course's primary deliverable, completed in teams of three to four students. The project is submitted in three staged milestones — a design specification document, a functional prototype, and a final deployable module with accompanying technical documentation. Each milestone is reviewed by both faculty and peer evaluators.

**Weekly Lab Exercises & Participation:** Structured laboratory sessions accompanying lectures require students to complete applied tasks in format conversion, metadata implementation, and accessibility auditing. Active, prepared participation is expected in all seminar discussions.

**Peer Review Portfolio:** Students participate in structured peer review of at least two other teams' project milestones, submitting written evaluations that demonstrate critical engagement with technical and pedagogical criteria.
