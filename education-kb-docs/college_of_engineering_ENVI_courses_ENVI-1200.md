### Course Description

This advanced course examines the intersection of political systems, governance frameworks, and legal structures as they apply to environmental management, resource protection, and ecological policy-making at local, national, and international scales. Students will critically analyse the role of regulatory bodies, legislative processes, and international treaties in shaping environmental outcomes, with particular attention to the tensions between economic development and ecological sustainability. Drawing on interdisciplinary methodologies, the course equips students to evaluate and engage with the complex institutional landscapes that govern environmental decision-making in the contemporary world.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically evaluate the development and application of major domestic and international environmental laws and treaties, identifying their strengths, limitations, and enforcement mechanisms
- Analyse the political forces, interest groups, and institutional actors that influence the formation and implementation of environmental policy across multiple levels of governance
- Apply legal reasoning and policy analysis frameworks to assess real-world environmental disputes, regulatory conflicts, and compliance challenges
- Synthesise knowledge from law, political science, and environmental science to construct evidence-based arguments regarding contested environmental governance issues
- Assess the implications of emerging challenges — including climate change, transboundary pollution, and biodiversity loss — for existing legal and political frameworks, and propose informed reform strategies

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Policy Analysis Paper | 30% | A substantive written analysis (3,500–4,500 words) examining a current or historical environmental law or policy dispute. Students are expected to engage with primary legal sources, legislative records, and peer-reviewed scholarship. Due at the end of Week 8. |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book examination comprising short-answer and essay questions assessing comprehension of core legal frameworks, political theory, and regulatory mechanisms covered in the first half of the course. |
| Moot Court / Policy Simulation | 25% | Students will participate in a structured group simulation — either a moot environmental tribunal or a legislative committee exercise — designed to develop oral advocacy, negotiation, and stakeholder analysis skills. Assessment includes both individual performance and group preparation materials. |
| Final Research Project | 20% | A collaborative research project (groups of 2–3) producing a policy brief of approximately 2,000 words, directed at a specified real or hypothetical government body, addressing a pressing environmental governance problem and recommending actionable legal or regulatory reforms. |
| Participation and Seminar Engagement | 5% | Regular, substantive contribution to seminar discussions, case study workshops, and peer critique sessions throughout the semester. |
