## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-4800: Cybersecurity Fundamentals provides an advanced exploration of the principles, tools, and methodologies used to protect digital systems, networks, and data from malicious threats and unauthorized access. Students will engage with both theoretical frameworks and applied techniques spanning cryptography, network security, vulnerability analysis, and incident response. This course prepares students for professional practice in cybersecurity roles and lays the groundwork for advanced specialization within the field.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and evaluate common cybersecurity threats, attack vectors, and vulnerabilities affecting modern networked systems and applications
- Apply cryptographic principles and protocols to design and assess secure communication systems
- Implement and assess network defense strategies, including firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and access control mechanisms
- Conduct structured vulnerability assessments and develop coherent incident response plans in accordance with industry and regulatory standards
- Demonstrate ethical reasoning and professional responsibility in cybersecurity practice, including compliance with relevant legal frameworks and institutional policy

---

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-3200: Computer Networks and Protocols** — foundational understanding of network architecture, TCP/IP, and data transmission
- **COMP-3150: Operating Systems** — proficiency in OS-level concepts including process management, file systems, and system permissions
- **COMP-2400: Data Structures and Algorithms** — required programming background at the intermediate level
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.5 is recommended; students who have not completed the above prerequisites must obtain written instructor approval prior to registration

---

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Midterm Examination (20%)** — A written exam covering cryptographic principles, threat modeling, and network security concepts addressed in the first half of the course
- **Final Examination (25%)** — A comprehensive written exam assessing cumulative understanding of all course topics, including scenario-based problem solving
- **Vulnerability Assessment Project (25%)** — A team-based assignment in which student groups perform a structured vulnerability assessment on a simulated or sandboxed environment, producing a formal written report with findings and remediation recommendations
- **Incident Response Case Study (15%)** — An individual written analysis of a real-world (or simulated) cybersecurity incident, evaluating the response strategy and proposing improvements grounded in course frameworks
- **Lab Assignments and Participation (15%)** — A series of practical, hands-on laboratory exercises conducted using the Peculiar U Cybersecurity Lab environment, assessing applied technical skill and engagement with course material

### Required Materials and Textbooks

Students are expected to obtain and engage with all required materials prior to the first week of class:

- **Hargreave, D. & Tolin, S. (2021).** *Foundations of Information Security: Threats, Defenses, and the Modern Attack Surface* (3rd ed.). Westbrook Academic Press. — Primary course text covering cryptography, network defense, and threat analysis
- **Castellan, M. & Orvino, P. (2020).** *Applied Network Security: Protocols, Practice, and Policy* (2nd ed.). Northgate University Publishing. — Supplementary text used heavily in the network security and firewall modules
- **Drevlow, I. (2022).** *Ethical Hacking and Vulnerability Assessment: A Practitioner's Guide*. Ridgeline Technical Press. — Required for the Vulnerability Assessment Project; students may use digital or print editions
- **Peculiar U Department of Computer Science (2024).** *COMP-4800 Laboratory Manual and Sandboxed Environment Guide* — Distributed electronically through the course management portal at no additional cost; required for all lab sessions

Students are also expected to have reliable access to a computer capable of running virtualization software. All required virtual environments and lab tools will be provided through the Peculiar U Cybersecurity Lab portal.
