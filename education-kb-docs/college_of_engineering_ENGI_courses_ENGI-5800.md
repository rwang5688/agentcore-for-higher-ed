## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced course provides an in-depth examination of microcontroller architecture, programming, and system integration, equipping students with the skills necessary to design, implement, and optimize embedded systems for real-world engineering applications. Topics include processor selection, peripheral interfacing, interrupt-driven programming, power management, and hardware-software co-design principles. Students will engage with both theoretical frameworks and hands-on laboratory experiences to develop complete microcontroller-based solutions from concept through deployment.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Design and implement embedded systems using industry-standard microcontroller platforms, applying knowledge of memory architecture, instruction sets, and peripheral integration
- Develop and debug firmware in C and assembly language, demonstrating proficiency in interrupt handling, timer configuration, and communication protocol implementation (UART, SPI, I²C)
- Analyze power consumption characteristics and apply low-power design strategies appropriate to resource-constrained embedded environments
- Evaluate microcontroller selection criteria for specific application requirements, considering processing speed, memory capacity, I/O availability, and cost constraints
- Apply hardware-software co-design methodologies to produce complete, documented embedded system solutions that meet defined functional and performance specifications

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **ENGI-3210 – Digital Systems Design** (minimum grade of C)
- **ENGI-3450 – Embedded Programming Fundamentals** (minimum grade of C)
- **ENGI-3310 – Circuit Analysis and Electronics II** (minimum grade of C)
- Proficiency in C programming is strongly recommended; students without prior embedded programming exposure are advised to consult with the course instructor before registering

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Assignments (6 sessions) | 25% |
| Capstone Design Project | 20% |
| Weekly Problem Sets | 10% |

**Examinations:** The midterm and final examinations assess students' understanding of microcontroller architecture, programming concepts, and system design principles through a combination of multiple-choice, short-answer, and circuit/code analysis questions. The final examination is comprehensive.

**Laboratory Assignments:** Six structured lab sessions provide hands-on experience with microcontroller development boards, requiring students to submit written lab reports documenting design decisions, test results, and analysis for each assignment.

**Capstone Design Project:** Completed in teams of two to three students, the capstone project requires the full design, implementation, testing, and documentation of an original microcontroller-based system. A formal written report and an oral presentation to faculty are required. Project proposals are due at Week 5, with final deliverables submitted during the last week of term.

**Problem Sets:** Weekly problem sets reinforce lecture material and develop quantitative problem-solving skills in areas such as timing calculations, protocol analysis, and power budgeting.
