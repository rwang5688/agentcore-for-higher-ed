## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6400 provides an advanced examination of the principles and applications of nuclear engineering, encompassing reactor physics, radiation transport, and nuclear materials science. Students will develop a rigorous understanding of neutron interactions, thermal-hydraulic systems, and the design and safety analysis of nuclear reactor systems. Emphasis is placed on integrating theoretical foundations with contemporary engineering challenges, including waste management, nonproliferation, and the evolving role of nuclear energy in global power generation.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyse neutron flux distributions and apply transport equations to model reactor core behaviour under steady-state and transient conditions
- Evaluate the thermal-hydraulic performance of reactor systems and apply heat transfer principles to coolant design and safety analysis
- Apply radiation shielding principles and dose assessment methodologies in compliance with current regulatory and safety standards
- Critically assess the structural and mechanical behaviour of nuclear materials under irradiation, including fuel cycle considerations and waste characterisation
- Design and evaluate reactor control systems, incorporating feedback mechanisms and demonstrating understanding of reactor kinetics and stability analysis

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed all of the following prior to enrolment:

- ENGI-4110 Thermodynamics and Fluid Mechanics II
- ENGI-4320 Introduction to Nuclear Physics and Radiation Science
- ENGI-4450 Heat Transfer and Thermal Systems
- MATH-4200 Partial Differential Equations and Applied Mathematics
- PHYS-4150 Quantum Mechanics for Engineers

Students are also expected to have a working proficiency in at least one computational modelling environment (e.g., MATLAB, Python, or equivalent) and a foundational understanding of materials science at the undergraduate level.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Closed-book written examination covering reactor physics, neutron transport, and radiation fundamentals |
| Final Examination | 30% | Comprehensive closed-book examination spanning all course topics, including thermal-hydraulics and reactor kinetics |
| Design Project | 25% | Team-based reactor systems design project culminating in a formal written report and oral presentation to a simulated regulatory review panel |
| Problem Sets (×6) | 15% | Six graded problem sets distributed throughout the semester, emphasising computational and analytical methods |
| Research Paper | 10% | Individual written paper (3,000–4,000 words) critically evaluating a contemporary issue in nuclear engineering such as advanced reactor concepts, waste disposal policy, or small modular reactor development |

Active participation in laboratory sessions and simulation workshops is expected, though not separately graded. Students must achieve a passing grade in both the examination component and the project component independently to pass the course.
