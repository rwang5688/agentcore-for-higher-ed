### Course Description

Applied Data Science provides students with a practical, hands-on introduction to the core methodologies and tools used in modern data analysis, equipping them to collect, process, visualize, and interpret real-world datasets. Drawing on foundational principles of statistics, programming, and domain reasoning, the course emphasizes the application of data science workflows to solve meaningful problems across diverse fields. Students will develop technical fluency alongside critical thinking skills necessary for responsible and effective use of data-driven methods.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply standard data science workflows—including data acquisition, cleaning, exploration, modeling, and communication—to structured and semi-structured datasets.
- Use programming tools and libraries to perform quantitative analysis, generate visualizations, and summarize findings for technical and non-technical audiences.
- Evaluate the appropriateness of common statistical and computational methods for a given dataset and research question.
- Identify and address issues of data quality, bias, and ethical responsibility in the collection, analysis, and presentation of data.
- Collaborate effectively on team-based data projects, demonstrating the ability to document processes, reproduce results, and communicate conclusions clearly.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in DATA-0200 must have successfully completed the following, or obtained instructor consent:

- **DATA-0101: Introduction to Data and Computation** or **CS-0101: Foundations of Programming** — ensuring basic familiarity with a high-level programming language (Python or R preferred).
- **MATH-0120: Introduction to Statistics** or **MATH-0115: Probability and Statistical Reasoning** — providing grounding in descriptive and inferential statistics.

Students without formal prerequisites who possess demonstrable equivalent experience should consult with the course coordinator before registration.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Lab Assignments (6 throughout semester) | 30% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Group Data Project (proposal, analysis, final report) | 30% |
| Individual Reflection Portfolio | 10% |
| Participation and In-Class Activities | 10% |

**Lab Assignments** are completed in scheduled lab sessions and assess students' practical ability to apply course tools and techniques to provided datasets. **The Midterm Examination** tests conceptual understanding of data science methods, workflow reasoning, and interpretation of analytical outputs. **The Group Data Project** is a semester-long collaborative investigation in which student teams identify a dataset of interest, formulate a research question, conduct a full analysis, and present findings in both written and oral form. **The Individual Reflection Portfolio** consists of short written reflections submitted at key course milestones, documenting each student's developing understanding and self-assessed growth. Active engagement in lectures and structured in-class exercises contributes to the **Participation** component.
