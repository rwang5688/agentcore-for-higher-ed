## Course Description

### Course Description

DATA-1300 provides an advanced examination of machine learning methodologies and their application within large-scale data environments, equipping students with both the theoretical foundations and practical competencies required to design, implement, and evaluate predictive models. Topics include supervised and unsupervised learning paradigms, neural network architectures, model optimization, and ethical considerations in algorithmic decision-making. Students will engage with real-world datasets throughout the course, applying cutting-edge computational tools to solve complex, domain-spanning problems.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically evaluate and select appropriate machine learning algorithms for a given analytical problem, justifying methodological choices with reference to theoretical principles and empirical evidence
- Design, train, and validate predictive models using industry-standard frameworks, demonstrating proficiency in managing model performance metrics such as precision, recall, and F1-score
- Apply advanced techniques including ensemble methods, regularization strategies, and hyperparameter tuning to optimize model performance on complex datasets
- Interpret and communicate the outputs of machine learning systems to both technical and non-technical audiences, incorporating considerations of fairness, bias, and model transparency
- Develop end-to-end machine learning pipelines that integrate data preprocessing, feature engineering, model training, and deployment-ready evaluation within a reproducible workflow

### Prerequisites

Given the 850-level designation of this course, students are expected to have substantial prior preparation. The following prerequisites are required:

- Completion of DATA-0600: Foundations of Statistical Learning or equivalent with a grade of B or higher
- Completion of DATA-0750: Applied Data Analysis and Predictive Modeling or equivalent
- Demonstrated proficiency in Python or R programming, as evidenced by completion of CS-0400: Programming for Data Scientists or departmental placement assessment
- Graduate standing or senior undergraduate standing with departmental approval
- Working knowledge of linear algebra, multivariate calculus, and probability theory (MATH-0500 or equivalent is strongly recommended)

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Machine Learning Implementation Project | 30% |
| Research Paper and Presentation | 15% |
| Weekly Problem Sets and Lab Submissions | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations assess theoretical understanding of machine learning concepts, algorithm derivations, and the ability to critically analyze model outputs. The final examination is cumulative and includes case-based analytical questions.

**Machine Learning Implementation Project:** Conducted in small teams, this capstone-style project requires students to identify a substantive real-world problem, source and preprocess appropriate data, develop and compare multiple machine learning models, and present findings in both a written technical report and an oral defense before a faculty panel. Projects are evaluated on methodological rigor, interpretability of results, and quality of communication.

**Research Paper:** Students will author an individual scholarly paper (3,500–5,000 words) critically reviewing a contemporary development in machine learning — such as transformer architectures, federated learning, or adversarial robustness — situating it within the broader literature and assessing its practical and ethical implications. A formal in-class presentation of 15–20 minutes accompanies the written submission.

**Problem Sets and Lab Submissions:** Weekly computational labs are assigned to reinforce lecture content and build hands-on proficiency. Submissions are assessed for both technical correctness and clarity of annotation and documentation.
