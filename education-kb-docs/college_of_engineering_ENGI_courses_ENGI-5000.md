This course provides a rigorous introduction to the fundamental principles governing the behavior of fluids at rest and in motion, with applications drawn from civil, mechanical, and chemical engineering contexts. Students will explore core concepts including fluid statics, continuity, momentum, and energy equations, developing both theoretical understanding and practical problem-solving competencies. Emphasis is placed on the application of these principles to real-world engineering systems, preparing students for advanced study in areas such as thermodynamics, hydraulics, and aerodynamics.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Define and apply fundamental fluid properties, including viscosity, density, pressure, and surface tension, to the analysis of static and dynamic fluid systems
- Derive and apply the continuity, Navier-Stokes, and Bernoulli equations to model fluid flow in a variety of engineering scenarios
- Analyze internal and external flow problems, including pipe flow, boundary layer behavior, and flow over submerged bodies, using appropriate analytical and computational methods
- Apply dimensional analysis and similarity principles, including the use of the Buckingham Pi theorem, to scale physical fluid systems and interpret experimental data
- Demonstrate proficiency in interpreting and constructing flow diagrams, velocity profiles, and pressure distributions relevant to engineering design problems

### Prerequisites

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Reports (×5) | 25% |
| Problem Sets (×8) | 15% |
| Design Project & Presentation | 15% |

**Examinations:** Two written examinations will assess students' theoretical understanding and quantitative problem-solving ability. Both examinations are closed-book; a formula sheet provided by the instructor will be permitted.

**Laboratory Reports:** Students will complete five structured laboratory sessions covering experimental measurement of viscosity, hydrostatic forces, pipe flow characteristics, drag on submerged bodies, and open-channel flow. Each session requires a formal written report submitted within one week of the laboratory date.

**Problem Sets:** Eight problem sets distributed throughout the semester will reinforce lecture content and prepare students for examination assessments. Late submissions will be penalized at a rate of 10% per calendar day.

**Design Project:** Working in teams of three to four, students will undertake a semester-long engineering design project requiring the application of fluid mechanics principles to a practical system — such as a pipe network, HVAC distribution system, or small-scale hydraulic structure. Teams will submit a written technical report and deliver a formal oral presentation during the final two weeks of the semester.
