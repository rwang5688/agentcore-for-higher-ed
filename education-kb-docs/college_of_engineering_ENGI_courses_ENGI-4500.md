## Course Description

### Course Description

Integrated Circuit Devices provides a rigorous examination of the physical principles, fabrication techniques, and operational characteristics of modern semiconductor devices as applied in integrated circuit design. Students explore the behavior of diodes, bipolar junction transistors, metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistors (MOSFETs), and related components within the context of contemporary IC architectures. Emphasis is placed on bridging theoretical device physics with practical engineering analysis and design methodologies used in industry.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the fundamental physical mechanisms governing charge transport, carrier concentration, and energy band structures in semiconductor materials
- Model and characterize the electrical behavior of core IC devices including p-n junctions, BJTs, MOSFETs, and CMOS structures under various operating conditions
- Apply DC and small-signal analysis techniques to evaluate device performance parameters such as gain, transconductance, and output resistance
- Interpret and construct device characteristic curves and use them to inform circuit-level design decisions
- Evaluate the impact of fabrication processes and material properties on device performance, reliability, and scaling limitations in modern integrated circuits

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination I | 20% |
| Midterm Examination II | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Reports & Simulations | 20% |
| Design Project & Presentation | 10% |
| Problem Sets & Homework | 5% |

**Examinations:** Two midterm examinations and a comprehensive final examination assess students' understanding of device physics, analytical modeling, and problem-solving in semiconductor device contexts. Exams are closed-book unless otherwise noted, with an approved equation sheet provided.

**Laboratory Reports & Simulations:** Students complete a series of structured laboratory exercises using industry-standard SPICE-based simulation tools (e.g., LTspice, Cadence) to characterize device behavior and verify theoretical models. Formal written reports are required for each laboratory session.

**Design Project & Presentation:** Working in teams of two to three, students undertake a semester-long device analysis and design project culminating in a written technical report and a formal oral presentation delivered to faculty and peers. Projects require students to select a specific IC device application, analyze relevant device parameters, and propose design optimizations supported by simulation data.

**Problem Sets & Homework:** Weekly problem sets reinforce lecture material and develop quantitative problem-solving skills. Late submissions are subject to a penalty as outlined in the course syllabus.
