## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6600 provides an advanced treatment of optimization theory and its application to complex engineering systems, covering deterministic and stochastic modeling techniques including linear programming, integer programming, nonlinear optimization, and metaheuristic approaches. Students will develop rigorous mathematical foundations alongside practical computational skills to formulate, analyze, and solve large-scale optimization problems arising in real-world engineering contexts. Emphasis is placed on bridging theoretical frameworks with modern algorithmic tools and software environments used in professional engineering practice.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Formulate and classify engineering problems as appropriate optimization models, selecting suitable solution methodologies based on problem structure and constraints
- Apply classical and advanced optimization algorithms—including gradient-based methods, branch-and-bound, dynamic programming, and evolutionary algorithms—to solve deterministic and stochastic engineering problems
- Critically evaluate the computational complexity, convergence properties, and sensitivity of optimization solutions with respect to model parameters and assumptions
- Utilize industry-standard optimization software and programming environments to implement, test, and validate models for engineering applications such as structural design, resource allocation, and network flow
- Communicate optimization model formulations, solution strategies, and results clearly and rigorously through written reports and oral presentations appropriate for a professional engineering audience

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- ENGI-4210: Linear Systems and Mathematical Modeling, or equivalent
- MATH-4305: Advanced Linear Algebra and Matrix Analysis, or equivalent
- ENGI-4420: Probability and Stochastic Processes for Engineers, or equivalent
- Working proficiency in at least one technical computing environment (e.g., MATLAB, Python, or Julia) is strongly recommended
- Graduate standing in the College of Engineering or written instructor consent required for students outside the program

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 20% |
| Computational Project | 25% |
| Research Paper and Presentation | 10% |

**Problem Sets:** Six graded assignments distributed throughout the semester, requiring both analytical derivations and computational implementation of optimization methods covered in lectures.

**Midterm Examination:** A closed-book written examination covering theoretical foundations and algorithmic methods from the first half of the course. One formula sheet is permitted.

**Final Examination:** A comprehensive written examination encompassing all course material, with emphasis on integrating concepts across multiple optimization paradigms. One formula sheet is permitted.

**Computational Project:** Students will select an open-ended engineering optimization problem, develop and implement an appropriate model, and produce a written technical report detailing their methodology, results, sensitivity analyses, and conclusions. Projects may be completed individually or in pairs with instructor approval.

**Research Paper and Presentation:** A short critical review paper (minimum 2,500 words) examining a recent application of optimization in a chosen engineering domain, accompanied by a 15-minute in-class presentation delivered to peers and course instructors.
