## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-10500 provides graduate-level students with a rigorous introduction to the principles, tools, and methodologies underlying modern cybersecurity practice and cyber threat analysis. Students will examine the landscape of contemporary cyber attacks — including network intrusions, malware deployment, social engineering, and exploitation techniques — while developing hands-on proficiency with industry-standard security tools used in detection, prevention, and forensic response. The course balances theoretical foundations with practical laboratory exercises to prepare students for advanced study and professional work in cybersecurity and information assurance.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and categorize major classes of cyber attacks, including network-based, application-layer, and human-factor threats, and articulate the mechanisms by which they are executed
- Demonstrate competency in using foundational cybersecurity tools for tasks such as network scanning, vulnerability assessment, packet analysis, and intrusion detection
- Analyze system and network logs to identify indicators of compromise and reconstruct the timeline of a simulated or real-world security incident
- Evaluate the ethical, legal, and organizational dimensions of offensive and defensive security operations, including responsible disclosure and compliance frameworks
- Design and implement a basic security assessment plan for a given system environment, applying appropriate tools and methodologies to identify and mitigate vulnerabilities

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-10500 must have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent preparation:

- **COMP-4200: Computer Networks and Protocols** or equivalent undergraduate networking coursework
- **COMP-4450: Operating Systems Fundamentals** or equivalent undergraduate operating systems coursework
- Graduate standing in Computer Science, Information Technology, or a closely related discipline, or written permission of the instructor
- Working knowledge of at least one programming or scripting language (Python, Bash, or equivalent) is strongly recommended

Students without a formal computing background who hold professional experience in IT or systems administration may petition the department for a prerequisite waiver.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Laboratory Assignments (6 total) | 30% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Threat Analysis Case Studies (2 written reports) | 20% |
| Capstone Security Assessment Project | 20% |
| Participation & In-Class Exercises | 10% |

**Laboratory Assignments** are conducted in Peculiar U's isolated virtual security lab environment and cover practical application of tools introduced in lecture. Each lab includes a written reflection component.

**Threat Analysis Case Studies** require students to investigate a documented real-world or simulated cyber incident, produce a structured written analysis, and present findings to the class.

**Capstone Security Assessment Project** tasks student teams with conducting a full vulnerability assessment of an assigned simulated network environment, culminating in a professional-grade written report and oral defense.

**Midterm Examination** consists of a combination of multiple-choice, short-answer, and scenario-based questions covering foundational concepts and tool usage.
