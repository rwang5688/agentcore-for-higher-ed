## Course Description

**COMP-4400 | Cryptocurrency and Blockchain: An Introduction to Digital Currencies**
**Credit Points:** 3 | **Department:** Computer Science | **College of Engineering**

---

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the foundational principles of blockchain technology and decentralized digital currency systems, with particular emphasis on the cryptographic mechanisms and distributed computing architectures that underpin modern cryptocurrencies. Students will examine the design, operation, and real-world applications of blockchain networks, including consensus algorithms, smart contracts, and token economics, situating these technologies within broader contexts of financial systems and computational security. By the conclusion of the course, students will possess both the theoretical understanding and practical technical literacy necessary to evaluate, analyze, and engage with emerging blockchain-based platforms and protocols.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of COMP-4400, students will be able to:

- Explain the core cryptographic principles—including hash functions, public-key cryptography, and digital signatures—that enable secure, trustless transaction verification on blockchain networks.
- Describe and compare prominent consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work, Proof of Stake, and Delegated Byzantine Fault Tolerance, analyzing their respective trade-offs in security, scalability, and energy consumption.
- Analyze the structure and lifecycle of smart contracts, demonstrating the ability to read and interpret basic contract code within platforms such as Ethereum and assess their potential applications across diverse industries.
- Evaluate the regulatory, ethical, and socioeconomic implications of decentralized financial systems, including considerations of privacy, financial inclusion, environmental impact, and systemic risk.
- Design and present a technical analysis of an existing or proposed blockchain application, demonstrating integration of theoretical knowledge with applied problem-solving and critical evaluation skills.

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following courses or their approved equivalents prior to enrollment in COMP-4400:

- **COMP-2210: Data Structures and Algorithms** — Foundational understanding of algorithmic complexity and data organization is essential for engaging with distributed ledger architectures.
- **COMP-2340: Introduction to Computer Security** — Prior exposure to basic cryptographic concepts, network security principles, and threat modeling is required.
- **COMP-3150: Operating Systems and Distributed Computing** — Students must demonstrate familiarity with distributed system design, process management, and network communication protocols.
- **MATH-2120: Discrete Mathematics** — Proficiency in logic, set theory, and number theory provides necessary mathematical grounding for cryptographic study.

Students who have not completed the listed prerequisites but believe they possess equivalent preparation are encouraged to consult with the course coordinator or their academic advisor to request a prerequisite waiver.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-4400 will be evaluated through the following assessment components:

| Assessment | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book written examination covering cryptographic foundations, blockchain architecture, and consensus mechanisms addressed in the first half of the course. |
| Final Examination | 25% | A comprehensive closed-book examination spanning all course material, with emphasis on integrating concepts across modules and applying knowledge to novel scenarios. |
| Programming Assignments (×3) | 25% | Three structured lab-based assignments requiring students to implement and interact with cryptographic functions, simulate distributed ledger operations, and deploy a basic smart contract in a sandboxed test environment. |
| Research Paper | 15% | An individually authored paper of 2,500–3,500 words critically analyzing a specific blockchain application, protocol, or policy issue. Papers must adhere to IEEE citation formatting and demonstrate engagement with peer-reviewed literature. |
| Group Capstone Project | 15% | Working in teams of three to four, students will propose, design, and present a blockchain-based solution to a defined real-world problem.
