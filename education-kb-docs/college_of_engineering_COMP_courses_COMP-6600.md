## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-6600 provides a rigorous examination of discrete stochastic processes and their applications within computer science and engineering contexts. Students will explore the mathematical foundations of random processes, including Markov chains, queuing theory, and probabilistic modeling, with an emphasis on algorithm design and computational analysis. The course bridges theoretical probability with practical applications in areas such as network modeling, distributed systems, and machine learning infrastructure.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Construct and analyze discrete-time and continuous-time Markov chains, including steady-state analysis and convergence properties
- Apply stochastic modeling techniques to real-world computational problems such as network traffic analysis, load balancing, and randomized algorithms
- Evaluate the performance characteristics of queuing systems using probabilistic tools and simulation methods
- Develop and implement Monte Carlo methods and other stochastic simulation strategies to approximate solutions to complex computational problems
- Formulate and rigorously prove fundamental results in stochastic processes, including limit theorems, stopping times, and martingale theory

---

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-6600 must have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency:

- **COMP-3210: Probability and Statistics for Computing** or equivalent undergraduate-level probability course
- **COMP-4115: Algorithm Design and Analysis**
- **MATH-4320: Linear Algebra and Matrix Theory**
- Graduate standing in the College of Engineering or written permission from the supervising instructor

Students without a strong background in discrete mathematics and calculus-based probability are strongly encouraged to consult with their academic advisor prior to enrollment.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-6600 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 25% |
| Research Paper / Literature Review | 15% |
| Computational Project & Presentation | 15% |

**Problem Sets:** Six written problem sets will be distributed throughout the semester, requiring both analytical derivations and computational solutions. Collaboration is permitted in limited form per the department's academic integrity policy; all submitted work must be independently written.

**Research Paper:** Students will author a 3,000–4,500 word review paper examining a contemporary application of stochastic processes in computer science (e.g., probabilistic graphical models, stochastic gradient descent, or network reliability). Papers must cite a minimum of twelve peer-reviewed sources.

**Computational Project:** Working individually or in pairs, students will design and implement a stochastic simulation project of their choosing, subject to instructor approval. A formal 15-minute presentation will be delivered during the final two weeks of the semester. Project proposals are due by Week 4.

**Examinations:** Both the midterm and final examinations are closed-book with one permitted formula sheet. The final examination is cumulative.
