This course provides students with a comprehensive foundation in designing, implementing, and interpreting data visualizations using the Python programming language, with emphasis on industry-standard libraries and best practices for communicating complex data insights. Students will explore the full pipeline from raw data ingestion and transformation to the production of publication-quality static, interactive, and web-based visual outputs. Special attention is given to principles of visual design, accessibility, and the ethical responsibilities of those who communicate data to diverse audiences.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of COMP-15700, students will be able to:

- Construct a range of fundamental and advanced chart types — including histograms, scatter plots, heat maps, geographic plots, and time-series visualizations — using Python libraries such as Matplotlib, Seaborn, and Plotly.
- Apply established principles of visual design, including appropriate use of colour, scale, labelling, and layout, to create clear, accurate, and audience-appropriate data graphics.
- Manipulate, clean, and prepare structured and semi-structured datasets using Pandas and NumPy in preparation for visual analysis and presentation.
- Develop interactive and web-deployable dashboards using frameworks such as Dash or Streamlit, enabling end-user exploration of complex datasets.
- Critically evaluate existing data visualizations for accuracy, clarity, and potential bias, and articulate reasoned improvements in both written and verbal formats.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-15700 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Programming Assignments (5 × individual) | 30% |
| Midterm Examination | 15% |
| Visualization Critique Paper | 10% |
| Group Dashboard Project | 25% |
| Final Examination | 20% |

**Programming Assignments** are distributed throughout the semester and require students to complete visualization tasks of increasing complexity using provided or student-sourced datasets. Assignments are submitted via the course's version-controlled repository system and are assessed on both technical correctness and design quality.

**Midterm and Final Examinations** include a combination of short-answer conceptual questions, code interpretation exercises, and short programming tasks completed in a supervised lab environment.

**Visualization Critique Paper** (approximately 1,200–1,500 words) requires students to select a published data visualization from a scientific journal, news organization, or government report, and produce a written analysis evaluating its design choices, effectiveness, and potential ethical concerns, proposing concrete improvements with justification.

**Group Dashboard Project** is a semester-long collaborative assignment in which teams of three to four students design and deploy a fully interactive data dashboard on a topic of their choosing, subject to instructor approval. Teams present their work to the class during the final two weeks of the semester; presentations are evaluated by both instructors and peers.
