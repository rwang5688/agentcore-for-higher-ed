## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6200: John Hart U Michigan is an advanced graduate-level engineering course examining the research methodologies, manufacturing innovations, and systems engineering principles pioneered within the John Hart Laboratory at the University of Michigan. Students will engage with cutting-edge topics in advanced manufacturing, digital fabrication, and micro/nanoscale engineering processes, exploring both theoretical foundations and applied industrial contexts. The course bridges academic research with engineering practice, preparing students to contribute meaningfully to emerging challenges in precision manufacturing and scalable fabrication technologies.

### Learning Objectives

- Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of advanced manufacturing processes, including additive manufacturing, digital fabrication, and micro/nanoscale production techniques
- Critically evaluate primary research literature and technical documentation related to systems-level manufacturing design and process optimization
- Apply quantitative and computational methods to model, simulate, and analyze complex fabrication systems and manufacturing workflows
- Design and propose original engineering solutions informed by current research trends in precision and scalable manufacturing
- Communicate technical findings effectively through both written reports and formal oral presentations appropriate to a professional engineering audience

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following or equivalent coursework prior to enrollment:

- ENGI-4100: Fundamentals of Manufacturing Systems or ENGI-4150: Materials Processing and Design
- ENGI-4300: Engineering Analysis and Numerical Methods
- Graduate standing in the College of Engineering or written permission from the course coordinator
- Demonstrated proficiency in at least one computational or simulation environment (e.g., MATLAB, Python, ANSYS, or equivalent)

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Written Examination | 20% |
| Final Written Examination | 20% |
| Research Literature Review Paper | 25% |
| Fabrication Systems Design Project | 25% |
| Participation & Weekly Discussion Responses | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess students' mastery of core theoretical and applied concepts covered across the course modules. Examinations will include a combination of short-answer technical questions and extended problem-solving tasks.

**Research Literature Review Paper** requires students to identify, critically evaluate, and synthesize a minimum of ten peer-reviewed sources on a topic within advanced manufacturing, culminating in a formal academic paper of 4,000–5,500 words.

**Fabrication Systems Design Project** is a team-based capstone project in which student groups design a proposed manufacturing system or process innovation, supported by technical analysis, simulation data, and a final presentation to a panel of faculty reviewers.

**Weekly Discussion Responses** are short written reflections submitted via the course management platform in response to assigned readings and case studies.
