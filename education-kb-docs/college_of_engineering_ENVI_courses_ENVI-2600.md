---

This course provides a comprehensive examination of the Earth's cryosphere — encompassing glaciers, ice sheets, sea ice, permafrost, and snow cover — with a sustained focus on how accelerating climate change is reshaping these critical systems. Students will investigate the dynamic feedbacks between cryospheric processes and global climate, exploring the cascading consequences for sea-level rise, freshwater availability, ecosystems, and human communities. Drawing on current scientific research, remote sensing data, and field-based case studies, the course equips students to critically assess both the physical drivers of cryospheric change and the policy responses emerging at regional and international scales.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Describe the principal components of the cryosphere, their geographic distribution, and their roles within the broader Earth system, including albedo feedback mechanisms and thermohaline circulation;
- Analyze observational and modeled datasets to evaluate rates and patterns of ice loss in key regions such as the Arctic, Antarctic, Greenland, and high-mountain environments;
- Critically assess the downstream consequences of cryospheric change, including contributions to global mean sea-level rise, permafrost thaw dynamics, and disruption of freshwater systems;
- Evaluate the social, economic, and cultural vulnerabilities of communities — particularly Indigenous and coastal populations — disproportionately affected by cryospheric decline;
- Synthesize scientific evidence and current international frameworks to appraise mitigation and adaptation strategies relevant to cryosphere-related climate risks.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-2600 must have successfully completed the following, or received written instructor approval:

- **ENVI-1100: Introduction to Environmental Systems** or **ENVI-1120: Earth and Climate Fundamentals**
- **MATH-1210: Introductory Statistics for the Sciences** or equivalent quantitative methods coursework
- Completion of a minimum of 30 credit points at the undergraduate level

Students with a strong background in physical geography, atmospheric science, or earth sciences are particularly encouraged to enrol. No prior programming or remote sensing experience is required, though familiarity with basic data interpretation is expected.
