This course provides a rigorous examination of the principles and practices underlying the design, integration, and evaluation of modern computer systems, with emphasis on the interplay between hardware architecture, operating systems, and software engineering. Students will develop a comprehensive understanding of system-level constraints, performance trade-offs, and the engineering decisions that govern the construction of reliable, efficient, and scalable computing platforms. Topics include processor organization, memory hierarchies, I/O subsystems, system reliability, and the co-design of hardware and software components.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of COMP-4100, students will be able to:

- Analyze and evaluate the architectural components of modern computer systems, including CPU pipelines, cache hierarchies, and memory management units, in terms of performance and design trade-offs.
- Apply system engineering principles to design and integrate hardware and software subsystems, demonstrating awareness of reliability, fault tolerance, and scalability requirements.
- Critically assess the impact of operating system abstractions — including process scheduling, virtual memory, and I/O management — on overall system behaviour and performance.
- Develop and interpret low-level system programs and benchmarks to measure, profile, and optimize the performance of a computing system under real-world workloads.
- Communicate technical design decisions and system evaluation findings clearly and professionally through written reports and oral presentations appropriate to an engineering context.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following before enrolling in COMP-4100:

- **COMP-2210: Computer Organization and Architecture** or equivalent (minimum grade: C+)
- **COMP-2340: Operating Systems Fundamentals** or equivalent (minimum grade: C+)
- **COMP-2150: Data Structures and Algorithm Analysis** (minimum grade: C)
- **MATH-2010: Discrete Mathematics for Computing** or **MATH-2050: Linear Algebra and Applications**

Students who have not completed the listed prerequisites must obtain written departmental approval prior to registration. It is strongly recommended that students have prior experience with a systems programming language (C or C++) before enrolling.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-4100 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| System Design Project (Group) | 25% |
| Laboratory Assignments (×4) | 20% |
| Technical Report and Oral Presentation | 10% |

**Examinations:** Two formal written examinations will assess students' theoretical understanding of computer system architecture, operating system interfaces, and system engineering principles. The final examination is cumulative.

**System Design Project:** Conducted in groups of three to four students, this capstone project requires teams to design, implement, and benchmark a functional subsystem (e.g., a custom memory allocator, a simplified scheduling simulator, or an I/O management module). A final written report and group oral presentation are required.

**Laboratory Assignments:** Four structured laboratory exercises will provide hands-on experience with profiling tools, system-level programming, hardware performance counters, and inter-process communication. Labs are completed individually unless otherwise specified.

**Technical Report:** A formal individual technical report (minimum 2,500 words) documenting the student's contributions to the group project, supported by data, diagrams, and a critical evaluation of design decisions made throughout the semester.
