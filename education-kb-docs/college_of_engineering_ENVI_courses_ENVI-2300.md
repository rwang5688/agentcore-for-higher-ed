## Course Description

### Course Description

Introduction to Permaculture (ENVI-2300) provides students with a foundational understanding of permaculture design principles and their application in sustainable land management, food systems, and ecological restoration. Students will explore the ethics, philosophy, and practical techniques of permaculture as a framework for designing resilient human habitats that work in harmony with natural systems. This course integrates theoretical study with hands-on design exercises, preparing students to apply permaculture concepts across diverse environmental and engineering contexts.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the core ethics and principles of permaculture and articulate their relevance to contemporary environmental challenges
- Identify and analyse natural patterns, ecological relationships, and site-specific conditions as the basis for sustainable design
- Apply permaculture zoning, sector analysis, and design methodologies to produce basic site plans for residential or small-scale agricultural settings
- Evaluate the role of permaculture in food security, water management, soil health, and biodiversity conservation
- Communicate and present permaculture design proposals clearly, demonstrating awareness of social, cultural, and environmental considerations

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolment:

- ENVI-1100: Foundations of Environmental Science, or equivalent
- ENVI-1150: Introduction to Ecology and Natural Systems, or equivalent

Students with prior demonstrated experience in sustainable agriculture, landscape design, or related fields may apply to the Department of Environment and Natural Resources for prerequisite waiver consideration.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weighting | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written examination covering permaculture ethics, principles, and foundational theory |
| Design Portfolio | 35% | A series of progressively developed design exercises culminating in a complete site design submission |
| Field Observation Report | 15% | A structured written report based on a site visit to a working permaculture or sustainable agricultural system |
| Final Design Project | 25% | A collaborative group project requiring students to develop a comprehensive permaculture design proposal for an assigned site, including oral presentation |
| Participation & Studio Engagement | 5% | Active contribution to studio workshops, peer critique sessions, and in-class design activities |

### Required Materials

Students are expected to obtain the following materials prior to the first week of class:

- **Hargreave, D. & Sutcliffe, M.** (2019). *Living Systems Design: An Introduction to Permaculture Principles and Practice* (3rd ed.). Greenhollow Academic Press.
- **Vance, R. T.** (2021). *Soil, Water, and Canopy: Ecological Foundations for Sustainable Land Design*. Terraverde Publishing.
- **Osei-Bonsu, A., & Lindqvist, P.** (2020). *Pattern Languages in the Landscape: A Designer's Field Companion*. Northfield University Press.

Additional readings, case studies, and design templates will be made available through the Peculiar U course management portal. Students are also required to bring drawing materials, including graph paper, pencils, and a ruler, to all studio sessions.
