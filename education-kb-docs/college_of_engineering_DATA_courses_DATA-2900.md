## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to linear regression analysis and statistical modeling as foundational tools in data science. Students will explore simple and multiple linear regression, model diagnostics, and the assumptions underlying linear models, with an emphasis on applying these techniques to real-world datasets. Through a combination of theoretical grounding and hands-on computational practice, students will develop the skills necessary to construct, evaluate, and interpret predictive models.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Formulate and estimate simple and multiple linear regression models using both matrix algebra and computational tools
- Assess model fit and validate core assumptions of linear regression, including linearity, homoscedasticity, independence, and normality of residuals
- Identify and address common modeling challenges such as multicollinearity, outliers, and influential observations
- Apply variable selection techniques and model comparison criteria (e.g., AIC, BIC, adjusted R²) to build parsimonious and interpretable models
- Communicate the results of regression analyses clearly and accurately through written reports and data visualizations

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **DATA-1200: Introduction to Data Science** or equivalent
- **MATH-1410: Introductory Statistics and Probability**
- **MATH-1350: Calculus I**

A working familiarity with at least one statistical computing environment (R, Python, or equivalent) is strongly recommended. Students without formal programming experience are advised to consult their instructor during the first week of classes.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Lab Assignments (6 total) | 25% |
| Regression Modeling Project | 20% |
| Participation & Quizzes | 10% |

**Lab Assignments** are completed using statistical software and submitted via the course portal. Each assignment targets a specific set of modeling techniques covered in the preceding lectures.

**Regression Modeling Project:** Students will independently identify a real-world dataset, develop a regression model addressing a defined research question, and submit both a written report (approximately 1,500–2,000 words) and a short oral presentation during the final week of the term. Projects are evaluated on analytical rigor, interpretive clarity, and the quality of visual communication.

**Examinations** are closed-book but permit one double-sided formula sheet. The final examination is cumulative.
