### Course Description

This course introduces students to the foundational principles and techniques of inferential statistics, equipping them with the tools to draw meaningful conclusions about populations based on sample data. Topics covered include hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, regression analysis, analysis of variance (ANOVA), and the application of statistical reasoning within the context of data science. Students will develop both theoretical understanding and practical competency through hands-on engagement with real-world datasets and statistical software.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Distinguish between descriptive and inferential statistical approaches, and select appropriate methods based on the nature and structure of a given dataset.
- Formulate, execute, and interpret hypothesis tests including t-tests, chi-square tests, and ANOVA procedures, with correct application of significance levels and p-values.
- Construct and interpret confidence intervals for population parameters, demonstrating an understanding of sampling distributions and the Central Limit Theorem.
- Apply simple and multiple linear regression techniques to model relationships between variables and evaluate model fit using relevant diagnostic measures.
- Communicate statistical findings clearly and accurately to both technical and non-technical audiences, with an awareness of common errors in statistical interpretation.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in DATA-2300 is evaluated through a combination of continuous assessment and formal examination, designed to test both conceptual understanding and applied analytical skills.

- **Midterm Examination (25%)** — A written, closed-book examination held at the midpoint of the semester, covering hypothesis testing, sampling distributions, and confidence intervals. Questions will include both short-answer theoretical problems and applied data interpretation tasks.
- **Statistical Analysis Project (30%)** — Students will complete an independent project in which they identify a research question, collect or obtain an appropriate dataset, apply inferential methods covered in the course, and present their findings in a structured written report of approximately 2,000–2,500 words. Projects are submitted in two stages: a proposal (Week 6) and a final report (Week 13).
- **Weekly Lab Assignments (20%)** — Ten graded lab assignments completed using statistical software (R or Python, student's choice), each designed to reinforce weekly lecture content through practical data analysis exercises. Assignments are submitted via the course portal and graded on accuracy and clarity of interpretation.
- **Final Examination (25%)** — A comprehensive, closed-book examination administered during the official examination period, covering all course material. The final examination will include multiple-choice, short-answer, and extended-response questions, with a particular emphasis on the integration and application of inferential techniques across diverse data scenarios.

A passing grade in both the project component and the final examination is required to pass the course overall. Students who fail either component will be required to undertake a supplementary assessment as permitted under university policy.
