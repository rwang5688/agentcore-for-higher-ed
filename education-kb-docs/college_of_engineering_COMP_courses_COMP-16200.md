## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced graduate-level course examines search engine technology from a comprehensive interdisciplinary perspective, integrating core concepts in information retrieval, algorithmic design, and large-scale distributed systems with broader considerations of societal impact and commercial strategy. Students will critically analyze the architecture underlying modern search platforms, including crawling, indexing, ranking, and query processing pipelines, while situating these technical systems within the ethical, regulatory, and business contexts that shape their development and deployment. The course draws on current research, industry case studies, and hands-on implementation exercises to prepare students for leadership roles at the intersection of search technology, policy, and product development.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Design and critically evaluate core search engine components, including web crawlers, inverted index structures, and relevance ranking algorithms such as TF-IDF and PageRank variants, demonstrating mastery of state-of-the-art information retrieval methodologies.
- Analyze the societal and ethical implications of algorithmic search systems, including issues of bias, misinformation propagation, filter bubbles, privacy, and the disproportionate influence of dominant search platforms on public knowledge access.
- Assess the business models and competitive strategies of major search engine operators, including advertising ecosystems, monetization frameworks, data asset leverage, and the dynamics of platform market power.
- Apply machine learning and natural language processing techniques — including semantic search, neural ranking models, and query intent classification — to improve search quality and user experience in applied project settings.
- Synthesize technical, societal, and commercial considerations to formulate evidence-based policy recommendations and product strategies pertaining to the governance and future development of search technologies.

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced graduate standing required for COMP-16200 (Level 780), students are expected to have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency:

- **COMP-14100: Algorithms and Data Structures** or equivalent graduate-level coursework in algorithm design and complexity analysis
- **COMP-14350: Database Systems and Information Retrieval** or demonstrated familiarity with indexing structures and query processing
- **COMP-15200: Machine Learning Fundamentals** or equivalent preparation in supervised and unsupervised learning methods
- Proficiency in at least one high-level programming language (Python strongly recommended) and comfort working with large datasets
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or written instructor permission; students from Computer Science, Information Science, or related disciplines are particularly encouraged to enroll
