### Course Description

This course examines the complex and evolving relationship between the United States and the world's forest ecosystems, exploring how American consumption patterns, foreign policy, trade agreements, and conservation movements have shaped — and continue to shape — forests across six continents. Students will investigate the historical and contemporary roles of U.S. institutions, corporations, NGOs, and citizens in both the degradation and protection of global forest resources. Drawing on perspectives from environmental science, political economy, and cultural history, the course situates American engagement with forests within broader frameworks of colonialism, globalization, and ecological sustainability.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the historical trajectories of U.S. involvement in global deforestation and reforestation efforts, identifying key political, economic, and cultural drivers across different time periods and regions.
- Evaluate the environmental and socioeconomic impacts of American demand for timber, agricultural commodities, and paper products on tropical, boreal, and temperate forest biomes worldwide.
- Critically assess the role of U.S.-based international conservation organizations, bilateral aid programs, and multilateral environmental agreements in shaping forest governance in the Global South.
- Apply interdisciplinary frameworks — integrating concepts from ecology, political science, and environmental justice — to case studies of forest loss, restoration, and contested land use in regions including the Amazon Basin, Southeast Asia, and Central Africa.
- Develop and defend evidence-based policy recommendations addressing American responsibility and potential leadership in global forest conservation and sustainable resource management.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Participation & Discussion | 10% | Active, informed engagement in seminar discussions and small-group activities throughout the semester |
| Reading Response Papers | 15% | Six short responses (400–600 words each) to assigned readings, submitted prior to class on designated weeks |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | In-class examination combining short-answer and essay questions covering material from the first half of the course |
| Regional Case Study Paper | 25% | A 2,500–3,000 word analytical paper examining U.S. influence on forest dynamics in a student-selected world region, incorporating peer-reviewed sources and primary documents |
| Policy Brief & Presentation | 30% | A formal 1,500–2,000 word policy brief addressing a current issue in American engagement with global forests, accompanied by a 12–15 minute group presentation delivered to an audience of peers and invited faculty |

Late submissions will be penalized at 5% per calendar day unless an extension has been granted in writing by the supervising instructor no fewer than 48 hours before the deadline.
