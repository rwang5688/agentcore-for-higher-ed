## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-7900 provides an advanced, rigorous exploration of the methodologies and techniques used to optimize the performance of deep neural networks in real-world applications. Students will examine the theoretical foundations and practical implementation of hyperparameter tuning, regularization strategies, and gradient-based optimization algorithms, with an emphasis on diagnosing and resolving common training challenges. This course is designed for graduate-level students seeking to develop expert-level proficiency in building, refining, and deploying high-performance deep learning systems.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Systematically identify, tune, and evaluate key hyperparameters—including learning rates, batch sizes, and network architecture parameters—using both manual and automated search strategies such as grid search, random search, and Bayesian optimization.
- Apply and critically compare regularization techniques, including L1/L2 regularization, dropout, batch normalization, and early stopping, to mitigate overfitting and improve model generalization.
- Implement and analyze a range of advanced optimization algorithms—such as momentum-based gradient descent, RMSprop, Adam, and AdaGrad—and articulate the conditions under which each is most appropriate.
- Diagnose training pathologies including vanishing and exploding gradients, saddle points, and poor weight initialization, and apply established corrective strategies to address these issues.
- Design and execute structured experimentation pipelines to iteratively improve deep learning model performance, documenting and interpreting results with rigor appropriate to academic and professional standards.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-7900 must have satisfactorily completed the following, or received explicit instructor approval:

- **COMP-6200: Foundations of Machine Learning** or equivalent graduate-level course in machine learning theory and practice
- **COMP-6450: Introduction to Deep Learning** or demonstrated working knowledge of feedforward networks, backpropagation, and convolutional architectures
- **MATH-5110: Linear Algebra and Multivariate Calculus for Data Science** or equivalent coursework demonstrating proficiency in matrix operations, partial differentiation, and vector calculus
- Proficiency in Python and familiarity with at least one major deep learning framework (TensorFlow or PyTorch) is strongly recommended and assumed throughout the course

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-7900 will be evaluated through a combination of theoretical and applied assessments designed to reflect both conceptual mastery and practical competence:

- **Problem Sets (20%):** Four structured problem sets distributed throughout the semester, covering hyperparameter search strategies, regularization mathematics, and optimization algorithm derivations. Problem sets are to be completed individually.
- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written closed-book examination assessing theoretical understanding of regularization and optimization concepts covered in the first half of the course. The examination will include both analytical problem-solving and short-answer conceptual questions.
- **Lab Assignments (20%):** Three hands-on programming labs requiring students to implement and benchmark optimization and regularization techniques on standard benchmark datasets using PyTorch or TensorFlow. Lab reports documenting methodology, results, and analysis are required.
- **Research Paper Review and Presentation (15%):** Each student will select a peer-reviewed paper from a major venue (e.g., NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR) on a topic related to course content, prepare a written critical review (1,500–2,000 words), and deliver a 15-minute in-class presentation.
- **Final Project (25%):** A team-based applied project in which groups of two to three students identify a deep learning problem, apply hyperparameter tuning, regularization, and optimization strategies to improve a baseline model, and submit a comprehensive written report (5,000–7,000 words) along with a reproducible codebase. Projects will be presented in a final-week symposium format.

*All assessments must be submitted in accordance with Peculiar University's Academic Integrity Policy. Use of generative AI tools for completing assessments is prohibited unless explicitly authorized in writing by the course instructor.*
