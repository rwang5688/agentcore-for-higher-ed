## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced doctoral-level seminar provides a comprehensive examination of Software as a Service (SaaS) architectures, business models, and engineering paradigms within modern cloud computing ecosystems. Students will engage critically with the full lifecycle of SaaS product development, from multi-tenant system design and API-driven integration to subscription economics and service-level engineering. The course bridges theoretical frameworks with applied industry practice, preparing students to architect, evaluate, and lead the development of enterprise-grade SaaS solutions.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Design and critically evaluate scalable, multi-tenant SaaS architectures that address security, performance, and data isolation requirements at an enterprise level
- Analyze and apply SaaS-specific software engineering principles, including continuous deployment pipelines, microservices decomposition, and API lifecycle management
- Synthesize the technical and business dimensions of SaaS products, including pricing model structures, customer onboarding systems, and service-level agreement (SLA) engineering
- Formulate and defend original research contributions addressing open problems in SaaS reliability, scalability, or governance
- Critically assess emerging trends in SaaS delivery, including AI-integrated services, platform ecosystems, and regulatory compliance frameworks across global markets

### Prerequisites

Given the doctoral level of this course (980), students are expected to have completed or demonstrate equivalent competency in the following:

- COMP-71200 Cloud Computing Architectures or equivalent graduate-level cloud systems coursework

- Doctoral standing in Computer Science, Software Engineering, or a closely related discipline, or explicit written permission from the supervising instructor

### Assessment Methods

**Research Seminar Participation (10%)**
Students are expected to engage substantively in weekly seminar discussions, critically responding to assigned readings, peer presentations, and guest lectures from industry practitioners. Quality of contribution is weighted over quantity.

**Critical Literature Reviews (15%)**
Two formal literature reviews will be submitted across the semester, each surveying recent peer-reviewed scholarship and industry white papers on assigned SaaS topics. Reviews should demonstrate doctoral-level synthesis and identify gaps warranting further investigation.

**Architecture Design Project (25%)**
Working individually or in pairs, students will produce a complete SaaS system architecture proposal for a defined problem domain. Deliverables include a technical specification document, a security and compliance analysis, and a formal design presentation delivered to the seminar group and evaluated by a faculty panel.

**Original Research Paper (35%)**
Students will produce a conference-quality research paper (8,000–12,000 words) addressing a novel research question within the SaaS domain. The paper must follow an approved academic venue format and will be submitted in stages: proposal, draft, peer review response, and final submission. Students are encouraged to target submission to relevant venues following course completion.

**Doctoral Qualifying Examination (15%)**
A comprehensive written examination assessing mastery of core SaaS concepts, architectural principles, and the scholarly literature covered throughout the course. The examination includes both short-answer technical questions and an extended critical essay component.
