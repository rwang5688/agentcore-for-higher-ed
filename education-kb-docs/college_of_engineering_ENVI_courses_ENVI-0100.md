## Course Description

### Course Description

This introductory course examines the fundamental concepts of climate change and the strategies societies, ecosystems, and institutions employ to adapt to its wide-ranging effects. Students will explore the scientific basis of a changing climate alongside the social, economic, and policy dimensions of adaptive responses at local, regional, and global scales. No prior background in environmental science is required, making this course accessible to students from all disciplines who wish to develop a foundational understanding of one of the defining challenges of the modern era.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the basic mechanisms of climate change and distinguish between mitigation and adaptation as complementary response strategies
- Identify the physical, ecological, and human systems most vulnerable to climate change impacts and describe the factors that contribute to that vulnerability
- Evaluate a range of adaptation strategies — including infrastructure modification, ecosystem-based approaches, and community resilience planning — in terms of their feasibility and effectiveness
- Analyse how social equity, governance structures, and economic resources shape communities' capacity to adapt to climate-related risks
- Communicate climate adaptation concepts clearly to diverse audiences through written and oral formats

### Prerequisites

There are no formal prerequisites for this course. It is open to all students enrolled at Peculiar U, regardless of programme or prior academic background. A general curiosity about environmental issues and a willingness to engage with both scientific and social perspectives are strongly encouraged.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Participation & Attendance | 10% | Active engagement in lectures, seminars, and class discussions |
| Weekly Reading Responses | 15% | Short written reflections (200–300 words) submitted following assigned readings |
| Midterm Examination | 25% | In-class written exam covering Weeks 1–6; includes multiple choice and short-answer questions |
| Adaptation Case Study Report | 25% | Individual written report (1,200–1,500 words) analysing a real-world climate adaptation initiative |
| Final Group Presentation | 25% | Small-group presentation proposing an adaptation strategy for a selected community or region, assessed on research quality, feasibility, and delivery |

Assessment is designed to build progressively throughout the semester, allowing students to develop their analytical and communication skills in stages. Students are encouraged to consult instructors during office hours for guidance on written and group assignments.
