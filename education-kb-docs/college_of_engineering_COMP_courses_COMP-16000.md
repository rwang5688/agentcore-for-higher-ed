## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-16000: Ray Tracing for Global Illumination provides an advanced exploration of physically based rendering techniques, with a focus on the mathematical foundations and algorithmic design behind ray tracing systems used to simulate realistic light transport. Students will investigate global illumination models including path tracing, photon mapping, and bidirectional methods, developing a thorough understanding of how light interacts with surfaces, volumes, and participating media. This course bridges theoretical rendering science with practical implementation, preparing students to design and optimize high-fidelity rendering pipelines for use in research, interactive applications, and production environments.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Derive and implement core ray tracing algorithms, including Whitted-style ray tracing, Monte Carlo path tracing, and Metropolis Light Transport, demonstrating an understanding of their theoretical basis and computational trade-offs
- Analyze and apply the rendering equation and its approximations to simulate physically accurate global illumination, including indirect lighting, caustics, and subsurface scattering
- Design and optimize acceleration data structures such as Bounding Volume Hierarchies (BVH) and kd-trees to improve ray intersection performance in complex scenes
- Evaluate and compare sampling strategies—including importance sampling, stratified sampling, and quasi-Monte Carlo methods—and apply them to reduce variance in rendered output
- Develop a functional, well-documented ray tracing renderer as part of a semester-long project, demonstrating proficiency in shader design, material modeling, and render pipeline integration

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |

| Weekly Problem Sets | 10% |

**Progressive Renderer Implementation Project:** Students will build an increasingly sophisticated ray tracer over the course of the semester, beginning with a basic Whitted-style system and advancing through path tracing, volumetric rendering, and at least one advanced illumination technique of the student's choosing. Milestone submissions are required at Weeks 4, 8, and 12, with a final submission and live demonstration at the end of the semester.

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations are written and include a combination of mathematical derivations, algorithm analysis questions, and short conceptual responses. The final examination is cumulative with an emphasis on topics introduced in the second half of the course.

**Research Paper & Presentation:** Each student will select a seminal or contemporary paper from the rendering literature, produce a critical written analysis (2,500–3,500 words), and deliver a 20-minute in-class presentation summarizing the work and its implications for modern rendering systems.

**Weekly Problem Sets:** Short problem sets are assigned weekly and focus on mathematical derivations, pseudocode analysis, and conceptual application of lecture material.
