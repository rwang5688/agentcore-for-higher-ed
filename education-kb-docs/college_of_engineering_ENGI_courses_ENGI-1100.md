## Course Description

### Course Description

Convex Optimization I provides a rigorous introduction to the theoretical foundations and computational methods underlying convex optimization, with emphasis on applications in engineering design, signal processing, and systems analysis. Students will examine convex sets, convex functions, and duality theory, developing both analytical reasoning and practical problem-solving skills essential to modern engineering practice. This course establishes the mathematical groundwork necessary for advanced study in optimization, machine learning, and control systems.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and characterize convex sets and convex functions, and apply fundamental properties of convexity to engineering problems
- Formulate real-world engineering problems as convex optimization programs, including linear programs (LPs), quadratic programs (QPs), and second-order cone programs (SOCPs)
- Apply duality theory, including Lagrangian duality and KKT conditions, to analyze and solve constrained optimization problems
- Implement and evaluate numerical optimization algorithms using industry-standard computational tools, interpreting results within applied engineering contexts
- Critically assess the limitations of convex models and identify problem structures amenable to convex relaxation techniques

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 30% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Computational Project | 25% |
| Final Examination | 20% |
| Participation and In-Class Work | 5% |

**Problem Sets:** Six graded problem sets will be distributed throughout the semester, requiring students to apply theoretical concepts and computational techniques covered in lectures. Collaboration is permitted on a limited basis per the course collaboration policy outlined in the syllabus.

**Midterm Examination:** A closed-book, in-person examination covering Modules 1 through 4. A single hand-written reference sheet (8.5" × 11", both sides) is permitted.

**Computational Project:** Students will identify an engineering problem from their area of specialization, formulate it as a convex optimization program, implement a solution using CVX or an equivalent solver, and present findings in a written report (15–20 pages) and a 15-minute oral presentation during the final two weeks of the semester.

**Final Examination:** A comprehensive closed-book examination covering all course material. The same reference sheet policy as the midterm applies.

**Participation:** Active engagement during lectures, recitation sections, and office hours will be assessed qualitatively by the instructional team.
