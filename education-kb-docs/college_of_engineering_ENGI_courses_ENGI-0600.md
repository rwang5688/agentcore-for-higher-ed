## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-0600 provides an advanced examination of cellular solid materials — including foams, honeycombs, lattices, and porous structures — with emphasis on the relationships between microstructural architecture and mechanical, thermal, and acoustic properties. Students will engage with both classical analytical frameworks and contemporary computational modeling techniques to characterize and predict the behavior of cellular materials across a range of engineering applications. The course draws upon interdisciplinary knowledge spanning materials science, structural mechanics, and manufacturing to prepare students for research and professional practice in advanced materials design.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the geometric and topological features of cellular solid architectures and explain how relative density, cell geometry, and connectivity govern macroscopic material behavior
- Apply established scaling laws and constitutive models to predict the mechanical response — including elastic moduli, plastic collapse, and fracture behavior — of open- and closed-cell cellular materials
- Evaluate thermal, acoustic, and fluid transport properties of cellular solids and assess their performance in multi-functional engineering applications
- Design and optimize cellular material architectures for targeted performance criteria using computational and analytical tools, including finite element analysis
- Critically review and synthesize current primary literature to identify emerging research directions in additive manufacturing, bio-inspired cellular design, and functional gradient structures

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrollment:

- ENGI-0410 or equivalent: Mechanical Behavior of Materials
- ENGI-0420 or equivalent: Continuum Mechanics
- MATL-0350 or equivalent: Introduction to Materials Science and Engineering
- Graduate standing in Engineering or a closely related discipline, or instructor consent

Students are additionally expected to have working proficiency in at least one numerical analysis or finite element software environment (e.g., MATLAB, ANSYS, or ABAQUS).

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Design and Analysis Project | 30% |
| Literature Review Paper | 15% |
| Problem Sets (6 total) | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess students' comprehension of core theoretical frameworks, scaling relations, and property prediction methods for cellular solids. Both examinations include analytical problem-solving and conceptual short-answer components.

**Design and Analysis Project** requires students, working individually or in pairs, to design a cellular solid structure for a specified engineering application, perform mechanical and/or thermal analysis using finite element software, and present findings in both a written technical report and an oral presentation to the class.

**Literature Review Paper** (approximately 3,500–5,000 words) requires students to survey a minimum of fifteen peer-reviewed sources on a focused topic within cellular solids research, synthesize current knowledge, and identify open questions or opportunities for future investigation.

**Problem Sets** are distributed biweekly and cover computational, analytical, and conceptual problems drawn directly from lecture and reading material.
