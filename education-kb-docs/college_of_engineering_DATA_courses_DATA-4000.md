## Course Description

**DATA-4000 | The Data Scientist's Toolbox | 3 Credit Points**
*Department of Data Science, College of Engineering*

---

This course provides an integrated survey of the core computational tools, programming environments, and methodological frameworks that define contemporary data science practice. Students will develop hands-on proficiency with industry-standard platforms for data acquisition, wrangling, analysis, and visualization, situating each tool within a broader workflow context. Emphasis is placed on reproducibility, collaborative development practices, and the critical evaluation of tool selection across diverse data science problem domains.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate proficiency in essential data science programming environments, including version-controlled scripting, package management, and interactive notebook workflows
- Apply systematic data wrangling and transformation techniques to messy, real-world datasets using appropriate computational libraries and tools
- Design and execute end-to-end data science pipelines that prioritize reproducibility, documentation, and collaborative best practices
- Critically evaluate and select appropriate tools and frameworks given the scale, structure, and domain context of a given analytical problem
- Communicate analytical processes and findings effectively through structured technical reporting and visual presentation of results

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in DATA-4000 must have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency:

- **DATA-2200: Foundations of Data Science** or **CS-2150: Introduction to Programming for Data Applications**
- **MATH-2410: Probability and Statistical Reasoning**
- Demonstrated working knowledge of at least one high-level programming language (Python or R strongly recommended)
- Sophomore standing or above (minimum 45 credit points completed)

Students who have not completed the formal prerequisites may petition the Department of Data Science for a prerequisite waiver, subject to instructor approval and evidence of equivalent preparation.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly Lab Assignments (×10) | 25% |
| Midterm Practical Examination | 20% |
| Collaborative Pipeline Project | 25% |
| Technical Portfolio Submission | 20% |
| Participation & Peer Code Review | 10% |

**Weekly Lab Assignments:** Ten structured laboratory exercises assigned throughout the semester, each targeting a specific tool or workflow component covered in lecture. Submissions must include fully reproducible code and a brief written reflection.

**Midterm Practical Examination:** A timed, hands-on examination administered in the department's computing lab. Students will be presented with a novel dataset and a set of analytical tasks to complete using tools and methods covered in the first half of the course.

**Collaborative Pipeline Project:** Completed in teams of three to four, this project requires students to design, implement, document, and present a complete data science workflow addressing a real-world problem of the team's choosing. A written technical report and a live demonstration are required at the end of the semester.

**Technical Portfolio Submission:** Students will curate and submit a personal portfolio of their work throughout the course, hosted in a version-controlled repository, accompanied by a reflective commentary documenting their growth in tool fluency and methodological thinking.

**Participation & Peer Code Review:** Active contribution to in-class discussions and formal written peer reviews of at least two classmates' lab submissions are required.
