### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous and application-focused introduction to the principles and practice of deep learning, equipping students with the theoretical foundations and hands-on skills necessary to design, train, and evaluate neural network architectures for real-world problems. Topics include feedforward networks, convolutional and recurrent architectures, optimization strategies, regularization techniques, and deployment considerations in modern computational environments. Students will engage with contemporary frameworks and datasets to bridge the gap between theoretical understanding and practical implementation at an advanced level.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of the mathematical and statistical foundations underlying modern deep learning models, including gradient-based optimization, backpropagation, and loss function design.
- Design and implement deep neural network architectures—including CNNs, RNNs, and transformer-based models—using industry-standard frameworks to address classification, regression, and generative tasks.
- Critically evaluate model performance through appropriate validation strategies, interpret diagnostic metrics, and apply regularization and hyperparameter tuning techniques to mitigate overfitting and improve generalization.
- Conduct independent applied research by formulating a deep learning problem, selecting and preprocessing relevant datasets, and systematically experimenting with architectural and training choices to produce reproducible results.
- Communicate technical findings and model design decisions clearly and effectively in both written and oral formats appropriate to a professional or academic audience.

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed all of the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-84200 – Machine Learning: Theory and Methods** or equivalent, with a grade of B or higher
- **COMP-76110 – Applied Linear Algebra for Computational Sciences** or equivalent
- **STAT-71400 – Probability and Statistical Inference for Engineers** or equivalent
- Demonstrated proficiency in Python programming, including experience with NumPy, pandas, and at least one scientific computing environment; proficiency may be established via **COMP-60300 – Scientific Computing in Python** or a faculty-approved competency assessment
- Graduate standing or instructor permission required; this course is restricted to Level 990 (doctoral and advanced postgraduate) students

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Sets (×4) | 20% | Four structured assignments assessing mathematical derivations, conceptual understanding, and implementation exercises across core topics |
| Midterm Examination | 15% | Written in-class examination covering theoretical foundations, architectural design principles, and optimization methods |
| Lab Portfolio | 20% | A curated collection of completed guided laboratory exercises demonstrating implementation competency across CNNs, RNNs, and attention mechanisms |
| Research Paper | 20% | A graduate-level written report (approximately 6,000–8,000 words) presenting an original applied deep learning investigation, including literature review, methodology, results, and critical discussion |
| Final Project | 20% | An end-to-end deep learning system developed in teams of two to three students, accompanied by a public-facing code repository, a technical report, and a formal oral presentation to course faculty |
| Participation & Seminar Engagement | 5% | Active engagement in weekly seminar discussions, peer code reviews, and invited lecture sessions |

*A minimum overall grade of B− is required for this course to count toward degree requirements in the Computer Science doctoral program.*
