## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the foundational principles of Bitcoin and broader cryptocurrency technologies, exploring the cryptographic, economic, and computational mechanisms that underpin decentralized digital currencies. Students will examine the architecture of blockchain systems, consensus protocols, and the technical design of peer-to-peer financial networks. Emphasis is placed on developing both theoretical understanding and practical analytical skills relevant to the rapidly evolving landscape of digital assets and distributed ledger technology.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the cryptographic primitives underlying Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies, including hash functions, digital signatures, and public-key cryptography
- Describe the structure and operation of blockchain technology, including how blocks are constructed, validated, and appended to a distributed ledger
- Analyze consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work and Proof of Stake, and evaluate their respective security and efficiency trade-offs
- Identify and critically assess the economic incentives, network dynamics, and scalability challenges inherent to decentralized cryptocurrency systems
- Evaluate emerging cryptocurrency technologies and use cases, including smart contracts, altcoins, and decentralized finance (DeFi) applications

---

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-1100: Introduction to Computer Science** or equivalent foundational programming coursework
- **COMP-1150: Discrete Mathematics for Computing** or equivalent (covering basic number theory, logic, and set theory)
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.0 is recommended

Students without prior programming experience are strongly advised to consult with their academic advisor before registering. Familiarity with basic algebra and introductory statistics is assumed throughout the course.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Programming Assignments (3) | 25% |
| Research Paper | 15% |
| Weekly Quizzes | 10% |
| Participation & Discussion | 5% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess students' comprehension of cryptographic concepts, blockchain architecture, and consensus mechanisms through a combination of multiple-choice, short-answer, and analytical questions.

**Programming Assignments** require students to implement foundational components of cryptocurrency systems, such as a simplified hash function demonstration, a basic blockchain data structure, and a transaction verification script, using Python or another approved language.

**Research Paper** (approximately 1,800–2,500 words) tasks students with independently investigating a contemporary issue in the cryptocurrency space — such as regulatory challenges, environmental impact, or a specific protocol comparison — and presenting a well-reasoned, evidence-based argument.

**Weekly Quizzes** are brief assessments administered at the start of selected class sessions to reinforce reading comprehension and lecture material.

### Required Materials and Textbooks

Students are expected to obtain the following materials before the first week of class:

1. **Nakagawa, S., & Ellford, T. R.** (2021). *Decentralized Systems: Cryptography and Consensus in the Digital Age* (3rd ed.). Harwick Academic Press. ISBN: 978-1-74832-091-4

2. **Castellane, M. B.** (2020). *Blockchain Fundamentals: Architecture, Protocols, and Applications*. Pinnacle Technical Publishing. ISBN: 978-0-56271-338-7

3. **Osei-Bonsu, D., & Throckmorton, L.** (2022). *Digital Currencies and the Future of Finance: An Analytical Approach*. Meridian University Press. ISBN: 978-1-88204-467-2

Additionally, students will be required to access a number of peer-reviewed articles, technical white papers (including the original Bitcoin white paper by Satoshi Nakamoto), and supplementary readings made available through the Peculiar U library portal. A functioning laptop capable of running Python 3.x or later is required for all programming assignments.
