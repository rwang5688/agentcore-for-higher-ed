### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive examination of the regulatory frameworks governing energy production, distribution, and consumption, with particular emphasis on the environmental consequences of energy policy decisions at local, national, and international levels. Students will critically analyse the intersections between energy law, environmental protection statutes, and sustainable development objectives, drawing on contemporary case studies from both developed and emerging economies. The course further explores the evolving landscape of renewable energy regulation, carbon markets, and the legal mechanisms available to reconcile industrial energy demands with ecological preservation.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and interpret the principal legislative and regulatory instruments governing the energy sector, including emissions standards, licensing regimes, and environmental impact assessment requirements.
- Evaluate the environmental implications of diverse energy regulatory frameworks, applying critical analysis to real-world policy outcomes across different jurisdictions.
- Assess the role of governmental bodies, international organisations, and non-governmental actors in shaping energy and environmental policy.
- Demonstrate an understanding of market-based environmental instruments, including carbon trading schemes, renewable energy credits, and energy efficiency mandates, and appraise their effectiveness in achieving sustainability goals.
- Synthesise technical, legal, and environmental considerations to propose and defend evidence-based recommendations for regulatory reform in the energy sector.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-0800 must have successfully completed the following, or obtained written permission from the course coordinator:

- **ENVI-0210** – Environmental Law and Policy *(or equivalent)*

Students are also expected to have a working familiarity with introductory economics and basic quantitative methods as acquired through general undergraduate study at the 200 level or above.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weighting | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book written examination covering regulatory frameworks and core theoretical concepts addressed in the first half of the course. |
| Regulatory Analysis Paper | 25% | An individual written paper (2,500–3,000 words) critically analysing an energy regulation of the student's choosing, assessing its environmental effectiveness and identifying areas for reform. |
| Group Policy Simulation Project | 30% | Students will work in small teams to simulate a regulatory negotiation scenario, presenting their proposed energy-environment policy framework to a panel of instructors in a formal oral presentation. A written report (3,500–4,000 words per group) is also required. |
| Final Examination | 25% | A comprehensive open-note examination requiring extended analytical responses, integrating regulatory, environmental, and policy dimensions covered across the full course. |

*Attendance and active participation in seminar discussions are expected of all students. While not formally graded, consistent non-participation may be noted and may affect borderline grade decisions at the discretion of the instructors.*
