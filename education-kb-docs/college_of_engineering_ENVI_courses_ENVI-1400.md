## Course Description

### Course Description

This course examines the complex and dynamic relationships between food systems and environmental processes, exploring how agricultural production, food distribution, and consumption patterns shape and are shaped by ecological conditions. Students will investigate topics including land use, water resources, biodiversity, climate change, and the socio-environmental dimensions of food security at local, regional, and global scales. Drawing on interdisciplinary perspectives from environmental science, ecology, and systems thinking, the course equips students to critically evaluate the sustainability of contemporary and emerging food systems.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the environmental impacts of conventional and alternative agricultural systems, including effects on soil health, water quality, and atmospheric composition
- Evaluate the role of food production and supply chains in driving land-use change, biodiversity loss, and greenhouse gas emissions
- Apply systems-level thinking to assess the feedback relationships between ecological processes and food security under conditions of environmental stress
- Critically interpret quantitative and qualitative data pertaining to resource use efficiency, ecological footprints, and sustainability metrics within food systems
- Formulate evidence-based arguments and policy recommendations addressing the tension between food production demands and long-term environmental integrity

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (Level 950), students are expected to have completed the following or receive instructor approval prior to enrolment:

- **ENVI-6200** – Foundations of Environmental Systems, or equivalent
- **ENVI-6350** – Ecology and Natural Resource Management, or equivalent
- Demonstrated competency in quantitative data analysis, either through prior coursework or professional experience
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or a closely related discipline is strongly recommended

Students without formal prerequisite coursework but with substantial professional experience in agriculture, environmental management, or food systems policy may petition the Department of Environment and Natural Resources for a prerequisite waiver.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Seminar Participation | 10% | Active engagement in weekly discussions, peer critique, and in-class exercises |
| Critical Response Papers (×3) | 15% | Short analytical papers (800–1,000 words each) responding to assigned readings and current events in food-environment policy |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written examination covering core conceptual frameworks, case studies, and technical content from the first half of the course |
| Systems Analysis Project | 25% | Individual or paired project requiring students to conduct an in-depth environmental assessment of a selected food system or supply chain, culminating in a written report (3,500–5,000 words) |
| Final Research Paper | 30% | A substantial independent research paper (6,000–8,000 words) addressing an original question at the intersection of food production and environmental change, incorporating primary data, peer-reviewed literature, and a policy implications section |

All written work must adhere to the departmental style guide. Late submissions will be penalized at 5% per calendar day unless an extension is approved in advance by the supervising instructor.
