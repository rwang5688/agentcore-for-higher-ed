## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6100 provides an advanced exploration of the principles, processes, and applications of nanoscale manufacturing, bridging fundamental nanoscience with practical engineering implementation. Students will investigate the design and fabrication of nanoscale structures and devices using both top-down and bottom-up manufacturing approaches, with emphasis on precision, scalability, and material behavior at the nanometer scale. The course integrates cutting-edge research with real-world industrial contexts, preparing graduates to contribute meaningfully to fields such as biomedical engineering, semiconductor fabrication, and advanced materials development.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Evaluate and compare top-down and bottom-up nanofabrication techniques, including lithography, self-assembly, chemical vapor deposition, and atomic layer deposition, selecting appropriate methods for given engineering applications
- Analyze the unique physical, chemical, and mechanical properties of materials at the nanoscale and explain how quantum and surface effects influence manufacturing outcomes
- Design nanoscale structures and systems using industry-standard modeling and simulation tools, demonstrating an understanding of process constraints and tolerability thresholds
- Critically assess quality control, metrology, and characterization methods — including scanning electron microscopy (SEM), atomic force microscopy (AFM), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) — within nanomanufacturing workflows
- Synthesize knowledge of safety protocols, environmental considerations, and ethical responsibilities specific to nanomaterial handling, production, and industrial scale-up

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment in ENGI-6100:

- **ENGI-4200: Advanced Materials Science and Engineering** or equivalent, with a minimum grade of B-
- **ENGI-4410: Microfabrication and Semiconductor Processing**
- **CHEM-3300: Physical Chemistry II** or **PHYS-3350: Quantum Mechanics for Engineers**
- **ENGI-3150: Engineering Mathematics III** (covering partial differential equations and Fourier analysis)

Students without the listed prerequisites who believe they possess equivalent preparation must seek written instructor approval no later than one week prior to the commencement of the term. Graduate standing is strongly recommended.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Reports (×4) | 20% |
| Research Paper | 20% |
| Process Design Project | 15% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations are written, closed-book assessments covering theoretical concepts, problem-solving scenarios, and critical analysis of nanomanufacturing processes and case studies. The final examination is cumulative.

**Laboratory Reports:** Students will complete four supervised laboratory sessions — including AFM characterization, thin-film deposition, nanolithography, and nanoparticle synthesis — and submit a formal technical report for each session within two weeks of completion. Reports must follow IEEE formatting standards.

**Research Paper:** Each student will produce an independently written research paper (minimum 4,000 words) reviewing an emerging nanomanufacturing technology or process. Papers must draw on a minimum of fifteen peer-reviewed sources published within the last seven years and will be evaluated on technical rigor, scholarly writing, and depth of critical analysis.

**Process Design Project:** Working in teams of three to four, students will design a complete nanomanufacturing process for a specified device application. Teams will submit a written process specification document and deliver a formal oral presentation (20 minutes plus Q&A) to a panel of course instructors during the final two weeks of term.
