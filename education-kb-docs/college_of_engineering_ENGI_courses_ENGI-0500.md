### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous foundation in the principles and applications of electronic circuits, devices, and systems at an advanced undergraduate and graduate entry level. Students will examine the behavior of passive and active components, circuit analysis techniques, and the fundamentals of analog and digital electronics within both theoretical and applied engineering contexts. Emphasis is placed on developing practical laboratory skills alongside a strong conceptual understanding of electronic systems design and troubleshooting.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and solve DC and AC circuit problems using established techniques including Kirchhoff's Laws, Thévenin's theorem, and nodal analysis
- Identify, characterize, and apply the operational principles of core electronic components including resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, and transistors
- Design and evaluate basic analog circuits, including amplifiers, filters, and rectifiers, to meet specified performance criteria
- Interpret and construct circuit schematics, breadboard prototypes, and printed circuit layouts using industry-standard tools and conventions
- Apply systematic diagnostic and troubleshooting methodologies to identify and resolve faults in electronic circuits and systems

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-0500 must have successfully completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency:

- **ENGI-0300: Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering** or **PHYS-0210: Physics II – Electricity and Magnetism** (minimum grade of C+)
- **MATH-0320: Differential Equations and Linear Algebra** (minimum grade of C)
- Prior laboratory experience in a physical sciences or engineering discipline is strongly recommended

Students who are uncertain whether their background meets prerequisite requirements are encouraged to consult with the course instructor or departmental advisor prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Reports (6 submissions) | 25% |
| Circuit Design Project | 20% |
| Problem Sets and Quizzes | 10% |

**Examinations:** Two formal examinations will assess students' theoretical knowledge and quantitative problem-solving skills. The midterm is held in-class; the final examination is administered during the official examination period.

**Laboratory Reports:** Students will complete six structured laboratory sessions throughout the semester, submitting formal reports for each. Reports must include data analysis, circuit diagrams, and a discussion of results relative to theoretical predictions.

**Circuit Design Project:** A capstone design project completed individually or in pairs, requiring students to design, simulate, build, and present a functional electronic circuit addressing a defined engineering problem. A written technical report and brief oral presentation to the instructional team are required.

**Problem Sets and Quizzes:** Weekly problem sets and periodic short quizzes reinforce core concepts and prepare students for examination components.
