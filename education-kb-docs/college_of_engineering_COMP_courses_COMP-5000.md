## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the field of data science, examining its foundational principles, methodologies, and real-world applications across diverse industries. Students will explore the full data science pipeline, from data acquisition and cleaning through analysis, modeling, and interpretation, developing a critical understanding of how data-driven insights inform decision-making. Emphasis is placed on bridging theoretical frameworks with practical tools and ethical considerations central to modern data science practice.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Define the scope and interdisciplinary nature of data science, distinguishing it from related fields such as statistics, machine learning, and data engineering
- Apply fundamental data wrangling and exploratory analysis techniques to real-world datasets using industry-standard programming tools
- Evaluate and select appropriate analytical and predictive modeling methods based on the characteristics of a given dataset and problem context
- Interpret and communicate data-driven findings clearly and accurately to both technical and non-technical audiences
- Identify and critically assess ethical concerns in data science practice, including issues of bias, privacy, data governance, and algorithmic accountability

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-5000 must have successfully completed the following:

- **COMP-3200: Foundations of Programming** or demonstrated equivalent programming proficiency
- **MATH-3100: Applied Statistics and Probability**
- **COMP-3410: Introduction to Databases and Data Management**

Students without formal prerequisites who possess substantial professional experience in a quantitative or computing-related discipline may petition the Department of Computer Science for a prerequisite waiver. Approval is at the discretion of the course coordinator.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Closed-book written exam covering theoretical concepts, data science definitions, pipeline stages, and foundational methodologies |
| Final Examination | 25% | Comprehensive written exam encompassing all course material, including case analysis and short-form applied problem sets |
| Data Analysis Project | 30% | A multi-stage individual project in which students select a real-world dataset, formulate a research question, conduct exploratory and predictive analysis, and present findings in a structured written report |
| Lab Assignments (×5) | 15% | Weekly guided laboratory exercises applying course concepts using Python or R; submitted via the course portal |
| Participation & Discussion Posts | 10% | Active engagement in weekly seminar discussions and submission of reflective posts responding to assigned readings and case studies |

*Note: A minimum grade of 50% must be achieved on the combined examination components to pass the course regardless of overall weighted average.*

### Required Materials

Students are expected to obtain the following materials prior to the first week of instruction. All texts are available through the Peculiar U Library digital lending portal.

- **Jaganathan, F., & Welser, M. (2022).** *The Data Science Landscape: Principles, Practice, and Purpose* (3rd ed.). Northgate Academic Press. — Primary course text covering foundational concepts, pipeline methodology, and disciplinary context.

- **Luedeke, L., & Brizzee, F. (2021).** *Wrangling the World: A Practical Guide to Data Acquisition, Cleaning, and Exploration*. Crestline Technical Publishers. — Supplementary applied text used primarily in laboratory sessions.

- **Verhey, M. (2023).** *Ethics in the Age of Algorithms: Data, Society, and Responsibility* (2nd ed.). Ironwood University Press. — Required reading for ethics modules and discussion post assignments.

- **Course Reader: COMP-5000 Selected Readings** — A compiled packet of peer-reviewed articles, industry white papers, and case studies assembled by the instructional team; available for purchase at the Peculiar U Campus Bookstore or as a digital download via the course management system.

Students are additionally required to have access to a laptop capable of running **Python 3.10+** and **R 4.2+**. Installation guides and recommended package lists will be distributed during the first laboratory session. No specific software purchase is required; all tools used in this course are open-source and freely available.
