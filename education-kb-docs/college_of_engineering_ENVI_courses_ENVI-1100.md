## Course Description

### Course Description

ENVI-1100 provides an advanced examination of the legal frameworks, regulatory structures, and policy mechanisms governing environmental protection and natural resource management at domestic, transnational, and international levels. Students will critically analyze the interplay between statutory law, administrative rulemaking, judicial interpretation, and policy development as applied to pressing environmental challenges including climate change, pollution control, biodiversity loss, and resource extraction. The course draws on interdisciplinary perspectives from law, engineering, economics, and the natural sciences to equip students with the analytical tools necessary to navigate and contribute to complex environmental governance landscapes.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and interpret major environmental statutes, regulations, and international agreements, and evaluate their practical effectiveness in achieving stated environmental goals
- Critically assess the roles and responsibilities of governmental agencies, courts, legislative bodies, and non-governmental stakeholders in the formulation and enforcement of environmental law and policy
- Apply legal reasoning and policy analysis to real-world environmental disputes, regulatory proceedings, and resource management scenarios
- Evaluate the tensions between economic development imperatives and environmental protection objectives, including considerations of environmental justice and equity
- Synthesize technical and scientific evidence within legal and policy frameworks to develop well-reasoned arguments and recommendations for environmental governance reform

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-1100 should have successfully completed the following or demonstrate equivalent academic preparation:

- ENVI-4200: Foundations of Environmental Science and Governance

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Regulatory Analysis Paper | 25% |
| Policy Reform Proposal Project | 20% |
| Seminar Participation & Case Discussions | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations assess students' comprehension of statutory frameworks, case law, regulatory principles, and policy theory. The final examination is cumulative and includes short-answer and extended essay components requiring students to apply legal reasoning to novel fact patterns.

**Regulatory Analysis Paper:** Students will produce an individual research paper (4,000–5,500 words) examining a specific environmental regulation or legal instrument of their choosing. The paper must evaluate the regulation's legislative history, implementation record, enforcement challenges, and policy outcomes.

**Policy Reform Proposal Project:** Completed in small groups, this project requires students to identify a documented gap or failure in existing environmental law or policy, research the relevant legal and scientific literature, and present a structured reform proposal. Groups will deliver a formal oral presentation to the class and submit a written report (3,000–4,000 words per group).

**Seminar Participation & Case Discussions:** Active, informed participation in weekly seminars and structured case discussions is expected. Students will be evaluated on the quality, consistency, and analytical depth of their contributions.
