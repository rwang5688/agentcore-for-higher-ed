## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-5600: Deep Learning Explained provides a rigorous and comprehensive exploration of deep learning architectures, algorithms, and their practical applications across diverse domains. Students will examine the theoretical foundations underpinning neural networks, including optimization strategies, regularization techniques, and the mathematical frameworks that drive modern artificial intelligence systems. Emphasis is placed on bridging theoretical understanding with hands-on implementation, equipping students to design, evaluate, and deploy deep learning solutions to real-world problems.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Articulate the mathematical and computational principles underlying deep learning models, including feedforward networks, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and transformer architectures.
- Design and implement deep learning pipelines using contemporary frameworks, demonstrating proficiency in data preprocessing, model construction, training, and performance evaluation.
- Apply advanced techniques such as transfer learning, attention mechanisms, and generative modelling to solve complex, domain-specific problems.
- Critically analyse the limitations, ethical implications, and computational costs associated with deploying large-scale deep learning systems in real-world environments.
- Communicate technical findings and model interpretability results effectively through written reports and oral presentations to both specialist and non-specialist audiences.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolment:

- **COMP-3210: Machine Learning Fundamentals** — foundational exposure to supervised and unsupervised learning methods, model evaluation, and feature engineering.
- **COMP-3185: Data Structures and Algorithms II** — advanced understanding of algorithmic complexity and computational thinking.
- **MATH-3310: Linear Algebra and Multivariate Calculus** — proficiency in matrix operations, gradient computation, and vector calculus essential for understanding network optimization.
- **STAT-2200: Probability and Statistical Inference** — working knowledge of probability distributions, Bayesian reasoning, and statistical modelling.

Students lacking one or more prerequisites may seek written permission from the course coordinator. Prior programming experience in Python is strongly recommended.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written in-class examination covering theoretical concepts from the first half of the course, including network architectures and optimization methods. |
| Implementation Assignments (×3) | 30% | Three progressive coding assignments requiring students to implement, train, and evaluate deep learning models on provided and self-sourced datasets. |
| Research Paper Review | 15% | A structured critical analysis (1,500–2,000 words) of a peer-reviewed deep learning paper selected from an approved reading list. |
| Group Capstone Project | 25% | A collaborative project in which student teams of 3–4 design, train, and present an original deep learning system addressing a defined problem. Includes a written report and live demonstration. |
| Final Examination | 10% | A cumulative written examination assessing integration of course concepts, conducted during the university's official examination period. |

Late submissions are subject to a 10% penalty per day unless prior accommodation has been arranged with the instructor.
