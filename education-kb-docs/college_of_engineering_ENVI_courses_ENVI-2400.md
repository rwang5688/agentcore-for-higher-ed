## Course Description

### Course Description

This course introduces students to the foundational principles of microeconomic theory and examines their application to the allocation, use, and management of natural resources. Topics include supply and demand analysis, market structures, externalities, public goods, and cost-benefit analysis as they relate to environmental and resource economics. Students will develop analytical frameworks for evaluating policy interventions, resource scarcity, and the economic dimensions of environmental challenges.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain and apply core microeconomic concepts — including utility maximization, production theory, and market equilibrium — within the context of natural resource systems
- Analyse market failures such as externalities, open-access problems, and public goods as they pertain to environmental and resource management scenarios
- Apply cost-benefit analysis and valuation techniques to assess trade-offs in natural resource allocation and environmental policy
- Evaluate the economic rationale for various regulatory and market-based instruments, including taxes, subsidies, cap-and-trade systems, and property rights frameworks
- Interpret and critically assess empirical data and economic models relevant to natural resource issues at local, national, and global scales
