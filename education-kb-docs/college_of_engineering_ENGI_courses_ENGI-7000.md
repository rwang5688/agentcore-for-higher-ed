## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous and comprehensive treatment of the Fourier Transform and its theoretical foundations, with emphasis on practical engineering applications across signal processing, communications, imaging, and system analysis. Students will develop facility with both continuous and discrete Fourier methods, exploring the mathematical structures that underpin modern engineering analysis and design. Connections to related integral transforms, including the Laplace and Z-transforms, are examined alongside numerical implementation strategies relevant to contemporary engineering practice.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Derive and apply the continuous Fourier Transform and its inverse to analyze signals and linear time-invariant systems in both time and frequency domains
- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) and the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) algorithm, including their computational complexity and practical limitations
- Apply Fourier methods to engineering problems in areas such as filter design, spectral analysis, image processing, and communications systems
- Evaluate the relationships between the Fourier Transform and related transforms, including Laplace, Z-transform, and short-time Fourier analysis, selecting appropriate tools for a given engineering context
- Implement numerical Fourier analysis techniques using standard engineering computing environments and critically assess the effects of sampling, windowing, and aliasing on real-world data

---

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-7000 must have successfully completed the following, or demonstrate equivalent preparation:

- **ENGI-5200: Signals and Linear Systems** – foundational treatment of continuous and discrete-time signals, convolution, and LTI systems
- **MATH-4110: Advanced Engineering Mathematics** – covering complex analysis, differential equations, and introductory transform methods
- **ENGI-4450: Digital Signal Processing I** or equivalent exposure to sampling theory and basic filter design
- Graduate standing in the College of Engineering, or written permission of the supervising instructor

Students without the recommended background in complex variables or linear algebra are strongly encouraged to consult with the course coordinator before registering.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 total) | 30% | Bi-weekly assignments covering theoretical derivations, transform calculations, and applied engineering problems |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | In-class written examination testing core Fourier theory and foundational application techniques |
| Computational Project | 25% | Individual or paired project requiring implementation and analysis of Fourier methods on a real or synthetic engineering dataset, submitted with written technical report |
| Final Examination | 20% | Comprehensive written examination covering all course material, with emphasis on integration of concepts |
| Participation & In-Class Problems | 5% | Engagement with worked examples, in-class exercises, and discussion of engineering case studies |

The computational project requires submission of documented code (MATLAB, Python, or approved equivalent) alongside a formal technical report of approximately 2,500–4,000 words. Grading rubrics for all assessments are distributed at the start of term.

### Required Materials and Textbooks

Students are required to obtain the following materials prior to the first week of instruction:

1. **Hargreaves, D. F., & Tsvetkov, M. (2019).** *Spectral Methods in Engineering Analysis: Fourier Theory and Transform Techniques* (3rd ed.). Aldermoor Academic Press. — Primary course textbook; all problem set readings are drawn from this volume.

2. **Oyelaran, B. C. (2021).** *The Discrete Fourier Transform: Algorithms, Applications, and Computational Perspectives* (2nd ed.). Westbrook Engineering Series, Halloway & Sons Publishing. — Supplementary text for the DFT and FFT modules; selected chapters are required reading.

3. **Kesselman, R., & Novotná, P. (2017).** *Applied Signal Analysis: A Laboratory Workbook for Graduate Engineers* (1st ed.). Peculiar University Press. — Workbook used in conjunction with the computational project and laboratory exercises; available for purchase through the university bookstore or digital download via the university library portal.

All three texts are placed on two-hour course reserve at the Peculiar University Brennan-Holloway Engineering Library. Additional journal articles, lecture notes, and supplementary readings will be distributed through the course learning management system throughout the term.
