### Course Description

This course provides a foundational introduction to the principles governing the motion of spacecraft and other bodies in space, with emphasis on orbital mechanics, trajectory analysis, and the forces acting on vehicles beyond Earth's atmosphere. Students will explore Kepler's laws, Newton's law of gravitation, and the mathematical frameworks used to describe, predict, and manipulate orbital paths. Practical applications including satellite deployment, orbital transfers, and interplanetary mission design are examined throughout the course.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Describe and apply Kepler's laws of planetary motion and Newton's universal law of gravitation to solve problems involving two-body orbital systems
- Classify and analyze different orbit types — including circular, elliptical, parabolic, and hyperbolic — using appropriate mathematical and graphical methods
- Calculate orbital parameters such as period, velocity, altitude, and eccentricity for spacecraft in Earth orbit and beyond
- Apply the concepts of orbital maneuvers, including Hohmann transfers and plane changes, to evaluate mission design scenarios
- Interpret and construct basic trajectory diagrams and translate physical scenarios into equations of motion within an inertial reference frame

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in AERO-0200 must have successfully completed the following:

- **MATH-0101** – Calculus I (minimum grade: C)
- **MATH-0102** – Calculus II (minimum grade: C)
- **PHYS-0101** – Introductory Classical Mechanics (minimum grade: C)

A working knowledge of vectors, trigonometry, and Newton's laws of motion is strongly assumed. Students without the recommended background are advised to consult the instructor prior to enrollment.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 25% |

| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 25% |
| Orbital Mission Design Project | 15% |
| In-Class Participation & Quizzes | 5% |

**Problem Sets** are assigned approximately every two weeks and involve both analytical derivations and numerical calculations. **The Orbital Mission Design Project** is a semester-long individual assignment in which students design a simplified mission trajectory for a hypothetical spacecraft, culminating in a written technical report submitted during the final week of classes. Examinations are closed-book but permit the use of a university-approved formula sheet provided by the instructor.
