### Course Description

This advanced graduate course provides a rigorous examination of the legal frameworks, regulatory mechanisms, and policy instruments designed to address the multifaceted challenges of climate change at local, national, and international levels. Students will critically evaluate the effectiveness of existing climate governance structures, including treaty regimes, emissions trading schemes, and domestic environmental legislation, while exploring the tensions between economic development imperatives and ecological sustainability. The course integrates perspectives from environmental law, public policy, and engineering practice to equip students with the analytical tools necessary to engage meaningfully with real-world climate governance challenges.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically analyse the architecture of international climate law, including foundational agreements such as the UNFCCC, the Kyoto Protocol, and the Paris Agreement, and assess their practical enforceability and limitations within sovereign legal systems.
- Evaluate domestic and regional regulatory approaches to greenhouse gas mitigation and adaptation, including carbon pricing mechanisms, renewable energy mandates, and land-use planning frameworks, in terms of their legal robustness and policy effectiveness.
- Apply interdisciplinary methodologies to assess the intersection of engineering practice and environmental law, identifying the legal responsibilities and compliance obligations facing professionals in technical and industrial sectors.
- Synthesise competing stakeholder perspectives — including those of government bodies, civil society, indigenous communities, and private industry — to develop balanced, evidence-informed recommendations for climate policy reform.
- Demonstrate advanced proficiency in legal and policy research, including the identification and interpretation of primary legal sources, regulatory instruments, and comparative case law relevant to climate governance.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-0600 are expected to have completed or demonstrate equivalent competency in the following:

- **ENVI-0400 Environmental Law and Governance** or **ENVI-0410 Introduction to Environmental Policy** (or approved equivalent)
- **ENVI-0420 Climate Science for Policy Professionals** or demonstrated foundational knowledge of climate systems and their socioeconomic dimensions
- Graduate standing (Level 500 or above) within the College of Engineering or a closely related discipline
- Proficiency in academic legal and policy research methods, as evidenced by prior coursework or professional experience

Students from non-law backgrounds who have not completed a formal environmental law prerequisite are strongly encouraged to consult with the course coordinator prior to enrolment to determine suitability and any recommended preparatory readings.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Policy Analysis Paper | 30% | An individual written analysis (3,500–4,500 words) examining a selected national or subnational climate policy instrument. Students will assess the legal basis, implementation mechanisms, and measurable outcomes of their chosen policy, with critical reference to scholarly and legal sources. |
| Moot Policy Negotiation Exercise | 20% | A structured group simulation in which students represent assigned state or non-state actors in a multilateral climate negotiation scenario. Assessed on the quality of legal argumentation, negotiation strategy, and collaborative engagement. |
| Comparative Law Research Essay | 35% | A substantive research essay (5,000–6,500 words) comparing climate litigation or regulatory frameworks across at least two jurisdictions. Students are expected to demonstrate command of primary legal sources and engage critically with current academic debate. |
| Seminar Participation and Reflective Portfolio | 15% | Active, informed contribution to weekly seminar discussions, complemented by a short reflective portfolio (five entries of approximately 300 words each) documenting the student's evolving engagement with course themes and any shifts in analytical perspective. |

*All written submissions must adhere to the Peculiar U Graduate School citation guidelines. Late submissions will be subject to the standard University penalty policy unless a formal extension has been approved in advance.*
