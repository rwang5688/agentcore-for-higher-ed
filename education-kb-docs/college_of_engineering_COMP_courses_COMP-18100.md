## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-18100: Wireless Networking provides an advanced examination of the principles, architectures, and protocols underpinning modern wireless communication systems. Students will explore the theoretical foundations and practical implementations of wireless networks, including cellular systems, Wi-Fi standards, and emerging technologies such as 5G and beyond. The course emphasizes both design considerations and the analytical skills required to evaluate network performance, security vulnerabilities, and optimization strategies in real-world deployment scenarios.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the fundamental principles of wireless signal propagation, modulation techniques, and spectrum management as they apply to contemporary network design
- Evaluate and compare major wireless networking standards and protocols, including IEEE 802.11 variants, Bluetooth, and cellular architectures (3G through 5G)
- Design and configure wireless network topologies with appropriate consideration for coverage, capacity, interference mitigation, and quality of service
- Assess security threats and vulnerabilities inherent to wireless environments and implement appropriate countermeasures and encryption frameworks
- Apply quantitative performance analysis methods to diagnose, troubleshoot, and optimize wireless network systems under varying load and environmental conditions

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through a combination of the following components:

- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written examination covering theoretical foundations, wireless standards, and protocol analysis from the first half of the course. Questions will include both short-answer and problem-solving formats.
- **Final Examination (25%):** A comprehensive written examination encompassing all course material, with emphasis on integration of concepts across wireless system design, security, and performance evaluation.
- **Laboratory Assignments (25%):** A series of six structured lab sessions in which students configure, test, and analyze wireless network environments using industry-standard simulation and hardware tools. Lab reports are required for each session.
- **Research Paper (15%):** An individually authored academic paper (3,000–4,500 words) examining a current or emerging wireless technology or deployment challenge. Papers must adhere to IEEE citation formatting guidelines and will be evaluated for technical depth, clarity, and scholarly rigor.
- **Capstone Network Design Project (15%):** A team-based project (groups of 3–4) requiring students to design, document, and present a comprehensive wireless network solution for a specified organizational scenario. Deliverables include a written technical report and a formal in-class presentation evaluated by faculty panel.
