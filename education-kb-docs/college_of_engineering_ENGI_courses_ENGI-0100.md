## Course Description

### Course Description

Advanced Analog Integrated Circuits (ENGI-0100) provides students with a foundational introduction to the principles and design methodologies underlying analog integrated circuit systems. This course explores the behaviour of fundamental analog building blocks, including operational amplifiers, voltage regulators, and signal conditioning circuits, within integrated environments. Students will develop both theoretical understanding and practical skills necessary for analysing and evaluating analog circuit performance in real-world engineering contexts.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and describe the fundamental components and topologies used in analog integrated circuit design
- Analyse the behaviour of basic analog building blocks such as amplifiers, filters, and comparators under varying operating conditions
- Apply core circuit analysis techniques, including small-signal modelling and frequency response analysis, to integrated analog systems
- Interpret manufacturer datasheets and technical documentation to evaluate the suitability of analog integrated components for given applications
- Demonstrate basic laboratory skills in constructing, testing, and measuring the performance of simple analog integrated circuits

### Prerequisites

As a 100-level introductory course, ENGI-0100 has no formal prerequisites. However, students are strongly encouraged to have completed secondary-level physics with a concentration in electricity and magnetism, and to possess a working familiarity with basic algebraic and arithmetic principles. Prior exposure to fundamental circuit concepts, such as Ohm's Law and Kirchhoff's Laws, is considered highly beneficial and will support student success in this course.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 25% |
| Final Examination | 35% |
| Laboratory Reports (×4) | 20% |
| Problem Sets (×6) | 15% |
| In-Class Participation & Quizzes | 5% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess students' theoretical understanding of analog circuit principles and their ability to perform quantitative circuit analysis under timed conditions.

**Laboratory Reports** are submitted following each scheduled practical session and require students to document experimental procedures, present measured data, and critically compare empirical results against theoretical predictions.

**Problem Sets** are assigned at regular intervals throughout the term and are designed to reinforce lecture content through structured analytical exercises.

**In-Class Participation and Quizzes** include short unannounced quizzes and active engagement in discussions, rewarding consistent attendance and preparation.

Students are also required to bring a non-programmable scientific calculator to all examinations. Access to simulation software (CircuitSim Pro, institutional licence provided) will be introduced in Week 3 and used throughout the laboratory component of the course.
