### Course Description

This advanced course provides a rigorous treatment of dynamic behavior in chemical engineering systems, with an emphasis on the mathematical modeling of process transients and the design of feedback and feedforward control strategies. Students will examine the principles underlying process identification, stability analysis, and controller tuning within the context of industrial chemical processes. Special attention is given to the integration of modern computational tools with classical control theory to address real-world process optimization and safety challenges.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Develop and analyze dynamic mathematical models for chemical processes, including continuous stirred-tank reactors, distillation columns, and heat exchangers, using ordinary differential equations and Laplace transform methods.
- Design and evaluate proportional-integral-derivative (PID) control systems, applying systematic tuning methods such as Ziegler-Nichols, Cohen-Coon, and model-based approaches to achieve desired closed-loop performance.
- Conduct stability analyses of process control systems using frequency-domain techniques, including Bode plots, Nyquist diagrams, and root locus methods, to assess gain and phase margins.
- Apply advanced control strategies — including cascade control, ratio control, feedforward compensation, and multivariable control concepts — to complex, industrially relevant process scenarios.
- Utilize process simulation and control software to model dynamic systems, implement control algorithms, and critically interpret simulation outputs in support of engineering decision-making.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-0700 will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written examination covering dynamic modeling, Laplace transform analysis, and introductory control system design. The examination will include both analytical problem-solving and conceptual questions.
- **Final Examination (25%):** A comprehensive written examination assessing all course content, with an emphasis on the integration of modeling, analysis, and advanced control strategies as developed across the full term.
- **Problem Sets (20%):** A series of structured assignments distributed throughout the semester, requiring students to apply theoretical concepts and computational methods to progressively complex process control problems.
- **Control System Design Project (25%):** A major individual or paired project in which students model a chemical process of their choosing, design and justify a full control scheme, and present findings in a formal written engineering report adhering to professional standards.
- **Laboratory / Simulation Practicals (10%):** Scheduled computational laboratory sessions in which students implement and test control algorithms using process simulation software, submitting concise technical reports for each session.
