## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced doctoral-level course provides a rigorous examination of the physical principles, engineering methodologies, and emerging technologies underlying the direct conversion of solar and thermal energy into electrical power. Students will engage with both established and cutting-edge conversion systems, including photovoltaic devices, thermoelectric generators, thermophotovoltaic systems, and concentrating solar technologies, with emphasis on material properties, system efficiency, and real-world implementation challenges. The course integrates theoretical analysis with applied design frameworks to prepare graduates for research and professional leadership in renewable energy conversion engineering.

### Learning Objectives

- Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the thermodynamic and quantum mechanical principles governing solar-to-electrical and thermal-to-electrical energy conversion processes
- Analyze and compare the performance characteristics, efficiency limits, and material constraints of photovoltaic, thermoelectric, and thermophotovoltaic conversion systems
- Design and evaluate integrated solar/thermal conversion systems using advanced computational modeling and simulation tools
- Critically assess current research literature to identify gaps, emerging innovations, and pathways toward next-generation conversion technologies
- Develop and defend original research proposals or engineering design solutions that address efficiency, scalability, or cost barriers in direct energy conversion applications

### Assessment Methods

**Laboratory Design Projects (35%)**
Students will complete two substantive design projects over the course of the semester. The first requires the computational modeling and performance analysis of a photovoltaic or thermoelectric system under defined operational constraints. The second is an open-ended advanced design project in which students propose, model, and evaluate an original or significantly improved conversion technology. Both projects require formal written reports and oral defense presentations to the instructional team.

**Research Paper and Seminar Presentation (25%)**
Each student will author an original research paper of graduate caliber (minimum 8,000 words) critically examining a current topic in direct solar or thermal energy conversion. Papers must engage substantively with recent peer-reviewed literature. Students will present their findings in a formal departmental seminar setting and respond to questions from faculty and peers.

**Midterm Examination (20%)**
A comprehensive written examination covering foundational conversion principles, efficiency theory (including Shockley-Queisser and Carnot limits), material science considerations, and system architecture. Questions will include both analytical problem-solving and critical synthesis components.

**Final Examination (15%)**
A closed-book cumulative examination emphasizing integration of course concepts, advanced problem-solving in conversion system design, and evaluation of hypothetical engineering scenarios presented by the examination committee.

**Participation and Doctoral Seminar Engagement (5%)**
Active, substantive participation in weekly doctoral seminars, peer critique sessions, and guest lecturer discussions is required and assessed on quality of contribution throughout the term.
