### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive examination of the principles, methodologies, and practical techniques underlying the security of modern computer systems, including operating systems, networks, and application environments. Students will investigate threat modeling, vulnerability analysis, cryptographic foundations, and defensive architecture design within both enterprise and distributed computing contexts. Emphasis is placed on developing a security-first mindset, equipping students to identify, assess, and mitigate real-world threats through both theoretical understanding and hands-on technical practice.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and analyze common attack vectors, system vulnerabilities, and threat models applicable to modern computing environments, including malware, privilege escalation, and injection-based exploits
- Apply core cryptographic principles — including symmetric and asymmetric encryption, hashing, and digital signatures — to design and evaluate secure communication and data protection strategies
- Demonstrate proficiency in the use of industry-standard security tools for penetration testing, intrusion detection, log analysis, and vulnerability scanning within controlled, ethical laboratory environments
- Evaluate the security posture of a given system or network architecture and produce structured risk assessments with prioritized remediation recommendations aligned to established frameworks such as NIST and ISO/IEC 27001
- Articulate the legal, ethical, and professional responsibilities associated with cybersecurity practice, including relevant legislation, disclosure standards, and codes of conduct governing security research

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following before enrolling in COMP-4200:

- **COMP-2150: Operating Systems and Architecture** — foundational understanding of OS internals, process management, memory systems, and file structures is assumed throughout the course
- **COMP-2310: Networking Fundamentals** — proficiency in TCP/IP protocols, network topology, and packet-level communication is required for the network security modules
- **COMP-3010: Introduction to Algorithms and Data Structures** — ability to read and reason about code at an intermediate level is essential for vulnerability analysis exercises
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.0 within the Department of Computer Science is required for enrollment

Students without the above prerequisites who believe they have equivalent preparation through professional experience may petition the Department of Computer Science for a prerequisite waiver prior to the enrollment deadline.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components. Final grade breakdowns are detailed in the course syllabus distributed by the assigned instructor at the start of term.

**Midterm Examination (20%)**
A closed-book, timed written examination covering theoretical content from the first half of the course, including cryptography, access control models, authentication systems, and threat classification. Some sections may include structured short-answer and scenario-based analysis questions.

**Final Examination (25%)**
A comprehensive closed-book examination administered during the university's formal examination period. Students will be expected to integrate knowledge across all course modules, including practical concepts introduced in laboratory sessions.

**Security Audit and Risk Assessment Report (20%)**
Students will produce an individual written report of approximately 2,500–3,500 words in which they conduct a structured security audit of a provided case study system. Reports must include a documented threat model, identified vulnerabilities mapped to CVE or OWASP categories, and a prioritized remediation plan.

**Capture the Flag (CTF) Laboratory Portfolio (20%)**
Students will complete a series of supervised, ethics-bounded CTF challenges over the course of the term. A written reflection and technical write-up documenting methods, tools used, and lessons learned must accompany each completed challenge. Portfolios are submitted at the end of term.

**Group Security Architecture Design Project (15%)**
Working in teams of three to four, students will design a security architecture proposal for a fictional organization scenario provided by the instructor. Deliverables include a written proposal, a formal architecture diagram, and a 20-minute in-class presentation with Q&A.

**Participation and In-Class Activities (0%)**
Regular attendance and active engagement in laboratory sessions and class discussions are expected and may be considered in borderline grading situations at instructor discretion.
