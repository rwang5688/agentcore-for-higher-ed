This advanced course provides a rigorous examination of the theory and practical application of digital signal processing techniques, including discrete-time systems, transform analysis, and filter design. Students will investigate the mathematical foundations underlying modern DSP algorithms and explore their implementation in real-world engineering contexts such as communications, audio processing, and control systems. Emphasis is placed on bridging theoretical understanding with computational tools to analyse, design, and evaluate signal processing systems.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyse discrete-time signals and systems using z-transforms, Discrete Fourier Transforms (DFT), and the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) algorithm
- Design and evaluate digital filters, including both Finite Impulse Response (FIR) and Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) architectures, to meet specified performance criteria
- Apply sampling theory, quantisation principles, and the Nyquist-Shannon theorem to real-world signal acquisition and reconstruction problems
- Implement DSP algorithms using industry-standard computational tools and assess their performance in terms of computational complexity and numerical precision
- Critically evaluate DSP system designs within applied engineering domains, including communications, biomedical signal processing, and audio engineering

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (Level 850), students are expected to have substantial prior knowledge in relevant foundational areas. The following prerequisites are required:

- Completion of an undergraduate degree in Electrical Engineering, Computer Engineering, or a closely related discipline, or equivalent demonstrated competency
- Prior coursework in signals and systems, including continuous-time signal analysis and Laplace transforms
- Proficiency in linear algebra and advanced calculus, including complex number analysis
- Demonstrated experience with at least one scientific computing environment (e.g., MATLAB, Python/NumPy, or equivalent)
- Prior graduate-level standing or departmental approval required for enrolment

Students who are uncertain whether their background meets these requirements are encouraged to consult with their assigned instructor before the start of term.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-1300 is evaluated through a combination of theoretical, computational, and analytical assessment tasks designed to reflect both the depth and breadth of the discipline.

- **Problem Sets (25%):** A series of six structured problem sets distributed throughout the term, covering discrete-time analysis, transform methods, and filter design. Problem sets are to be submitted individually and are intended to reinforce lecture material and prepare students for examinations.
- **Computational Laboratory Projects (30%):** Three laboratory projects requiring students to implement DSP algorithms using approved computational tools. Projects include signal analysis of real-world datasets, digital filter design and validation, and a concluding project involving the design of a complete DSP pipeline for a specified engineering application.
- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A closed-book written examination covering foundational topics from the first half of the course, including sampling theory, z-transforms, and DFT analysis. A formula sheet approved by the course team will be provided.
- **Research Paper (10%):** A critical written paper of approximately 2,500–3,000 words in which students survey and evaluate a contemporary application of DSP technology, situating their analysis within current literature. Papers must conform to IEEE formatting standards and be submitted via the departmental academic writing portal.
- **Final Examination (15%):** A comprehensive written examination covering all course material, with emphasis on filter design methodologies, FFT applications, and system performance evaluation. The final examination is open-note, with restrictions as specified in the course schedule.

All assessments must be submitted in accordance with Peculiar University's Academic Integrity Policy. Late submissions will be penalised at 10% per calendar day unless an extension has been approved in advance by the supervising instructor.
