## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-4900: Cybersecurity Risk Management provides an advanced examination of the methodologies, frameworks, and analytical techniques used to identify, assess, and mitigate cybersecurity risks within organizational and enterprise environments. Students will explore the intersection of technical security controls, regulatory compliance, and strategic risk decision-making, with particular emphasis on real-world threat modeling and incident response planning. This course prepares students for professional roles in information security, risk advisory, and technology governance by bridging theoretical risk frameworks with applied security practice.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and classify cybersecurity threats, vulnerabilities, and assets using established risk assessment methodologies, including NIST, ISO 27001, and FAIR frameworks
- Develop and evaluate comprehensive risk treatment plans that align organizational security posture with business objectives and regulatory requirements
- Apply quantitative and qualitative risk analysis techniques to model potential security incidents and their projected financial and operational impacts
- Design and assess security control architectures appropriate to identified risk profiles, including preventive, detective, and corrective controls
- Interpret and communicate risk findings through professional documentation, including risk registers, audit reports, and executive-level risk briefings

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-4900 will be evaluated through the following components:

**Midterm Examination (20%)**
A written examination assessing students' understanding of core risk management frameworks, threat taxonomy, and vulnerability classification. The exam includes both short-answer and scenario-based analytical questions.

**Final Examination (20%)**
A comprehensive written examination covering all course material, with an emphasis on integrating risk assessment techniques and regulatory compliance considerations into coherent security strategies.

**Risk Assessment Project (30%)**
Students will conduct a full-cycle cybersecurity risk assessment for a simulated or approved real-world organization. The project includes asset identification, threat modeling, vulnerability analysis, a completed risk register, and a formal written report submitted in two stages: a project proposal (Week 5) and a final deliverable (Week 14). Students may work individually or in pairs with prior instructor approval.

**Case Study Analyses (15%)**
Three written case study responses distributed throughout the semester. Each analysis requires students to evaluate a documented cybersecurity incident or organizational failure, identify risk management breakdowns, and propose evidence-based remediation strategies. Approximately 800–1,200 words each.

**Weekly Lab Assignments (10%)**
Hands-on laboratory exercises completed during scheduled lab sessions, covering tools and techniques such as vulnerability scanning, risk matrix construction, control mapping, and threat modeling software.

**Participation and Professionalism (5%)**
Active engagement in seminar discussions, peer review sessions, and in-class scenario exercises. Students are expected to contribute meaningfully to group dialogue and demonstrate preparedness throughout the term.
