## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive examination of the principles governing the occurrence, movement, and distribution of groundwater within the hydrological cycle. Students will explore the physical and chemical properties of subsurface water systems, aquifer dynamics, and the interaction between groundwater and surface water environments. Emphasis is placed on both theoretical foundations and practical applications relevant to water resource management, environmental engineering, and sustainable infrastructure planning.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the fundamental principles of groundwater flow, including Darcy's Law, hydraulic conductivity, and aquifer storativity, and apply these concepts to real-world subsurface systems
- Interpret hydrogeological data to characterize aquifer properties, evaluate groundwater recharge and discharge zones, and assess the sustainability of groundwater resources
- Apply quantitative methods and analytical models to solve problems related to well hydraulics, pumping tests, and groundwater yield estimation
- Evaluate the vulnerability of groundwater systems to contamination and demonstrate understanding of transport mechanisms for dissolved constituents in porous media
- Communicate engineering assessments of groundwater systems through written technical reports and field data interpretation, demonstrating professional standards of practice

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Midterm Examination (20%)** – A closed-book written examination covering theoretical principles of groundwater occurrence, flow equations, and aquifer characterization covered in the first half of the course
- **Final Examination (25%)** – A comprehensive examination assessing cumulative knowledge of all course material, including quantitative problem-solving and conceptual application
- **Laboratory Assignments and Problem Sets (25%)** – Weekly or bi-weekly assignments requiring students to apply course concepts to numerical problems, data interpretation exercises, and software-based modeling tasks
- **Field Investigation Report (20%)** – A formal technical report produced in small groups following a supervised field exercise involving site characterization, water table measurement, and data collection at an approved field site
- **Research Paper (10%)** – An individually authored paper (1,500–2,500 words) examining a current issue in groundwater resource management, contamination, or policy, submitted in professional engineering report format
