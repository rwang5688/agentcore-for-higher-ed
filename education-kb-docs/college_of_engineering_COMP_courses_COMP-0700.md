### Course Description

This advanced graduate-level course examines the design, analysis, and implementation of algorithms tailored for processing, querying, and extracting insights from large-scale datasets that exceed the capacity of conventional computational approaches. Students will explore foundational and cutting-edge techniques including streaming algorithms, approximation methods, randomized algorithms, and distributed computing paradigms as applied to real-world big data challenges. Emphasis is placed on both theoretical rigor and practical application, preparing students to address scalability and efficiency demands in modern data-intensive environments.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the computational complexity and scalability characteristics of algorithms designed for massive datasets, applying appropriate formal frameworks such as the streaming model, the MapReduce model, and external memory models.
- Design and implement efficient approximation and randomized algorithms, including sketching and sampling techniques, that provide provable performance guarantees under resource constraints.
- Evaluate and apply distributed algorithmic frameworks to partition, process, and aggregate large-scale data across parallel and distributed computing architectures.
- Critically assess trade-offs between accuracy, time complexity, space complexity, and communication cost in the context of big data algorithm selection and design.
- Synthesize current research literature in big data algorithms to propose, develop, and present original solutions to open or applied computational problems in the field.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem Sets (4 assignments) | 25% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Research Paper Review & Critique | 15% |
| Semester Research Project | 30% |
| Class Participation & Presentations | 10% |

**Problem Sets:** Four structured problem sets distributed throughout the semester will assess students' ability to apply algorithmic techniques covered in lectures, including derivations, proofs of correctness, and complexity analyses.

**Midterm Examination:** A closed-book, in-class examination covering theoretical foundations, algorithm design principles, and analytical methods presented in the first half of the course.

**Research Paper Review & Critique:** Students will select a peer-reviewed publication from a provided list of recent conference proceedings or journals, produce a formal written critique (2,500–3,500 words), and deliver a brief oral summary to the class.

**Semester Research Project:** Working individually or in pairs, students will identify a significant big data algorithmic problem, conduct an original investigation (theoretical, empirical, or both), and submit a final written report (8,000–12,000 words) along with a formal presentation during the final examination period. A project proposal and intermediate progress report are required at designated checkpoints during the semester.

**Class Participation & Presentations:** Active, informed engagement in seminar discussions, peer critique sessions, and at least one formal lecture-style presentation of assigned reading material is expected and evaluated.
