### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous and comprehensive introduction to the foundational principles of computer science and software development, with a focus on applied programming using the Python language. Students will explore core computational thinking concepts including algorithmic design, data structures, control flow, and object-oriented programming paradigms within a structured and supportive learning environment. By the conclusion of the course, students will possess the theoretical grounding and practical programming proficiency necessary to pursue more advanced study in computer science, software engineering, or data-driven disciplines.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of COMP-9100, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a working understanding of fundamental computer science concepts, including computational thinking, algorithmic reasoning, and the organisation of modern computing systems.
- Write, test, and debug functional Python programs that employ variables, control structures, functions, and standard data types with confidence and clarity.
- Apply foundational principles of object-oriented programming, including the design and implementation of classes, inheritance, and encapsulation, to solve structured programming problems.
- Analyse and evaluate the efficiency and correctness of basic algorithms and data structures, including lists, dictionaries, and stacks, in the context of real-world problem scenarios.
- Develop collaborative and independent programming projects that adhere to professional standards of code documentation, readability, and version control practice.

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (Level 7935), students are expected to meet the following prerequisites prior to enrolment:

- Completion of a recognised undergraduate degree in a quantitative, scientific, or technical discipline, or equivalent professional experience as assessed by the Department of Computer Science.
- Demonstrated proficiency in formal logical reasoning and mathematical foundations, equivalent to undergraduate-level discrete mathematics or formal logic (MATH-4200 or equivalent).
- Prior exposure to any structured programming environment or scripting language is strongly recommended, though not strictly required. Students with no programming background are advised to complete the departmental self-assessment module available through the College of Engineering student portal before the commencement of classes.

Students who are uncertain whether they meet the prerequisites are encouraged to contact the Department of Computer Science directly to discuss their suitability for enrolment.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-9100 will be evaluated through a combination of formative and summative assessment activities designed to measure both theoretical understanding and applied programming competency.

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Programming Assignments (×4) | 40% | Four individual coding assignments of increasing complexity, submitted via the university's online coding platform. Assignments assess practical Python proficiency, code quality, and problem-solving approach. |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book written examination assessing theoretical knowledge of computer science fundamentals, algorithmic analysis, and Python syntax and semantics. |
| Collaborative Project | 25% | A team-based software development project completed in groups of three to four students. Teams will design, build, and present a Python application addressing a defined problem scenario. Assessment includes a project report, functional codebase, and an oral presentation to the instructional panel. |
| Final Examination | 15% | A comprehensive closed-book examination covering all course material, including practical code-reading and short-answer questions focused on algorithmic reasoning and object-oriented design. |

*A minimum passing grade of 50% across all components is required. Students must achieve at least 40% on both the Midterm and Final Examinations independently to be eligible for a passing course grade. Late submission penalties and academic integrity policies are governed by the Peculiar U Academic Regulations Handbook.*
