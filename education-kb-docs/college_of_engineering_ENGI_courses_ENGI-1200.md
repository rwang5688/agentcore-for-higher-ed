## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-1200 Convex Optimization 2 is an advanced graduate-level course that extends foundational convex optimization theory into sophisticated algorithmic design, duality theory, and large-scale computational methods. Students will engage with cutting-edge techniques including proximal gradient methods, interior-point methods, and distributed optimization frameworks, with particular emphasis on engineering applications such as signal processing, machine learning systems, and network flow problems. The course places strong demand on mathematical rigor and expects students to develop both theoretical fluency and practical implementation skills for solving complex real-world optimization problems.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Rigorously analyze and apply advanced duality theory, including Lagrangian relaxation, strong duality conditions, and KKT optimality conditions in complex constrained settings
- Design, implement, and critically evaluate large-scale optimization algorithms including proximal methods, augmented Lagrangian methods, and alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)
- Apply interior-point and barrier methods to semidefinite and second-order cone programming problems arising in engineering contexts
- Formulate and solve distributed and decentralized optimization problems relevant to networked engineering systems and multi-agent environments
- Demonstrate mastery of convergence analysis techniques and derive formal complexity bounds for a range of first- and second-order optimization algorithms

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **ENGI-1100: Convex Optimization 1** (or demonstrated equivalent knowledge approved by the course coordinator)
- **MATH-600: Advanced Linear Algebra and Matrix Analysis** or equivalent graduate-level linear algebra
- **MATH-610: Real Analysis II** or equivalent, with a passing grade of B or higher
- Proficiency in a scientific computing environment (Python, MATLAB, or Julia) as demonstrated through prior coursework or departmental assessment
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or written permission from a course instructor

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 total) | 30% | Bi-weekly theoretical and computational assignments |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Closed-book written examination covering duality, KKT conditions, and interior-point theory |
| Research Paper Review | 15% | Critical written analysis of a faculty-assigned peer-reviewed optimization paper |
| Computational Project | 20% | Individual or paired implementation of an optimization algorithm applied to an engineering dataset, with written report |
| Final Examination | 15% | Comprehensive closed-book examination |

**Note:** Problem sets will include both analytical proof-writing components and computational exercises. Late submissions will be penalized at 10% per day unless an extension is formally approved by the supervising instructor. Students are encouraged to consult with instructors Castillo, Cuentas, Greenbank, Kopman, Mai, Borth, Reamey, Deamaral, or Chenier during posted office hours.

### Required Materials

Students are expected to acquire the following materials before the first week of instruction:

- **Vandermeer, P. & Okafor, S. T.** (4th ed.). *Convex Analysis and Algorithmic Optimization: Theory and Engineering Applications.* Arclight Academic Press. *(Primary textbook)*
- **Ristova, N. L.** (2nd ed.). *Large-Scale Optimization Methods for Engineers.* Halford & Crane Scientific Publishing. *(Required supplementary text)*
- **Iglesias, D. R. & Tan, W.** (1st ed.). *Duality, Decomposition, and Distributed Systems: A Graduate Primer.* Osthaven University Press. *(Required supplementary text)*
- Course Reader: *ENGI-1200 Selected Topics and Proofs*, compiled by the instructional team and available for purchase through the Peculiar U Engineering Copy Centre or digitally via the university's course portal

All computational assignments will use open-source software. Installation guides and required package lists will be distributed during the first class session.
