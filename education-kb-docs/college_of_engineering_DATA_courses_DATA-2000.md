## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced course examines the foundational and applied design principles underlying effective data visualization through the Tableau platform, with emphasis on the relationship between visual encoding, human perception, and analytical communication. Students will develop expertise in translating complex datasets into coherent, audience-appropriate visual narratives using industry-standard design frameworks. The course integrates theoretical perspectives on information design with hands-on Tableau implementation, preparing students for high-level professional and research roles in data communication.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically evaluate and apply core visual design principles — including hierarchy, contrast, alignment, and color theory — within the Tableau environment to produce analytically rigorous and aesthetically effective dashboards
- Construct advanced interactive visualizations and calculated fields in Tableau that support exploratory and explanatory data analysis across diverse dataset types
- Synthesize established frameworks in information design (including Tufte's data-ink principles and pre-attentive attribute theory) to justify and defend visualization design decisions
- Assess audience needs, data literacy levels, and communication contexts to tailor visualization outputs appropriately for technical and non-technical stakeholders
- Identify and remediate common visualization anti-patterns — including chart junk, misleading scales, and inappropriate chart selection — through systematic critique and iterative redesign

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **DATA-5100: Foundations of Data Visualization** or equivalent, with a minimum grade of B
- **DATA-4800: Introduction to Business Intelligence Tools** or equivalent demonstrated proficiency
- **STAT-4200: Applied Statistics for Data Science** or equivalent
- Demonstrated intermediate proficiency in Tableau Desktop (verified through departmental placement assessment or completion of an approved Tableau training certification)
- Familiarity with relational data structures and basic SQL querying is strongly recommended

Students who are uncertain whether their background meets these requirements are encouraged to consult with the course coordinators before registering.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Design Critique Portfolio | 20% |
| Midterm Examination (written + applied) | 20% |
| Dashboard Redesign Project | 25% |
| Capstone Visualization Project & Presentation | 30% |
| Participation & Peer Review Contributions | 5% |

**Design Critique Portfolio:** Students will submit a cumulative portfolio of six structured critiques analyzing real-world visualizations against course design frameworks. Submissions are due at designated intervals throughout the semester.

**Midterm Examination:** A two-part examination consisting of a written theory component assessing knowledge of design principles and a practical applied component requiring students to construct and annotate a visualization in Tableau under timed conditions.

**Dashboard Redesign Project:** Working individually, students will select a publicly available dataset and an existing visualization artifact, identify its design deficiencies, and produce a fully redesigned Tableau dashboard with an accompanying written rationale (2,000–2,500 words).

**Capstone Visualization Project & Presentation:** Students will undertake an original end-to-end visualization project — from data acquisition and cleaning through final dashboard delivery — addressing a defined analytical question. A 15-minute formal presentation to a panel including course instructors is required.

**Participation & Peer Review:** Active engagement in seminar discussions and completion of structured peer feedback exercises for fellow students' submitted work.
