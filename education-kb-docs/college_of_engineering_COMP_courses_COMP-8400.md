## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-8400: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence provides a comprehensive foundation in the theoretical principles and practical applications of artificial intelligence, covering core topics such as search algorithms, knowledge representation, machine learning, natural language processing, and intelligent agent design. Students will engage with both classical AI methodologies and contemporary approaches, gaining an understanding of how intelligent systems are designed, evaluated, and deployed in real-world environments. This course serves as the gateway to advanced study in AI and related disciplines within the Computer Science curriculum.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain and apply fundamental AI concepts, including search strategies, heuristics, and constraint satisfaction problems, to solve computational challenges
- Design and implement basic intelligent agents capable of perceiving their environment and making goal-directed decisions
- Evaluate and compare machine learning approaches, including supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning paradigms, and apply them to appropriate problem domains
- Analyse the ethical, societal, and professional implications of artificial intelligence systems, including considerations of bias, fairness, transparency, and accountability
- Communicate technical AI concepts and project outcomes clearly through written reports and structured presentations appropriate to a professional engineering context

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolment in COMP-8400:

- **COMP-6100: Data Structures and Algorithms** – A solid understanding of algorithmic complexity, graph structures, and recursive problem-solving is essential for engaging with AI search methods and optimization techniques
- **COMP-6210: Discrete Mathematics for Computing** – Proficiency in logic, probability, and set theory underpins the formal reasoning frameworks used throughout this course
- **COMP-6350: Programming Fundamentals II** – Students must demonstrate competency in at least one high-level programming language (Python strongly recommended) and be comfortable with object-oriented design principles
- **MATH-6120: Linear Algebra and Calculus for Engineers** – A working knowledge of matrix operations, differentiation, and vector spaces is required for the machine learning components of the course

Students who have not completed the listed prerequisites but believe they possess equivalent preparation may apply for a prerequisite waiver through the Department of Computer Science Undergraduate and Postgraduate Advising Office.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-8400 will be evaluated through the following assessment components:

| Assessment | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Programming Assignments (×3) | 35% | Three individual coding assignments distributed across the semester, covering search algorithms, supervised learning, and agent design respectively. Submitted via the university's online portal with accompanying written documentation. |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book written examination assessing comprehension of theoretical concepts covered in Weeks 1–7, including search strategies, knowledge representation, and introductory machine learning. |
| Group Research Project | 25% | Teams of 3–4 students will design, implement, and evaluate an AI system addressing a real-world problem of their choosing, subject to instructor approval. A written technical report (minimum 4,000 words) and a formal in-class presentation are required. |
| Final Examination | 20% | A comprehensive closed-book examination covering all course material. Students must achieve a minimum score of 50% on the final examination to pass the course, regardless of performance on other assessments. |

All assessments must be submitted or sat in accordance with the deadlines published in the course schedule. Late submission penalties and academic integrity policies are governed by the Peculiar University Student Assessment Regulations, available through the Office of Academic Affairs.
