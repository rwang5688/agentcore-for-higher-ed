## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the design, fabrication, and analysis of Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS), exploring the fundamental principles governing device behavior at the microscale. Students will investigate the interplay between mechanical, electrical, thermal, and fluidic phenomena as they apply to the design of miniaturized sensors, actuators, and integrated systems. Emphasis is placed on bridging theoretical foundations with practical design methodologies, preparing students for advanced research and professional work in the rapidly evolving field of microsystems engineering.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of the physical principles governing MEMS device behavior, including scaling laws, surface-to-volume effects, and coupled-domain phenomena at the microscale.
- Apply established microfabrication techniques — including photolithography, thin-film deposition, etching, and wafer bonding — to the conceptual and practical design of MEMS structures.
- Utilize analytical and computational modeling tools to simulate and predict the mechanical, electrical, and thermal performance of MEMS components and integrated systems.
- Design and evaluate MEMS-based sensors and actuators for target applications, applying design-for-manufacturability principles and performance trade-off analysis.
- Critically interpret and communicate technical findings related to MEMS device design through written reports and formal presentations consistent with professional engineering standards.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-5600 are expected to have completed the following or equivalent coursework:

- **ENGI-3210: Mechanics of Materials** — foundational understanding of stress, strain, elastic behavior, and structural mechanics required for microscale beam and membrane analysis.
- **ENGI-3340: Introduction to Semiconductor Devices** — familiarity with semiconductor physics, p-n junctions, and basic electronic behavior in solid-state systems.
- **ENGI-3450: Fluid and Thermal Systems** — knowledge of heat transfer principles and fundamental fluid mechanics applicable to microfluidic and thermally actuated MEMS components.
- **MATH-3120: Differential Equations and Linear Algebra** — mathematical proficiency necessary for modeling dynamic MEMS systems and performing eigenvalue-based structural analyses.

Students lacking one or more prerequisites may seek instructor approval for enrollment and are advised to consult the course coordinator before registering.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through a combination of individual assessments and collaborative design work, as outlined below:

- **Midterm Examination (20%)** — A written examination covering fundamental MEMS physics, scaling laws, material properties at the microscale, and core microfabrication processes.
- **Final Examination (25%)** — A comprehensive written examination encompassing all course material, with emphasis on design analysis, device modeling, and coupled-domain problem-solving.
- **Design Project (30%)** — A semester-long team-based project in which student groups conceptualize, model, and present a complete MEMS device design for a specified application domain. Deliverables include a preliminary design report, a computational simulation package, and a formal oral presentation evaluated by the instructional team.
- **Laboratory Reports (15%)** — Written reports submitted following each designated laboratory or simulation session, documenting experimental or computational procedures, results, and critical analysis.
- **Problem Sets and Participation (10%)** — Regularly assigned problem sets reinforcing lecture content, alongside assessed participation in design critiques and in-class discussions.
