## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-1300: Artificial Intelligence provides an advanced exploration of the theoretical foundations and practical methodologies underlying intelligent computational systems. Students examine core AI paradigms including search algorithms, knowledge representation, machine learning, probabilistic reasoning, and natural language processing, with an emphasis on designing and implementing intelligent agents capable of solving complex, real-world problems. The course bridges foundational computer science principles with cutting-edge research, preparing students for both applied and scholarly work in the field.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and implement classical and modern AI search strategies, including heuristic, adversarial, and constraint-based approaches, evaluating their computational efficiency and applicability across problem domains
- Design and construct knowledge representation systems using logic-based and probabilistic frameworks, demonstrating an understanding of reasoning under uncertainty
- Apply supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning techniques to real datasets, critically assessing model performance, generalization, and ethical implications
- Evaluate the theoretical underpinnings of neural network architectures, including deep learning models, and implement functional prototypes for tasks such as classification, regression, and sequence modeling
- Critically engage with current literature in artificial intelligence, synthesizing research findings to identify open problems, emerging methodologies, and the broader societal impact of AI systems

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (Level 850), students are expected to have substantial prior knowledge in relevant areas. The following prerequisites are required unless a formal waiver is granted by the supervising instructor or department advisor:

- Completion of a graduate-level course in Machine Learning or Computational Intelligence (or equivalent demonstrated competency)
- Proficiency in at least one high-level programming language suitable for AI development (Python strongly recommended; demonstrated experience with libraries such as NumPy, TensorFlow, or PyTorch is expected)
- Graduate-level coursework in Linear Algebra and Probability & Statistics, or equivalent preparation
- Familiarity with data structures, algorithms, and algorithm complexity analysis at the advanced undergraduate or graduate level
- Students without formal prerequisites but with significant professional or research experience in AI or related fields should consult the course coordinator prior to enrollment

### Assessment Methods

Assessment in COMP-1300 is designed to evaluate both theoretical mastery and practical competency across a range of formats:

- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written examination assessing students' understanding of foundational AI concepts, search algorithms, logic-based reasoning, and probabilistic methods. The examination includes both analytical problem-solving questions and short-essay components requiring students to compare and critique methodological approaches.
- **Research Paper (25%):** An individually authored research paper of 6,000–8,000 words in which students conduct a critical survey of a specific subdomain within artificial intelligence (e.g., explainable AI, multi-agent systems, adversarial machine learning). Papers must engage with a minimum of fifteen peer-reviewed sources and demonstrate original synthesis and scholarly argumentation.
- **Practical Implementation Projects (30%):** Two graded programming projects completed across the semester. The first project requires students to implement and evaluate a classical AI algorithm (such as A* search, minimax with alpha-beta pruning, or a Bayesian network) on a defined problem set. The second project involves the design, training, evaluation, and documented analysis of a machine learning or deep learning model applied to a real-world dataset of the student's choosing, subject to instructor approval.
- **Seminar Participation and Presentation (15%):** Students are expected to engage substantively in weekly seminar discussions and to deliver one formal oral presentation (20–25 minutes) summarizing and critiquing a significant recent publication in the AI literature. Presentations will be evaluated on depth of understanding, clarity of communication, and quality of engagement with peer questions.
- **Final Examination (10%):** A comprehensive final examination covering the full breadth of course content, with emphasis on integrating knowledge across topics and applying AI concepts to novel problem scenarios.

*All assessments must be submitted in accordance with Peculiar University's Academic Integrity Policy. Use of AI-assisted writing tools must be disclosed and used only in accordance with guidelines published by the instructor at the start of the term.*
