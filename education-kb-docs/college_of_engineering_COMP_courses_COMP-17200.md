This course introduces students to the foundational theories of mind as explored through the lens of computer science, examining how complex cognitive behaviour can emerge from the interaction of simpler computational processes. Drawing on Marvin Minsky's seminal framework, students will explore how intelligence — both human and artificial — may be understood as the coordinated activity of many interacting agents or modules. The course bridges classical cognitive science, introductory artificial intelligence, and computational thinking, offering students an interdisciplinary foundation for understanding the nature of mind and intelligent systems.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the "society of mind" model and articulate how distributed, modular processes can give rise to complex intelligent behaviour in both biological and artificial systems
- Identify and describe key computational concepts — including agents, representations, and emergent behaviour — as they apply to models of cognition
- Compare and contrast classical symbolic approaches to artificial intelligence with connectionist and agent-based models of mind
- Apply basic computational thinking skills to analyse simple problems in perception, memory, and reasoning from a cognitive science perspective
- Evaluate the philosophical and ethical implications of modelling the human mind as a computational system

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-17200 must have completed or be concurrently enrolled in the following:

- **COMP-11000 Introduction to Computer Science** or equivalent foundational programming experience
- **MATH-10100 Discrete Mathematics I** or equivalent
- No prior coursework in psychology or cognitive science is required, though **PSYC-10100 Introduction to Psychology** is strongly recommended as a co-requisite

Students who are uncertain whether their background is sufficient are encouraged to consult with their assigned instructor prior to enrolment.
