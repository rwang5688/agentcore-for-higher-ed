### Course Description

This introductory course provides students with a broad and accessible foundation in the principles and practices of data science, requiring no prior technical background. Students will explore the data science lifecycle — from data collection and cleaning through analysis, visualization, and interpretation — using real-world datasets and beginner-friendly tools. By the end of the course, students will have gained the conceptual vocabulary and practical confidence to pursue further study in data science, statistics, or related computing disciplines.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of DATA-0100, students will be able to:

- Describe the fundamental stages of the data science pipeline, including data acquisition, preprocessing, analysis, and communication of results.
- Identify and apply basic statistical concepts — such as measures of central tendency, variability, and correlation — to interpret simple datasets.
- Produce clear and informative data visualizations using introductory tools and explain the design choices that make visualizations effective or misleading.
- Demonstrate an awareness of ethical considerations in data science, including issues of data privacy, algorithmic bias, and the responsible use of data in decision-making.
- Articulate the role of data science across a variety of industries and disciplines, recognizing its relevance to real-world problems and careers.

### Prerequisites

DATA-0100 is a 100-level introductory course with no formal prerequisites. It is designed to be accessible to students from all majors and backgrounds. A basic comfort with high school-level mathematics (arithmetic, simple algebra, and graphical interpretation) is assumed. Students who have already completed DATA-0200 or any 200-level statistics course may not receive credit for this course.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly Reflection Quizzes (10 × 3%) | 30% |
| Lab Assignments (4 practical exercises) | 25% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Data Story Project | 25% |

**Weekly Reflection Quizzes:** Short, low-stakes quizzes administered at the end of each instructional week to reinforce key concepts and vocabulary from lectures and readings.

**Lab Assignments:** Four structured, hands-on exercises completed using course-provided tools, guiding students through real dataset exploration, basic statistical analysis, and visualization tasks. Labs are designed to be completed during scheduled lab sessions with instructor support available.

**Midterm Examination:** A written examination covering foundational concepts from the first half of the course, including the data science lifecycle, statistical fundamentals, and data ethics. The exam will include multiple-choice, short-answer, and diagram-based questions.

**Final Data Story Project:** Working individually or in pairs, students will select a publicly available dataset, conduct a guided exploratory analysis, produce a set of visualizations, and present their findings in a written "data story" narrative (approximately 1,000–1,500 words). The project is intended to demonstrate the student's ability to connect technical work to meaningful, communicable conclusions. A brief in-class presentation may be required at the instructor's discretion.
