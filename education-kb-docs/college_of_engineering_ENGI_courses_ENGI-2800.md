## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-2800: Engineering Ethics provides a rigorous examination of the moral responsibilities and professional obligations inherent to engineering practice across all disciplines. Students will explore ethical frameworks as they apply to real-world engineering decisions, including considerations of public safety, environmental impact, professional integrity, and societal consequence. Drawing on landmark case studies and contemporary challenges, this course equips future engineers with the critical thinking tools necessary to navigate complex ethical dilemmas throughout their careers.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply foundational ethical theories — including utilitarianism, deontology, and virtue ethics — to engineering scenarios involving competing professional and societal interests
- Analyse historical and contemporary engineering failures and disasters to identify systemic ethical breakdowns and articulate lessons for professional practice
- Evaluate the responsibilities of engineers to diverse stakeholders, including clients, employers, the public, and the natural environment
- Demonstrate understanding of professional codes of conduct as established by recognised engineering bodies and assess their application in ambiguous professional situations
- Construct well-reasoned written and verbal arguments defending ethical positions on contested issues in engineering, technology, and infrastructure development

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed a minimum of 30 credit points at the post-secondary level, including **ENGI-1100: Introduction to Engineering Practice** or an equivalent foundational engineering course. Completion of or concurrent enrolment in **ENGI-1500: Engineering in Society** is strongly recommended. No prior coursework in philosophy or ethics is required, though students with such a background will find it beneficial.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Participation & In-Class Discussion | 10% |
| Case Study Response Papers (×3) | 30% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Group Ethical Analysis Project | 20% |
| Final Written Position Paper | 20% |

**Case Study Response Papers** require students to analyse assigned engineering incidents or dilemmas using ethical frameworks discussed in lectures, with each paper ranging between 800 and 1,200 words.

**Group Ethical Analysis Project** tasks small teams with researching a contemporary engineering ethics issue of their choosing, culminating in a formal written report and a 20-minute in-class presentation.

**Final Written Position Paper** is an independently researched, 2,500–3,000 word argument in which students defend a clear ethical stance on a significant issue in engineering practice, demonstrating engagement with course theory and relevant literature.
