## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-3100 Environmental Air Pollution provides a rigorous examination of the sources, transport mechanisms, and chemical transformations of pollutants in the atmospheric environment, with an emphasis on engineering principles and mitigation strategies. Students will investigate both natural and anthropogenic contributions to air quality degradation, including particulate matter, gaseous emissions, and photochemical smog formation. The course integrates regulatory frameworks, monitoring technologies, and control system design to equip students with the technical foundation necessary for professional practice in environmental engineering.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and classify major categories of air pollutants, their primary and secondary sources, and their roles in atmospheric chemistry and human health impacts
- Apply fluid mechanics and mass transfer principles to model the dispersion and transport of pollutants in various atmospheric conditions
- Evaluate and design engineering control technologies, including scrubbers, electrostatic precipitators, and catalytic converters, for industrial emission reduction
- Interpret and apply national and international air quality standards and regulatory guidelines, including emissions permitting and compliance monitoring protocols
- Analyse air quality data sets using quantitative methods to assess pollution levels, identify trends, and communicate findings in professionally appropriate formats

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **ENGI-2210** Fluid Mechanics (minimum grade: C)
- **CHEM-2105** Environmental Chemistry or **CHEM-2010** General Chemistry II (minimum grade: C)

Students who have not completed the stated prerequisites but believe they possess equivalent preparation should consult with the course instructor or their academic advisor to request a prerequisite waiver prior to the registration deadline.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Reports (4 submissions) | 20% |
| Dispersion Modelling Project | 20% |
| Regulatory Compliance Case Study | 10% |
| Weekly Problem Sets | 5% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** are closed-book assessments covering theoretical concepts, quantitative problem-solving, and the application of engineering principles to air pollution scenarios. The final examination is comprehensive.

**Laboratory Reports** correspond to four scheduled laboratory sessions in which students conduct emissions measurements, atmospheric sampling, and data analysis using industry-standard instrumentation. Each report must follow the departmental technical writing format.

**Dispersion Modelling Project** requires students, working in pairs, to select a real-world industrial facility and develop a Gaussian plume dispersion model to predict pollutant concentrations under varying meteorological conditions. A written technical report and an oral presentation are required.

**Regulatory Compliance Case Study** is an individual written assignment in which students evaluate a documented air pollution incident or enforcement action, analysing the technical, legal, and public health dimensions of the case.

**Weekly Problem Sets** are submitted at the beginning of each Thursday lecture session and assess students' ongoing understanding of course material.

### Required Materials

Students are expected to obtain the following materials before the first week of instruction:

- **Okonkwo, P.A., Veltri, S., & Marchetti, J.** (2021). *Air Quality Regulation and Compliance: An Engineering Practitioner's Guide*. Thornfield Publishing Group. — Used extensively in the regulatory compliance unit and for the case study assignment.
- **Bastian, E.C.** (2017). *Laboratory Methods in Environmental Monitoring: Air and Atmospheric Systems* (2nd ed.). Kessler Scientific Texts. — Required for all laboratory sessions; students must bring a personal copy to each lab.
- Access to **AtmoSim Pro** dispersion modelling software is required for the modelling project. A student licence is available at a subsidised rate through the College of Engineering Computing Services portal; full instructions will be distributed during the second week of class.
- Course readings, data sets, and supplementary technical standards will be made available through the Peculiar U course management system throughout the semester.
