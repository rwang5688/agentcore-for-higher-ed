This course provides a rigorous examination of the principles and methodologies underpinning the analysis and design of control systems in engineering applications, with emphasis on both continuous-time and discrete-time systems. Students will engage with classical and modern control theory, exploring feedback mechanisms, system stability, and controller design techniques applicable to real-world engineering challenges. Through integrated laboratory exercises and design projects, learners develop the practical competence to model, simulate, and optimize dynamic systems across a variety of engineering domains.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Derive and interpret mathematical models of dynamic systems using transfer functions, state-space representations, and block diagram algebra to characterize system behaviour under varying input conditions.
- Apply classical control design techniques — including PID control, root locus analysis, and frequency-domain methods such as Bode and Nyquist plots — to achieve specified performance criteria in closed-loop systems.
- Analyse system stability using established criteria including the Routh-Hurwitz criterion, gain and phase margin analysis, and Lyapunov-based methods for both linear and nonlinear systems.
- Design and evaluate discrete-time control systems, incorporating z-transform techniques and digital controller implementation strategies appropriate for sampled-data environments.
- Utilize industry-standard simulation software to model, test, and iteratively refine control system designs, demonstrating professional-level competency in virtual prototyping and performance validation.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Closed-book written examination covering theoretical foundations and analytical methods from Weeks 1–7 |
| Final Examination | 30% | Comprehensive closed-book written examination; students permitted one double-sided formula sheet |
| Laboratory Reports (×4) | 20% | Individual written reports submitted following each scheduled laboratory session; assessed on accuracy, analysis quality, and technical communication |
| Control System Design Project | 20% | Team-based design project requiring full system modelling, controller design, simulation, and a formal written report with oral presentation component |
| Weekly Problem Sets (×10) | 10% | Individual homework assignments reinforcing lecture content; lowest one score will be dropped from final calculation |

A minimum overall grade of 55% is required to pass this course. Students must also achieve a minimum aggregate score of 50% across examination components to be eligible for a passing grade, regardless of performance in other assessments.
