### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous yet accessible introduction to the fundamental concepts and techniques of probability theory, with an emphasis on applications relevant to data science and statistical reasoning. Students will explore classical and modern approaches to uncertainty, random variables, and probabilistic modelling, building a strong mathematical foundation for subsequent coursework in statistics and machine learning. Special attention is given to developing students' ability to construct, interpret, and critically evaluate probabilistic arguments in real-world analytical contexts.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of DATA-2700, students will be able to:

- Define and apply core probability axioms, including sample spaces, events, and probability measures, to solve both discrete and continuous problems
- Identify and work with common probability distributions, such as the binomial, Poisson, uniform, and normal distributions, recognising their appropriate applications
- Calculate and interpret expected values, variance, and covariance, and apply these concepts to analyse the behaviour of random variables
- Apply conditional probability and Bayes' Theorem to update beliefs and make inferences under uncertainty
- Construct basic probabilistic models to represent and analyse real-world phenomena, demonstrating sound mathematical reasoning and clear written interpretation of results

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in DATA-2700 must have successfully completed the following, or equivalent courses as approved by the Department of Data Science:

- **MATH-1100: Calculus I** — A solid grounding in differential and integral calculus is required, as probability theory draws extensively on these techniques in the treatment of continuous distributions
- **MATH-1200: Discrete Mathematics** — Familiarity with set theory, combinatorics, and logical reasoning is essential for understanding foundational probability concepts
- **DATA-1050: Introduction to Data Science** *(recommended but not required)* — Students with no prior exposure to data science contexts are encouraged to complete this course concurrently or in advance

Students who are uncertain about their preparedness are encouraged to consult with their academic advisor or contact the Department of Data Science directly prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in DATA-2700 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Sets (×6) | 30% | Six graded problem sets distributed throughout the semester, covering weekly lecture material and requiring written mathematical solutions |
| Midterm Examination | 25% | A closed-book, in-person examination held in Week 8, assessing cumulative understanding of probability axioms, combinatorics, and discrete distributions |
| Applied Modelling Project | 20% | An individual written project in which students construct and analyse a probabilistic model applied to a dataset or real-world scenario of their choosing, submitted in Week 13 |
| Final Examination | 25% | A comprehensive closed-book examination held during the official examination period, covering all course content with emphasis on continuous distributions, conditional probability, and Bayes' Theorem |

*Note: A minimum grade of 50% on the Final Examination is required to pass the course, regardless of cumulative standing. Students must also submit all six problem sets to be eligible for a passing grade.*
