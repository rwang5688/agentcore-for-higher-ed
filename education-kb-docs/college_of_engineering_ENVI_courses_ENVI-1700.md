## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the physical, chemical, and biological processes that govern Earth's climate system, including atmospheric dynamics, ocean-atmosphere interactions, and the carbon cycle. Students will examine how natural variability and anthropogenic forcing shape regional and global climate patterns across multiple timescales. Emphasis is placed on developing a scientific literacy that enables students to critically evaluate climate data, models, and projections.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the fundamental mechanisms driving Earth's energy balance, including radiative forcing, the greenhouse effect, and feedback loops within the climate system
- Analyze the roles of the atmosphere, hydrosphere, cryosphere, and biosphere as interconnected components of global climate regulation
- Interpret climate data sets and proxy records to distinguish natural climate variability from human-induced change
- Apply conceptual and quantitative frameworks to evaluate the reliability and uncertainty of climate model outputs
- Communicate evidence-based arguments about climate processes and their societal implications to both technical and non-technical audiences

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-1700 should have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency:

- **ENVI-1100: Introduction to Earth and Environmental Systems** or equivalent introductory earth science course
- **MATH-1210: Calculus I** or **MATH-1150: Pre-Calculus and Quantitative Reasoning**
- **PHYS-1020: Fundamentals of Physics I** (may be taken concurrently with instructor approval)

Students without formal prerequisites who possess significant professional or field experience in environmental sciences may petition the Department of Environment and Natural Resources for a prerequisite waiver prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Climate Data Analysis Projects (×2) | 25% |
| Research Paper | 20% |
| Weekly Response Posts & Participation | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess students' understanding of core concepts through a combination of multiple-choice, short-answer, and quantitative problem-solving questions. The final examination is cumulative and will include an extended essay component requiring students to synthesize material from across the course.

**Climate Data Analysis Projects** are completed in pairs and require students to work with publicly available datasets (e.g., NOAA, NASA GISS) to identify trends, anomalies, and feedback mechanisms. Written reports of approximately 1,500–2,000 words are submitted alongside annotated figures.

**Research Paper** is an individual written assignment of 2,500–3,500 words in which students investigate a specific climate process or regional climate phenomenon of their choosing. Papers must engage with peer-reviewed literature and demonstrate the ability to synthesize and critically evaluate scientific evidence. A proposal and annotated bibliography are submitted in Week 6, with the final paper due in Week 13.

**Weekly Response Posts & Participation** involve brief written reflections posted to the course learning management system in response to assigned readings or current climate science news, as well as meaningful engagement in seminar discussion.
