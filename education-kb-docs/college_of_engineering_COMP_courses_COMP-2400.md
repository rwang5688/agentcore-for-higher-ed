### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous and comprehensive introduction to the theory and practice of digital information as it underpins modern computing systems. Students will explore how data is represented, encoded, transmitted, and stored at the binary level, examining the mathematical and logical principles that govern the behavior of digital systems. Topics include number systems, data encoding schemes, error detection and correction, data compression, and the foundational role of information theory in computer science.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of binary, hexadecimal, and other positional number systems, and perform conversions and arithmetic operations across these systems.
- Explain and apply fundamental principles of information theory, including entropy, redundancy, and the quantification of information, as introduced by Shannon's model of communication.
- Analyze and implement common data encoding techniques for text, audio, images, and video, evaluating trade-offs in efficiency and fidelity.
- Apply error detection and correction algorithms — including parity bits, checksums, and Hamming codes — to identify and resolve data integrity issues in digital transmission.
- Design and evaluate basic lossless and lossy data compression strategies, understanding their theoretical limits and practical applications in modern computing contexts.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in this course will be evaluated through a combination of the following assessment components:

| Assessment | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 total, bi-weekly) | 25% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Laboratory Assignments (3 total) | 20% |
| Final Project | 20% |
| Final Examination | 15% |

**Problem Sets** are assigned on a bi-weekly basis and cover the theoretical and computational material introduced in lectures. Students are expected to show full working and justify their reasoning.

**Laboratory Assignments** are hands-on programming exercises in which students implement encoding, compression, and error-correction algorithms in a language of the instructor's choice (typically Python or C). Detailed specifications are distributed at the start of each laboratory cycle.

**Final Project** requires students to design and document a small-scale digital information system — such as a custom encoding scheme or a functional compression tool — culminating in both a written technical report and a short in-class presentation. Project topics must be approved by the supervising instructor no later than Week 8.

**Examinations** (Midterm and Final) are closed-book and will include a combination of multiple-choice questions, short-answer problems, and multi-step computational exercises. A formula reference sheet will be provided.
