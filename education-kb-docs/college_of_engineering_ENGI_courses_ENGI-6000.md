## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6000: Nano-to-Macro Transport Processes provides a rigorous, multiscale examination of heat, mass, and momentum transport phenomena spanning molecular, mesoscopic, and continuum length scales. Students will investigate the fundamental physical mechanisms governing transport at the nanoscale and develop the analytical and computational tools necessary to bridge these behaviors to macroscopic engineering systems. Emphasis is placed on the practical application of multiscale modeling frameworks to contemporary problems in nanotechnology, advanced materials, and energy systems engineering.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Articulate and apply the governing equations of transport phenomena across nanoscale, mesoscale, and macroscale regimes, identifying the assumptions and limitations inherent to each framework
- Analyze and model molecular-level transport mechanisms—including phonon transport, Brownian motion, and molecular diffusion—and relate these behaviors to observable macroscopic properties
- Employ multiscale modeling techniques, including molecular dynamics, Boltzmann transport equations, and continuum-based methods, to simulate and predict transport behavior in complex engineering systems
- Critically evaluate the breakdown of classical continuum assumptions at reduced length scales and apply appropriate corrective models such as slip-flow boundary conditions and non-Fourier heat conduction formulations
- Design and assess engineering solutions to real-world transport problems in domains such as nanofluidics, thermal management of microelectronics, and nanomaterial-enhanced energy systems

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrollment in ENGI-6000:

- **ENGI-3210: Fluid Mechanics** or equivalent
- **ENGI-3450: Heat and Mass Transfer**
- **ENGI-3820: Thermodynamics II**
- **MATH-3310: Partial Differential Equations and Applied Mathematics**
- Graduate standing or senior undergraduate status with a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.0, or instructor permission

Students are strongly encouraged to have foundational exposure to numerical methods and introductory materials science prior to enrollment. Programming experience in MATLAB, Python, or an equivalent computational environment is expected.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 25% |
| Multiscale Modeling Project | 20% |
| Research Paper & Presentation | 10% |

**Problem Sets** are assigned biweekly and require both analytical derivation and computational simulation. Collaboration is permitted; however, all submitted work must reflect individual understanding and be written independently.

**Multiscale Modeling Project** requires students to select an engineering system of current research relevance—such as a nanostructured heat exchanger, a drug-delivery nanoparticle system, or a next-generation semiconductor device—and develop a multiscale transport model using approved simulation tools. Projects are submitted in stages, including a proposal, a progress report, and a final technical report.

**Research Paper & Presentation** tasks students with reviewing and critically synthesizing a minimum of eight peer-reviewed sources on a selected topic in nano-to-macro transport, culminating in a written report (minimum 3,500 words) and a formal 15-minute oral presentation delivered to the class.

**Examinations** are closed-book but permit one double-sided formula reference sheet prepared by the student. Both examinations include a combination of derivation problems, quantitative analysis, and short conceptual responses.
