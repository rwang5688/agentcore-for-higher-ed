## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-7600: Higher Computing provides an advanced exploration of computational theory, systems design, and emerging paradigms in modern computing, with emphasis on the mathematical foundations and practical applications underpinning high-performance and distributed systems. Students engage critically with cutting-edge research in areas such as algorithmic complexity, parallel computation, and intelligent system architectures. This course is designed to cultivate rigorous analytical thinking and equip students with the advanced technical competencies required for research, industry leadership, and doctoral-level study.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of advanced computational complexity theory, including NP-completeness, approximation algorithms, and tractability boundaries
- Design, evaluate, and optimise algorithms for high-performance and distributed computing environments
- Critically analyse and apply formal models of computation, including automata theory, lambda calculus, and type-theoretic frameworks
- Synthesise contemporary research literature to identify open problems and propose informed solutions within a chosen area of higher computing
- Apply principles of fault tolerance, concurrency, and scalability in the design of complex computing systems

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-7600 must have successfully completed the following, or demonstrate equivalent competency approved by the department:

- **COMP-5210: Advanced Algorithms and Data Structures** (minimum grade: B)
- **COMP-5340: Operating Systems and Distributed Computing**
- **COMP-5480: Theory of Computation**
- **MATH-4120: Discrete Mathematics and Logic** or equivalent mathematical foundations coursework
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or written departmental approval

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written examination covering computational theory and algorithmic design principles |
| Final Examination | 25% | Comprehensive written examination spanning all course topics |
| Research Paper | 30% | An original 6,000–8,000-word scholarly paper on a faculty-approved topic in advanced computing, submitted in two stages (draft and final) |
| Systems Design Project | 20% | A collaborative or individual technical project requiring the design and implementation of a high-performance or distributed computing solution, with accompanying documentation and oral defence |
| Seminar Participation | 5% | Active, informed engagement in weekly research seminars and peer critique sessions |

Late submissions will be penalised at 5% per calendar day unless an extension has been formally approved by the supervising instructor. Academic integrity policies of Peculiar University apply in full to all assessed work.
