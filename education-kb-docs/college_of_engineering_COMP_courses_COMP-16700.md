---

This advanced graduate-level course provides an in-depth examination of the Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol (TCP/IP) suite, exploring the architectural principles, operational mechanics, and performance characteristics that underpin modern networked systems. Students will engage critically with protocol design at each layer of the TCP/IP stack, with particular emphasis on transport and network layer behavior, congestion control mechanisms, and emerging adaptations of the protocol suite for contemporary and high-demand networking environments. The course bridges theoretical foundations with hands-on analysis, equipping students to evaluate, troubleshoot, and design solutions for complex real-world networking challenges.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze the full TCP/IP protocol stack in detail, articulating the role, responsibilities, and interdependencies of each layer from the link layer through the application layer
- Evaluate TCP congestion control algorithms — including Tahoe, Reno, CUBIC, and BBR — and assess their performance trade-offs in varied network conditions including high-latency, high-bandwidth, and wireless environments
- Design and implement network configurations and diagnostic procedures using industry-standard tools such as Wireshark, tcpdump, and netstat to capture, interpret, and troubleshoot live packet data
- Critically compare TCP/IP with alternative and successor protocol frameworks, including QUIC and UDP-based transport solutions, assessing their suitability for latency-sensitive and large-scale distributed applications
- Synthesize knowledge of IP addressing, subnetting, routing protocols (OSPF, BGP), and NAT traversal to architect and evaluate scalable, efficient, and secure network topologies

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Protocol Analysis Assignments (×4) | 20% |
| Network Design and Implementation Project | 25% |
| Research Paper and Presentation | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Written examinations assess theoretical understanding of TCP/IP architecture, protocol behavior, addressing schemes, and congestion control. Questions include short-answer, problem-solving, and case-based analysis formats.

**Protocol Analysis Assignments:** Four structured laboratory assignments require students to capture and interpret network traffic using packet analysis tools, identify protocol anomalies, and produce detailed technical reports documenting their findings and methodology.

**Network Design and Implementation Project:** Completed in groups of two to three, this capstone project requires students to design, simulate, and document a scalable network topology addressing a provided real-world scenario. Deliverables include a technical specification document, a working simulation, and a recorded demonstration.

**Research Paper and Presentation:** An individual research paper (3,500–5,000 words) on an approved topic related to advanced TCP/IP concepts, protocol evolution, or emerging transport technologies, accompanied by a 15-minute in-class presentation and peer Q&A session.
