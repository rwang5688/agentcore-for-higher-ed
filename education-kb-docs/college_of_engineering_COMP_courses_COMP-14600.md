## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-14600 provides computer science students with a rigorous foundation in probability theory and its direct applications to computational systems, algorithms, and data analysis. Students will explore core probabilistic concepts including random variables, distributions, expectation, and stochastic processes, with an emphasis on practical implementation and problem-solving within computer science contexts. The course bridges theoretical mathematical reasoning with applied computational thinking, preparing students for advanced study in areas such as machine learning, cryptography, algorithm analysis, and systems design.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Define and apply fundamental concepts of probability theory, including sample spaces, events, conditional probability, and independence, within computational problem-solving contexts
- Analyze discrete and continuous probability distributions and select appropriate models for real-world computational scenarios
- Compute and interpret expected values, variance, and higher moments to evaluate the behavior and performance of randomized algorithms
- Apply probabilistic reasoning to algorithm analysis, including average-case complexity estimation and the evaluation of randomized and approximation algorithms
- Construct and interpret basic stochastic models relevant to computer science applications, such as Markov chains, random graphs, and probabilistic inference systems

---

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following courses prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-10200: Introduction to Programming and Computational Thinking** — foundational programming skills and algorithmic reasoning required
- **MATH-11500: Calculus I** — proficiency in differential and integral calculus necessary for continuous probability analysis
- **MATH-22100: Discrete Mathematics for Computer Scientists** — prior exposure to combinatorics, logic, and proof-based reasoning essential
- **MATH-23300: Linear Algebra and Matrix Methods** — recommended, as matrix operations are utilized in stochastic process topics

Students who have not completed MATH-22100 must obtain written instructor approval prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-14600 will be evaluated through the following assessment components:

| Assessment | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly Problem Sets (10 total) | 25% |
| Midterm Examination I | 15% |
| Midterm Examination II | 15% |
| Computational Programming Assignments (3 total) | 20% |
| Final Examination (cumulative) | 25% |

**Problem Sets** are assigned weekly and require both analytical derivations and computational exercises. Collaboration is permitted in accordance with the Academic Integrity Policy; however, all submitted work must be independently written.

**Programming Assignments** require students to implement probabilistic algorithms and simulation models using Python or a comparable language approved by the instructor. Assignments will include problems such as Monte Carlo simulation, Bayesian inference implementation, and Markov chain modeling.

**Examinations** are closed-book with an instructor-approved reference sheet permitted. Midterm examinations are administered in-class; the final examination is scheduled through the Registrar's Office during the official examination period.
