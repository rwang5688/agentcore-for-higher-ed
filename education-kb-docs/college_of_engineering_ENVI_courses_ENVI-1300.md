### Course Description

This advanced course provides students with critical frameworks for identifying, evaluating, and integrating openly available digital resources — including video lectures, open courseware, and multimedia environmental datasets — into rigorous academic and professional environmental practice. Students will examine the pedagogical, ethical, and technical dimensions of open educational resources (OERs) within the context of environmental and natural resource sciences, with particular attention to source credibility, data provenance, and disciplinary relevance. The course also challenges students to synthesise material drawn from diverse open platforms into coherent, evidence-based arguments addressing contemporary environmental challenges.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically evaluate the academic integrity, scientific accuracy, and disciplinary authority of freely available video and course materials in the environmental sciences
- Identify and apply appropriate open educational repositories and platforms — including institutional courseware archives, government environmental databases, and vetted multimedia libraries — for use in advanced research contexts
- Synthesise information drawn from open digital resources with peer-reviewed literature to produce coherent, well-substantiated environmental analyses
- Assess the ethical and intellectual property considerations associated with the use, adaptation, and redistribution of open course materials within academic and professional settings
- Design and present a structured resource portfolio that demonstrates informed curation of open materials relevant to a specific environmental or natural resource management problem

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (Level 850), students are expected to have completed the following or equivalent:

- **ENVI-6200: Advanced Environmental Research Methods** or equivalent graduate-level research methods course
- **ENVI-7100: Environmental Data Analysis and Interpretation**
- Demonstrated proficiency in academic information literacy, as assessed through prior graduate coursework
- Completion of a minimum of 60 credit points at postgraduate level within the College of Engineering or a cognate discipline
- Written approval from the course coordinator is required for students enrolling from outside the Department of Environment and Natural Resources

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment | Weighting | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Critical Resource Audit (Individual) | 25% | A structured written audit (2,500 words) evaluating five openly available video or course resources against established academic and disciplinary standards |
| Annotated Resource Portfolio | 30% | A curated collection of no fewer than twelve open educational resources, each accompanied by a detailed critical annotation addressing relevance, credibility, and applicability to a student-nominated environmental theme |
| Research Synthesis Paper | 30% | A 4,000-word paper integrating open course materials with primary literature to address a current environmental or natural resource management issue |
| Seminar Presentation | 15% | A 20-minute presented defence of the Research Synthesis Paper, including a structured Q&A with the instructional panel |

*All assessments must adhere to Peculiar U's Academic Integrity Policy. The use of open materials must be appropriately attributed in accordance with relevant Creative Commons licensing conventions.*
