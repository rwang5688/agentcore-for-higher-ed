## Course Description

### Course Description

Discrete Mathematical Structures provides an advanced exploration of the mathematical foundations essential to computer science, covering topics such as combinatorics, graph theory, logic, set theory, relations, and algebraic structures. Students will develop rigorous analytical and proof-writing skills applicable to algorithm design, complexity analysis, and formal reasoning in computing systems. The course emphasizes both theoretical understanding and practical application of discrete mathematics within the context of advanced computer science problems.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Construct and evaluate formal mathematical proofs using a variety of techniques, including direct proof, proof by contradiction, and mathematical induction
- Apply combinatorial principles and graph-theoretic methods to model and solve complex computational problems
- Analyze the properties of algebraic structures such as groups, rings, and lattices as they relate to computing applications
- Formulate and interpret problems using propositional and predicate logic, Boolean algebra, and formal language theory
- Demonstrate proficiency in applying discrete mathematical concepts to areas including algorithm correctness, data structures, and cryptographic foundations

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 30% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Research Paper / Theoretical Analysis | 20% |
| Final Examination | 20% |
| In-Class Participation & Quizzes | 10% |

**Problem Sets:** Six structured assignments distributed throughout the semester, requiring students to solve proof-based and applied problems corresponding to current lecture topics.

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Closed-book examinations assessing mastery of core theoretical concepts, proof construction, and problem-solving. The final examination is comprehensive.

**Research Paper / Theoretical Analysis:** A 10–15 page written paper in which students investigate an application of discrete mathematics to an area of their choosing within computer science (e.g., network theory, cryptography, formal verification). Papers must include original analysis and proper academic citation.

**Participation and Quizzes:** Short in-class or online quizzes administered periodically to reinforce lecture material, combined with assessed participation in seminar-style discussions.
