## Course Description

### Course Description

This course examines the environmental consequences of modern agricultural practices and explores evidence-based strategies for reducing agriculture's ecological footprint. Students will investigate issues including soil degradation, water contamination, greenhouse gas emissions, and biodiversity loss as they relate to food production systems at local, regional, and global scales. Emphasis is placed on practical mitigation frameworks, policy instruments, and emerging technologies that support a transition toward more sustainable and resilient agricultural systems.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and explain the primary environmental impacts associated with conventional agricultural practices, including effects on soil health, water quality, air quality, and biodiversity
- Evaluate a range of mitigation strategies—such as precision agriculture, cover cropping, integrated pest management, and agroforestry—and assess their effectiveness across different environmental contexts
- Apply basic environmental assessment tools to analyze the sustainability profile of a given agricultural system or practice
- Interpret relevant environmental legislation, policy frameworks, and international agreements that govern agricultural land use and emissions
- Communicate findings and recommendations regarding agricultural mitigation strategies clearly and effectively in both written and oral formats

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **ENVI-1100: Introduction to Environmental Science** or equivalent
- **BIOL-1200: Principles of Ecology** or equivalent
- Students with prior coursework in geography, earth sciences, or agricultural studies are encouraged to enroll; those without any natural science background should consult the instructor before registering

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Case Study Analysis (written, individual) | 20% |
| Group Mitigation Strategy Project & Presentation | 25% |
| Weekly Response Papers & Participation | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess comprehension of core concepts, terminology, and frameworks covered in lectures and assigned readings. Questions include multiple choice, short answer, and one extended analytical response.

**Case Study Analysis** requires each student to examine a real-world agricultural system, identify its principal environmental impacts, and propose a mitigation plan supported by course materials and independent research. Submissions should be 1,500–2,000 words.

**Group Mitigation Strategy Project** tasks teams of 3–4 students with developing a comprehensive mitigation proposal for an assigned agricultural sector or region. Teams will deliver a 20-minute presentation to the class and submit a supporting written report of 3,000–4,000 words.

**Weekly Response Papers** are short (300–400 word) written reflections on assigned readings or discussion topics, submitted prior to each Thursday session.

### Required Materials

Students are expected to obtain the following before the first week of classes:

- Halverson, P. R., & Corteau, M. J. (2019). *Feeding the World, Healing the Land: Environmental Approaches to Sustainable Agriculture* (3rd ed.). Greenvale Academic Press.
- Ndukwe, A. S., & Thalmann, R. (2021). *Agricultural Systems and Ecological Risk: An Introductory Framework*. Westbrook University Publishers.
- Osei-Bonsu, C. (2020). *Policy Tools for a Greener Food System: From Field to Framework*. Harrington & Lowe.

A course reader containing additional peer-reviewed articles, government reports, and case study materials will be made available through the university's online learning portal at the start of term. Students are not required to purchase the course reader separately.
