## Course Description

**COMP-13400 | Mobile Software Engineering | 3 Credit Points**
*Department of Computer Science | College of Engineering*
*Instruction provided by faculty of the Department of Computer Science*

---

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the principles and practices of mobile application development, with emphasis on designing, building, and deploying software for modern mobile platforms including Android and iOS. Students explore the full mobile development lifecycle, from requirements gathering and interface design through to testing, optimization, and distribution. Special attention is given to platform-specific constraints, user experience conventions, and the unique challenges posed by resource-limited, connectivity-dependent mobile environments.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Design and implement functional mobile applications for at least one major mobile platform, applying platform-appropriate UI/UX patterns and design guidelines
- Demonstrate understanding of mobile application architecture models, including MVC, MVVM, and component-based design, and apply these appropriately in project contexts
- Utilize mobile-specific APIs and device hardware features — including location services, camera interfaces, push notifications, and local storage — within developed applications
- Apply testing strategies relevant to mobile environments, including unit testing, device emulation, and real-device testing, to ensure application reliability and performance
- Evaluate and address key concerns in mobile development including battery efficiency, network latency, offline functionality, and secure data handling

---

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-11200 Introduction to Programming** or demonstrated equivalent proficiency
- **COMP-12150 Data Structures and Algorithms**
- **COMP-12310 Software Engineering Fundamentals** *(may be taken concurrently with instructor approval)*

Students are expected to have working knowledge of at least one object-oriented programming language (Java, Kotlin, Swift, or Python) and basic familiarity with version control systems such as Git. Prior experience with web technologies is beneficial but not required.

---

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Programming Assignments (×4) | 30% | Individual coding assignments targeting specific platform features and development concepts |
| Midterm Examination | 15% | Written exam covering theoretical foundations, architecture patterns, and platform concepts |
| Group Application Project | 35% | Team-based project spanning the full semester, culminating in a functional mobile application and formal presentation |
| Final Examination | 15% | Comprehensive written exam with practical problem-solving components |
| Participation & Lab Engagement | 5% | Active engagement in supervised lab sessions and in-class discussions |

**Note on the Group Application Project:** Teams of three to four students will develop a complete mobile application of their choosing, subject to instructor approval. Deliverables include a project proposal, design documentation, iterative prototype reviews, a final working build, and a recorded or live demonstration. Individual contribution assessments will be conducted alongside team evaluations.

### Required Materials

Students are required to obtain the following materials prior to the first week of instruction:

- **Farrell, D. & Okonkwo, T.** (2022). *Mobile Application Development: Architecture, Design, and Deployment* (3rd ed.). Halworth Academic Press. ISBN: 978-1-55203-874-2
- **Lindqvist, C.** (2021). *Cross-Platform and Native Mobile Engineering: A Practical Guide*. Stonebridge Technical Publishers. ISBN: 978-0-94871-203-6

**Recommended (not required):**
- **Mwangi, P. & Tscharke, R.** (2020). *UX Patterns for Mobile Interfaces*. Pellucid Design Press. ISBN: 978-1-60934-517-0

All students must have access to a personal computer capable of running at least one of the designated mobile development environments (Android Studio or Xcode). Access to a physical Android or iOS device is strongly encouraged, though not strictly required as emulators will be available via departmental lab resources. Software licenses and SDK downloads will be arranged through the department at no additional cost to enrolled students.
