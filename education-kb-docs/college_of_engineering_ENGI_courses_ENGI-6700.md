## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6700 provides students with a comprehensive introduction to microcontroller architecture, programming, and application within embedded systems. Students will explore the fundamental principles of hardware-software integration, gaining hands-on experience designing and implementing microcontroller-based solutions for real-world engineering problems. Emphasis is placed on developing practical proficiency in both circuit interfacing and low-level programming, preparing students for advanced study in embedded systems and digital electronics.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and explain the core components of microcontroller architecture, including memory, I/O ports, timers, and communication interfaces
- Write, compile, and debug embedded C programs to control microcontroller peripherals and respond to real-time inputs
- Design and construct basic interface circuits connecting microcontrollers to sensors, actuators, and external devices
- Apply systematic troubleshooting methodologies to diagnose and resolve hardware and software faults in embedded systems
- Evaluate design trade-offs in selecting appropriate microcontroller platforms for specific engineering applications

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-6700 must have successfully completed the following or equivalent coursework:

- **ENGI-4410 – Digital Logic and Circuit Design** (minimum grade: C+)
- **ENGI-4320 – Fundamentals of Electrical Circuits** (minimum grade: C+)
- **COMP-3210 – Introduction to Programming in C** or demonstrated proficiency in a structured programming language
- Completion of at least 60 undergraduate credit points in an Engineering or related discipline, or written permission from the supervising instructor

Students are also expected to have basic familiarity with circuit breadboarding and laboratory safety protocols prior to enrollment.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Laboratory Assignments (6 × guided practicals) | 30% |
| Midterm Written Examination | 20% |
| Programming Project 1 – Peripheral Control System | 15% |
| Programming Project 2 – Sensor-Actuator Integration | 15% |
| Final Design Project and Technical Report | 15% |
| Participation and Lab Preparation Quizzes | 5% |

**Laboratory Assignments** are completed in scheduled lab sessions and assess students' ability to implement course concepts in a supervised hardware environment. **Programming Projects** require individual submission of documented, functional embedded code with accompanying circuit diagrams. The **Final Design Project** is a team-based assignment in which students propose, build, and present an original microcontroller application; deliverables include a working prototype, a formal technical report, and an oral presentation to a panel of instructors.

### Required Materials and Textbooks

Students must obtain the following materials before the first laboratory session:

- **Vanholt, D. R. & Erssen, P. T.** (2021). *Embedded Systems Fundamentals: Architecture, Programming, and Design with the ARM Cortex-M Series* (3rd ed.). Bridgeford Technical Press. — Primary course textbook; all weekly readings are drawn from this volume.
- **Castellucci, M. & Osei-Prempeh, F.** (2019). *Practical Microcontroller Programming in C: From Registers to Real-World Applications*. Norwell Engineering Publications. — Supplementary programming reference; selected chapters assigned alongside projects.
- **ENGI-6700 Laboratory Manual** (current edition). Published by the Peculiar U College of Engineering and updated each semester; available for purchase through the campus print centre or download via the course portal.

**Hardware Kit – ENGI-6700 Embedded Systems Starter Pack:** Students are required to purchase the designated laboratory kit, available through the Peculiar U Engineering Stores. The kit includes an ARM Cortex-M4 development board, breadboard, jumper wire set, assorted sensors and actuators, resistor/capacitor component pack, USB programming cable, and a regulated 5V power supply module. A list of approved substitute kits is available from the department upon request.
