This advanced graduate course examines the ethical, social, and legal dimensions of data science practice, with emphasis on the responsibilities of data professionals operating within complex institutional, societal, and technological environments. Students will critically evaluate frameworks for algorithmic fairness, data privacy, consent, and the societal impact of predictive systems, drawing on both philosophical traditions and contemporary case studies from industry and government. The course prepares students to identify, navigate, and communicate ethical challenges inherent in the design, deployment, and governance of data-driven systems.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and apply major ethical frameworks—including utilitarian, deontological, and virtue-based approaches—to real-world data science scenarios involving harm, bias, and accountability.
- Evaluate the technical and normative dimensions of algorithmic fairness, identifying sources of bias in data collection, model training, and system deployment.
- Critically assess data privacy legislation, informed consent standards, and institutional data governance policies across domestic and international contexts.
- Synthesize interdisciplinary perspectives from law, sociology, philosophy, and engineering to construct well-reasoned ethical arguments regarding the use of emerging data technologies.
- Develop and communicate actionable ethical guidelines and impact assessments suitable for adoption within a professional data science team or organization.

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed a minimum of one graduate-level course in data science, machine learning, or a closely related computational field (e.g., DATA-500 or equivalent). Familiarity with statistical modeling and data pipeline fundamentals is expected. A background in introductory philosophy or social science methodology, while not required, is considered advantageous. Students from outside the Department of Data Science must obtain instructor permission prior to enrollment.

### Assessment Methods

- **Case Study Portfolio (30%):** Students compile a semester-long portfolio of written analyses responding to a series of assigned real-world and hypothetical ethical dilemmas in data science. Each entry should demonstrate integration of course frameworks and personal professional reflection. Final portfolios are submitted at the end of the term.
- **Research Paper (30%):** A substantial individual research paper (6,000–8,000 words) investigating an ethical issue of the student's choosing within the data science domain. Papers must engage with primary sources, current literature, and relevant legal or regulatory frameworks. A proposal and annotated bibliography are due at mid-semester.
- **Group Policy Brief (20%):** Student teams of three to four will produce a professional policy brief addressed to a fictional or real organization, recommending ethical standards or governance reforms for a specified data-driven application. Briefs are formally presented to the class during the final two weeks of the semester.
- **Participation and Seminar Discussion (20%):** Given the seminar format of this course, active, substantive engagement in weekly discussions is essential. Students will be evaluated on the quality, consistency, and intellectual rigor of their contributions, including two occasions on which each student is expected to lead discussion.
