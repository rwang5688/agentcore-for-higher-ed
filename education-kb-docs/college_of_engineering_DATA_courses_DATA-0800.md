### Course Description

This course provides an in-depth examination of linear regression as a foundational tool in statistical modeling and data science, covering both simple and multiple regression frameworks with rigorous mathematical treatment. Students will develop the theoretical understanding and applied competencies necessary to build, evaluate, and interpret regression models using real-world datasets. Emphasis is placed on the assumptions underlying linear regression, diagnostic techniques for model validation, and the responsible communication of quantitative findings to diverse audiences.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Derive and apply the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimation method to fit simple and multiple linear regression models, demonstrating a clear understanding of the underlying mathematical principles
- Evaluate the validity of core regression assumptions — including linearity, homoscedasticity, independence, and normality — using appropriate diagnostic plots and statistical tests
- Implement regularization techniques, including Ridge and Lasso regression, to address multicollinearity and overfitting in high-dimensional datasets
- Critically interpret regression outputs, including coefficient estimates, confidence intervals, and measures of model fit such as R², adjusted R², and root mean squared error (RMSE)
- Communicate analytical findings effectively through written reports and visual presentations, demonstrating awareness of ethical considerations related to model bias and data representation

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **DATA-0210: Introduction to Statistical Computing** or equivalent
- **DATA-0225: Probability and Statistical Inference**
- **MATH-0180: Linear Algebra I** (minimum grade of C+)
- **DATA-0300: Foundations of Data Analysis** (may be taken concurrently with instructor approval)

Students without formal programming experience in Python or R are strongly encouraged to complete the Department of Data Science's non-credit workshop, *Introduction to Statistical Programming*, before the start of term.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 25% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Applied Regression Analysis Project | 30% |
| Final Examination | 20% |
| Participation & Lab Engagement | 5% |

**Problem Sets** are assigned bi-weekly and require students to apply regression concepts to structured datasets using Python or R. Collaboration is permitted, but submissions must be written independently.

**Midterm Examination** is a closed-book written examination covering theoretical foundations, model derivation, and interpretation of regression outputs.

**Applied Regression Analysis Project** is a semester-long individual or paired project in which students select a real-world dataset, formulate a research question, construct and validate a regression model, and produce a formal written report (approximately 2,500–3,500 words) alongside a 15-minute oral presentation delivered during the final two weeks of term.

**Final Examination** is a comprehensive open-notes examination that includes both theoretical questions and a practical data analysis component to be completed using the statistical software of the student's choice.
