## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6800: The Art of Engineering explores the intersection of creative thinking, aesthetic sensibility, and rigorous engineering methodology, challenging students to approach complex technical problems through an integrated lens of both artistic and scientific reasoning. This advanced course examines how design philosophy, visual communication, and conceptual abstraction can elevate engineering solutions beyond mere functionality to achieve elegance, innovation, and human-centered impact. Students will engage with historical and contemporary case studies demonstrating how the most enduring engineering achievements reflect a harmonious synthesis of technical precision and artistic vision.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically evaluate engineering designs using both technical performance metrics and aesthetic principles drawn from art, architecture, and industrial design traditions
- Apply creative problem-solving frameworks and divergent thinking techniques to generate novel engineering solutions that balance functional requirements with elegance and clarity
- Communicate complex engineering concepts through sophisticated visual, written, and oral presentations that demonstrate fluency in both technical and humanistic discourse
- Analyze the ethical, cultural, and societal dimensions embedded within engineering artifacts and systems, situating technical decisions within broader human contexts
- Synthesize interdisciplinary methodologies to produce original engineering proposals that demonstrate measurable innovation informed by artistic and conceptual thinking

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrollment:

- ENGI-5200: Advanced Engineering Design Principles or equivalent
- ENGI-5410: Systems Thinking and Structural Analysis
- Completion of a minimum of 90 credit points toward an accredited undergraduate or graduate engineering degree
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 3.0 or departmental consent of instructor
- Recommended: prior coursework or demonstrated competency in visual arts, architecture, or design theory (DSGN-4100 or equivalent)

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Design Portfolio (30%):** A cumulative portfolio developed throughout the semester documenting the evolution of each major project, including sketches, prototypes, critical reflections, and final design rationales. Submitted at the end of the term.
- **Interdisciplinary Research Paper (25%):** A 4,000–5,500 word scholarly paper examining a significant engineering achievement through an aesthetic and cultural-critical framework, drawing on peer-reviewed sources from both engineering and humanities literature.
- **Collaborative Design Project (25%):** Working in teams of three to four, students will conceive, develop, and present an original engineering proposal that explicitly incorporates artistic principles. Assessment includes an oral presentation, technical documentation, and peer evaluation.
- **Seminar Participation and Critical Responses (10%):** Active, substantive engagement in weekly seminar discussions, supported by five short critical response papers (400–600 words each) responding to assigned readings and guest lectures.
- **Midterm Examination (10%):** A written examination assessing comprehension of theoretical frameworks, historical case studies, and core interdisciplinary methodologies covered in the first half of the course.
