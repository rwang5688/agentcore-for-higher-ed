This course provides a rigorous introduction to the foundational principles of probability theory and statistical inference as applied to real-world data science problems, with a focus on implementation using the Python programming language. Students will explore key concepts including probability distributions, hypothesis testing, regression analysis, and Bayesian reasoning, integrating theoretical understanding with hands-on computational practice. By the end of the course, students will be equipped to design and execute data-driven analyses, critically interpret statistical results, and communicate findings effectively within a professional or research context.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of DATA-3400, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a solid understanding of core probability concepts, including conditional probability, independence, random variables, and common discrete and continuous probability distributions.
- Apply inferential statistical methods — including hypothesis testing, confidence interval construction, and analysis of variance — to draw meaningful conclusions from datasets of varying complexity.
- Implement statistical models and data analysis pipelines using Python libraries such as NumPy, SciPy, pandas, and statsmodels, producing reproducible and well-documented code.
- Evaluate the assumptions underlying statistical techniques and assess the validity and limitations of analytical results in the context of real data science applications.
- Communicate statistical findings clearly and accurately through written reports and data visualisations, demonstrating awareness of ethical considerations in data interpretation and reporting.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in DATA-3400 must have successfully completed the following courses or their approved equivalents:

- **DATA-1100: Introduction to Data Science and Python Programming** — foundational Python programming skills and familiarity with data structures are assumed throughout this course.
- **MATH-2210: Calculus II** — comfort with integral and differential calculus is required for understanding continuous probability distributions and statistical derivations.
- **MATH-2150: Linear Algebra for Engineers** — matrix operations and vector space concepts are applied extensively in regression modelling and multivariate analysis.
- Students who have not completed these prerequisites but believe they have equivalent preparation should consult their academic advisor or contact the Department of Data Science directly prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in DATA-3400 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly Problem Sets (×8) | 20% | Eight graded problem sets combining theoretical probability/statistics exercises with Python-based computational tasks. Submitted via the course portal. |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book, in-person written examination covering probability theory, distributions, and introductory inferential statistics (Weeks 1–6 content). |
| Statistical Analysis Project | 25% | An individual project requiring students to select a real-world dataset, conduct a full statistical analysis in Python, and submit a written report (approximately 2,500–3,500 words) with accompanying code and visualisations. |
| Group Data Science Case Study | 15% | A collaborative group assignment (teams of 3–4) in which students apply statistical modelling techniques to a provided industry-style dataset and present findings in a 15-minute oral presentation. |
| Final Examination | 20% | A comprehensive closed-book, in-person written examination covering all course content, with an emphasis on applied statistical reasoning and interpretation. |
