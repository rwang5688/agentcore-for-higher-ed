### Course Description

This course provides students with a rigorous introduction to Lean Six Sigma methodologies as applied to engineering systems, manufacturing environments, and service-based operations. Students will explore the foundational principles of waste elimination, process variation reduction, and continuous improvement through the integrated DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control) framework and Lean thinking philosophy. Emphasis is placed on real-world application through case studies, data-driven decision-making tools, and collaborative process improvement projects aligned with industry standards.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the core principles and historical development of both Lean methodology and Six Sigma, and articulate how their integration creates a unified approach to process excellence
- Apply the DMAIC framework to identify, analyze, and resolve process inefficiencies and quality defects within engineering and operational contexts
- Utilize key Lean Six Sigma tools — including process mapping, value stream analysis, Pareto charts, control charts, fishbone diagrams, and failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA) — to diagnose and improve system performance
- Interpret basic statistical data relevant to process capability, variation analysis, and measurement system evaluation in support of evidence-based process improvement decisions
- Design and present a structured process improvement plan for a real or simulated engineering scenario, demonstrating competency in team-based problem solving and structured reporting

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Process Improvement Project | 30% |
| Lab Exercises and Tool Assignments | 15% |
| Participation and Reflective Journals | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess conceptual understanding of Lean Six Sigma theory, tool application, and interpretation of process data. Both examinations include a combination of multiple-choice, short-answer, and data analysis questions.

**Process Improvement Project** is a semester-long team-based assignment in which student groups of three to four identify a real or simulated process, apply the full DMAIC cycle, and present their findings in both a written report and a formal oral presentation during the final week of classes. Assessment criteria include rigor of analysis, appropriate tool selection, quality of recommendations, and professional communication.

**Lab Exercises and Tool Assignments** consist of structured weekly or biweekly exercises requiring students to apply specific Lean Six Sigma tools — such as value stream mapping, control chart construction, and FMEA completion — to provided datasets and process scenarios.

**Participation and Reflective Journals** require students to engage actively in class discussions, case study analyses, and guest lecture sessions, and to submit four short reflective journal entries throughout the semester connecting course concepts to engineering practice.
