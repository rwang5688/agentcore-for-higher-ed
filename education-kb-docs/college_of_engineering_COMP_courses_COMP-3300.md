### Course Description

This course explores the foundational structures of discrete mathematics with a strong emphasis on computational implementation and algorithmic problem-solving. Students will examine topics including graph theory, combinatorics, logic, set theory, relations, and number theory through both theoretical analysis and practical programming exercises. By bridging abstract mathematical concepts with real-world computational applications, the course equips students with the rigorous reasoning skills essential for advanced study in computer science, algorithm design, and software engineering.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Construct and evaluate formal logical arguments, proofs by induction, and proof by contradiction as applied to computational problems and algorithm correctness.
- Analyse and apply graph-theoretic concepts — including trees, paths, connectivity, and graph colouring — to model and solve computational problems programmatically.
- Design and implement combinatorial algorithms addressing permutations, combinations, recurrence relations, and generating functions within a programming environment.
- Apply principles of modular arithmetic, number theory, and Boolean algebra to problems in cryptography, data structures, and digital circuit design.
- Critically assess the computational complexity and efficiency of discrete algorithms, relating theoretical results to practical performance considerations.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-3300 must have successfully completed the following:

- **COMP-1100: Introduction to Programming** or equivalent, with a minimum passing grade of C or better.
- **MATH-2210: Foundations of Mathematical Reasoning** or **MATH-2200: Calculus II**, demonstrating prior exposure to formal mathematical structures.
- **COMP-2150: Data Structures and Algorithms**, ensuring familiarity with algorithmic thinking and basic complexity analysis.

Students lacking one or more prerequisites may seek written departmental approval from the course instructor prior to enrolment.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-3300 will be evaluated through a combination of theoretical and applied assessment components:

- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written examination covering logical proof techniques, set theory, relations, and introductory combinatorics. Questions will include both short-answer proofs and multi-step problem solving.
- **Final Examination (25%):** A comprehensive written examination spanning all course topics, with an emphasis on integration of concepts across graph theory, number theory, and algorithm design.
- **Programming Assignments (30%):** Four graded programming assignments requiring students to implement discrete algorithms in a designated language (typically Python or Java). Assignments assess both correctness and computational efficiency.
- **Problem Sets (15%):** Six written problem sets distributed throughout the semester, targeting proof construction, combinatorial reasoning, and graph analysis. Collaboration is permitted with proper attribution; submitted work must be independently written.
- **Research Paper (10%):** A 1,500–2,000 word individual paper in which students investigate a real-world application of discrete mathematics in computing (e.g., network routing, cryptographic protocols, scheduling algorithms). Papers are assessed on technical accuracy, clarity, and depth of analysis.
