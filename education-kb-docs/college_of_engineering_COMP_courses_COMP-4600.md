## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-4600: Introductory Computer Science Course (2016) provides students with a foundational understanding of core computational principles, algorithmic thinking, and fundamental programming paradigms as established within the 2016 curricular framework. Students will explore the theoretical underpinnings of computer science alongside practical implementation skills using industry-standard languages and tools. This course serves as a gateway to advanced study within the Computer Science discipline, equipping students with essential problem-solving and logical reasoning competencies.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a working understanding of fundamental programming constructs, including variables, control flow, loops, and functions, and apply these constructs to solve well-defined computational problems
- Analyze and design basic algorithms, evaluating their correctness and efficiency using introductory complexity analysis techniques
- Apply principles of structured and modular program design to produce readable, maintainable, and logically sound code
- Identify and describe core computer science concepts including data representation, memory management, and the relationship between hardware and software abstraction layers
- Collaborate effectively on small-scale programming tasks, articulating technical decisions clearly in both written and verbal forms

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrollment:

- MATH-1200: Discrete Mathematics or equivalent
- COMP-1100: Introduction to Programming Logic and Problem Solving
- COMP-2250: Foundations of Digital Systems
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.5 is recommended for students at the 3435 level

Students who have not completed the above prerequisites are encouraged to consult their academic advisor before enrolling.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Programming Assignments (×5) | 30% |
| Laboratory Work and Participation | 10% |
| Group Project and Presentation | 15% |

**Programming Assignments** are distributed throughout the semester and require students to implement solutions to structured computational problems, demonstrating mastery of course concepts at each stage of instruction.

**The Group Project** requires teams of three to four students to design, implement, document, and present a small software application addressing a defined problem domain. A written technical report is submitted alongside the final demonstration.

**Examinations** are closed-book and include a combination of multiple-choice, short-answer, and code-tracing questions. Students must achieve a passing grade on the Final Examination independently of other assessment components.
