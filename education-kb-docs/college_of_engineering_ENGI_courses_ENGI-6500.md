## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6500: Nuclear Reactions and Radiation provides an advanced foundational examination of nuclear physics principles as applied to engineering systems, covering nuclear reaction mechanics, radioactive decay processes, and the interaction of radiation with matter. Students will explore the theoretical and applied dimensions of fission, fusion, and neutron transport, with particular attention to safety protocols, shielding design, and reactor core behavior. This course serves as a critical gateway into graduate-level nuclear engineering study, bridging core physics theory with practical engineering application.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and mathematically model nuclear reaction types, including fission, fusion, and radioactive decay chains, applying conservation laws and binding energy principles
- Evaluate the interaction mechanisms between ionizing radiation and various materials, including attenuation, absorption, and scattering phenomena
- Apply neutron transport theory and diffusion equations to assess reactor core behavior and criticality conditions
- Design and assess radiation shielding solutions using established dosimetry standards and regulatory frameworks from bodies such as the NRC and IAEA
- Critically interpret experimental nuclear data and apply computational modeling tools to predict radiation behavior in engineered environments

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-6500 will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A closed-book written examination assessing comprehension of nuclear reaction theory, decay mechanics, and radiation interaction principles covered in the first half of the course. Students are permitted one hand-written reference sheet.
- **Final Examination (25%):** A comprehensive closed-book examination spanning all course material, with emphasis on integrative problem-solving across reaction modeling, shielding design, and criticality analysis.
- **Problem Sets (20%):** Eight graded problem sets distributed throughout the semester, requiring quantitative analysis and applied calculation. Collaboration is permitted, but submitted work must be independently written.
- **Laboratory Reports (20%):** Four formal laboratory reports corresponding to scheduled lab sessions, in which students conduct radiation measurement exercises and neutron flux estimation experiments using the university's nuclear laboratory facilities. Reports must conform to departmental formatting standards.
- **Research and Design Project (15%):** A semester-long group project (teams of 2–3 students) requiring the design of a radiation shielding system for a specified engineering scenario. Teams will submit a written technical report and deliver a 15-minute oral presentation during the final two weeks of term.
