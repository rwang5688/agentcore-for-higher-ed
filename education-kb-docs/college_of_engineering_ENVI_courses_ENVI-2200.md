### Course Description

This course examines the relationship between literary expression and the natural world, exploring how writers across cultures and historical periods have represented, interpreted, and responded to environmental landscapes, ecological crises, and human-nature dynamics. Students will engage with a broad range of texts — including fiction, poetry, nature writing, and environmental journalism — to develop critical frameworks for understanding how language shapes our perception of and responsibility toward the environment. The course situates literary analysis within the broader concerns of environmental science and policy, making it uniquely relevant to students pursuing careers in engineering, sustainability, and natural resource management.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and critically analyze major themes of ecological concern, environmental justice, and human-nature relationships as they appear across a range of literary genres and cultural traditions
- Apply foundational concepts from ecocriticism and environmental humanities to the close reading and interpretation of literary and nonfiction texts
- Articulate connections between literary representations of the environment and real-world issues such as climate change, land use, biodiversity loss, and resource extraction
- Compose well-structured analytical writing that integrates textual evidence with broader environmental and scientific contexts
- Demonstrate an appreciation for interdisciplinary thinking by drawing meaningful links between humanistic inquiry and the technical dimensions of environmental and engineering disciplines

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed **ENGL-1035: Academic Writing and Critical Thinking** or an equivalent first-year composition course prior to enrolling in ENVI-2200. A minimum grade of C or higher in the prerequisite course is required. Students with no prior background in literary studies are welcome; no previous coursework in environmental science is assumed or required.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly Reading Responses (8 × short written reflections, 150–250 words each) | 20% |
| Midterm Essay (1,200–1,500 words, close reading of an assigned text) | 20% |
| Environmental Text Analysis Project (group presentation and written report) | 25% |
| Final Research Essay (2,000–2,500 words, independently developed argument) | 30% |
| Participation and In-Class Discussion | 5% |

**Weekly Reading Responses** are submitted through the course portal before each Tuesday session and are assessed on the quality of engagement with the assigned readings rather than on formal argumentation. The **Environmental Text Analysis Project** requires groups of three to four students to select a contemporary work of environmental literature or nonfiction, present a critical analysis to the class, and submit a collaborative written report of 1,500–2,000 words. The **Final Research Essay** asks students to develop an original argument connecting a literary work of their choosing to a specific environmental issue, incorporating a minimum of five peer-reviewed or scholarly sources.

### Required Materials

Students are expected to acquire the following texts prior to the first week of class. All titles are available through the Peculiar U campus bookstore and the university library's course reserves portal.

- **Holloway, R. C., & Nkemdi, A. J.** *Words from the Watershed: An Anthology of Environmental Literature*. 3rd ed. Fernmere Academic Press, 2019.
- **Callender, S. M.** *Reading the Wild: An Introduction to Ecocriticism and Literary Analysis*. Thorngate University Press, 2021.
- **Osei-Bonsu, P., & Veltri, D.** *The Burning Page: Climate, Crisis, and Contemporary Writing*. Aldermoss Publishing, 2020.

A course reading packet containing supplementary essays, excerpts, and primary source materials will be made available through the Peculiar U course management system at no additional cost. Students are required to bring assigned readings to every class session, either in print or on a reading device. Use of mobile phones for reading purposes during class must be pre-approved by the instructor of record.
