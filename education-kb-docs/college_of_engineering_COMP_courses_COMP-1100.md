### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive and accessible introduction to artificial intelligence, designed to equip students from all academic backgrounds with foundational knowledge of AI principles, capabilities, and societal implications. Students will explore core AI concepts including machine learning, natural language processing, and computer vision, with an emphasis on practical understanding over advanced mathematics. By the end of the course, students will be prepared to engage critically and confidently with AI technologies in both professional and everyday contexts.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a working understanding of fundamental AI concepts, terminology, and subfields, including machine learning, neural networks, and data-driven decision making
- Evaluate the ethical, social, and economic implications of AI deployment across industries such as healthcare, education, finance, and criminal justice
- Apply entry-level prompt engineering and AI tool usage skills to solve practical, real-world problems in a structured and informed manner
- Critically assess the limitations, biases, and failure modes of AI systems and articulate responsible approaches to AI adoption
- Communicate clearly about AI technologies and their impact to both technical and non-technical audiences

---

### Prerequisites

As a Level 650 course, students are expected to have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-4200: Foundations of Computing** or equivalent introductory computing coursework
- **MATH-3100: Introduction to Quantitative Reasoning** or demonstrated comfort with basic statistical and logical reasoning
- Completion of at least 60 undergraduate credit points or instructor permission for students outside the Computer Science program
- No prior programming experience is required; however, familiarity with basic digital literacy is strongly recommended

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through a combination of the following assessment components:

| Assessment | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly Reflection Journals | 15% | Short written reflections (300–500 words) submitted after each module, connecting course concepts to current events or personal experiences |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Closed-book written examination covering Weeks 1–7, including multiple choice, short answer, and scenario-based questions |
| Group Case Study Project | 25% | Collaborative analysis of a real-world AI application, including a written report (2,000–3,000 words) and a 15-minute in-class presentation |
| Ethics & Society Essay | 20% | Individual research essay (1,500–2,500 words) critically examining an AI-related ethical dilemma of the student's choosing |
| Final Examination | 20% | Cumulative closed-book examination covering all course content, including practical interpretation of AI outputs and decision scenarios |

*All written submissions must adhere to the Peculiar U Academic Integrity Policy. The use of generative AI tools in assessed work must be disclosed and used only within the bounds permitted by the course instructor.*
