## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-10600: Introduction to Electrical Engineering and Computer Science I provides students with a foundational exploration of the core principles underlying both electrical engineering and computer science, bridging theoretical concepts with practical problem-solving methodologies. Students will examine fundamental topics including circuit analysis, digital logic, algorithmic thinking, and introductory programming within a unified engineering framework. This course establishes the essential technical vocabulary, analytical habits, and interdisciplinary perspective required for advanced study across the College of Engineering.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate understanding of fundamental electrical concepts, including voltage, current, resistance, and basic circuit behavior in both DC and AC systems
- Apply principles of digital logic and Boolean algebra to design and analyze simple combinational and sequential circuits
- Develop and implement basic algorithms and programs using a structured, high-level programming language, emphasizing clarity, correctness, and efficiency
- Translate real-world engineering problems into formal computational and mathematical models, applying systematic problem-solving strategies
- Communicate technical findings and design decisions effectively through written reports, code documentation, and collaborative laboratory exercises

---

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-10600 must have satisfied the following requirements:

- Completion of or concurrent enrollment in **MATH-10200: Pre-Calculus and Analytical Reasoning** or an equivalent qualifying examination score
- Demonstrated proficiency in high school-level algebra and basic logic, as assessed by the College of Engineering placement examination
- No prior programming experience is required; however, familiarity with computational thinking concepts is strongly recommended
- Students must be formally admitted to the College of Engineering or have written departmental approval prior to enrollment

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-10600 will be evaluated through a combination of the following components:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly Problem Sets | 20% |
| Laboratory Assignments | 25% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Programming Projects (×2) | 20% |
| Final Examination | 15% |

**Weekly Problem Sets:** Assigned each Monday and due the following Friday, covering circuit analysis, Boolean logic, and algorithmic reasoning from that week's lectures.

**Laboratory Assignments:** Hands-on sessions held in the Engineering Computing and Circuits Laboratory (ECCL), conducted in pairs. Students will complete eight structured lab exercises spanning breadboard circuit construction, logic gate implementation, and introductory programming tasks, with written lab reports submitted within one week of each session.

**Midterm Examination:** A written, closed-book examination administered in Week 8, covering all lecture and laboratory material from Weeks 1–7. One standard formula sheet (provided by the department) is permitted.

**Programming Projects:** Two individual programming projects assigned in Weeks 5 and 11, respectively. Projects require students to design, implement, document, and test programs of increasing complexity, with evaluation based on functionality, code quality, and a brief written design rationale.

**Final Examination:** A comprehensive, closed-book examination administered during the official examination period, covering all course material. One department-issued formula sheet is permitted.

*Note: Students must achieve a passing grade in both the laboratory component and the examination component independently to receive a passing grade for the course overall. Academic integrity is strictly enforced; all submitted work must represent the student's own effort in accordance with Peculiar University's Academic Honesty Policy.*
