## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-6300: Nonlinear Finite Element Analysis provides graduate-level students with a rigorous treatment of the theoretical foundations and computational implementation of nonlinear finite element methods as applied to structural, mechanical, and continuum systems. Topics include geometric and material nonlinearities, large deformation mechanics, contact and friction formulations, and iterative solution strategies including Newton-Raphson and arc-length methods. Students will develop practical competency in applying commercial and research-grade finite element software to model complex engineering systems exhibiting strongly nonlinear behavior.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Derive and apply the weak form of nonlinear governing equations for solid mechanics problems involving large strains, large displacements, and nonlinear constitutive relationships
- Implement and critically evaluate iterative and incremental solution procedures, including full and modified Newton-Raphson methods, for the resolution of nonlinear algebraic systems arising from finite element discretisation
- Formulate finite element models incorporating material nonlinearities such as plasticity, hyperelasticity, and viscoplasticity, demonstrating an understanding of appropriate constitutive model selection
- Analyse and interpret convergence behavior, numerical instability, and sources of error inherent to nonlinear finite element simulations, and apply appropriate remediation strategies
- Design, execute, and validate nonlinear finite element analyses of complex engineering structures using industry-standard software, critically assessing results against analytical or experimental benchmarks

- Proficiency in at least one scientific programming environment (MATLAB, Python, or equivalent), as demonstrated through prior coursework or departmental assessment
- Graduate standing in the College of Engineering or written permission of the course coordinator

### Required Materials and Textbooks

The following texts are required for the duration of the course. Students are strongly encouraged to obtain physical copies, as annotated reading and worked-problem exercises form a core component of independent study.
