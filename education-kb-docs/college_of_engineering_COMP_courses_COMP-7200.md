## Course Description

### Course Description

Foundations of Computer Graphics provides a rigorous introduction to the theoretical and practical principles underlying the generation and manipulation of two- and three-dimensional graphical imagery through computational methods. Students will explore the mathematical frameworks, rendering pipelines, and algorithmic techniques that form the basis of modern graphics systems, with particular emphasis on real-time and offline rendering paradigms. Coursework integrates both conceptual study and hands-on implementation to equip students with the skills necessary for advanced work in visual computing, game development, simulation, and related fields.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the graphics rendering pipeline, including geometry processing, rasterization, shading, and output merging stages
- Apply linear algebra, coordinate transformations, and projective geometry to model, position, and animate objects within two- and three-dimensional virtual environments
- Implement fundamental shading models and lighting algorithms, including Phong, Blinn-Phong, and physically based rendering approximations, using modern graphics APIs
- Analyze and evaluate the trade-offs between computational cost and visual fidelity in the design of real-time and offline rendering systems
- Design and develop original graphics applications that demonstrate proficiency with programmable shader pipelines and scene management techniques

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-5110 – Data Structures and Algorithm Design** or equivalent, with a minimum grade of C
- **COMP-5340 – Linear Algebra for Computing** or **MATH-5210 – Applied Linear Algebra**, with a minimum grade of C
- **COMP-5580 – Systems Programming and Memory Management**, with a minimum grade of C
- Demonstrated programming proficiency in C++ or a comparably low-level language, as evidenced by prior coursework or departmental assessment
- Completion of at least 60 credit points at the undergraduate level or concurrent enrollment in a recognized graduate or upper-division program

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Programming Assignments (4 submissions) | 40% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 20% |
| Capstone Graphics Project | 15% |
| Participation and Lab Attendance | 5% |

**Programming Assignments** are distributed at regular intervals throughout the semester and require students to implement core graphics techniques such as ray casting, transformation pipelines, texture mapping, and shadow rendering. Submissions must include documented source code and a written technical report.

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess theoretical understanding of mathematical foundations, pipeline architecture, and algorithmic principles. Both examinations are closed-book with an approved formula sheet permitted.

**Capstone Graphics Project** is a substantial individual or paired endeavour completed during the final five weeks of the semester. Students will design and implement an original interactive or rendered graphics application, culminating in a project demonstration and a formal written report submitted to the Department of Computer Science.
