### Course Description

This course examines the long-term trajectory of Earth's environmental systems in the context of accelerating human activity, climate change, and ecological disruption. Students will investigate the interconnected dynamics of atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere, and geosphere to understand how current decisions shape the planet's future across decades and centuries. Drawing on interdisciplinary perspectives from environmental science, engineering, and policy, the course equips students to engage critically with proposed solutions and emerging challenges facing Earth's natural systems.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Describe the major Earth systems and explain how anthropogenic pressures are altering their stability and function over time
- Analyze scientific data and projections related to climate change, biodiversity loss, resource depletion, and land-use change to draw evidence-based conclusions
- Evaluate the effectiveness, feasibility, and trade-offs of proposed mitigation and adaptation strategies at local, regional, and global scales
- Apply systems thinking to assess feedback loops and tipping points within interconnected environmental and human systems
- Communicate informed perspectives on environmental futures to diverse audiences through both written and oral formats

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Research Paper (2,000–2,500 words) | 25% |
| Group Scenario Project & Presentation | 20% |
| Weekly Reflection Journals (10 submissions) | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations are written and include a combination of short-answer and extended-response questions. Students are expected to demonstrate conceptual understanding of Earth systems and the ability to interpret and critically assess environmental data.

**Research Paper:** Each student will independently research a specific environmental challenge of their choosing and submit a formal academic paper evaluating its causes, projected future impacts, and potential solutions. Papers must incorporate a minimum of eight peer-reviewed sources and adhere to the Peculiar U. citation guidelines.

**Group Scenario Project:** Working in teams of three to four, students will develop a detailed environmental futures scenario for a selected region of the world, presenting their findings in a structured oral presentation of fifteen to twenty minutes during the final weeks of the semester. Written supporting materials must also be submitted.

**Weekly Reflection Journals:** Students will submit brief (200–300 word) reflective responses to assigned readings and lectures each week. These journals are assessed on the quality of engagement and critical thinking demonstrated rather than on a right-or-wrong basis.
