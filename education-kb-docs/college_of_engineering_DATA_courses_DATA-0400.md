This advanced course provides an in-depth exploration of data visualization principles and dashboard design using Tableau, equipping students with the technical and communicative skills necessary to transform complex datasets into compelling visual narratives. Students will engage with the full Tableau workflow—from data connection and preparation through to the publication and presentation of interactive dashboards—with a strong emphasis on audience-aware storytelling and design best practices. The course situates these technical skills within broader professional contexts, preparing students to communicate data-driven insights effectively across industry, research, and public-sector settings.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Design, build, and publish interactive Tableau dashboards that integrate multiple data sources and visualization types to support complex analytical narratives.
- Apply established principles of visual perception, color theory, and information hierarchy to create clear, accurate, and aesthetically purposeful data visualizations.
- Construct cohesive data stories by selecting and sequencing visualizations to guide diverse audiences through analytical findings in a logical and persuasive manner.
- Evaluate the ethical implications of data presentation, including issues of misrepresentation, accessibility, and the responsibilities of the data communicator.
- Demonstrate proficiency in data preparation and transformation within Tableau, including the use of calculated fields, parameters, level-of-detail expressions, and dashboard actions.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Dashboard Portfolio Projects (×3) | 45% |
| Data Story Presentation | 20% |
| Midterm Examination | 15% |
| Weekly Design Critiques & Participation | 10% |
| Final Capstone Dashboard | 10% |

**Dashboard Portfolio Projects:** Students will complete three scaffolded project assignments throughout the semester, each requiring the construction of a fully functional Tableau dashboard built from provided or independently sourced datasets. Projects increase in scope and complexity, culminating in multi-sheet, interactive dashboards with documented design rationale.

**Data Story Presentation:** Working individually or in pairs (as assigned), students will deliver a 12–15 minute live presentation using a Tableau Story, communicating a data-driven argument or analytical finding to a simulated professional audience. Presentations will be evaluated on narrative clarity, visual design, and the effectiveness of the Q&A response.

**Midterm Examination:** A written and practical examination assessing students' understanding of visualization theory, Tableau functionality, and dashboard design principles. The practical component will require students to build or modify a dashboard within a timed environment.

**Weekly Design Critiques & Participation:** Students will engage in structured peer critique sessions in which published visualizations—both student-produced and professionally sourced—are analyzed and discussed against course frameworks. Active, substantive participation is expected and assessed.

**Final Capstone Dashboard:** A culminating individual project in which students identify an original analytical question, source or compile a relevant dataset, and produce a polished, publication-ready Tableau dashboard accompanied by a written reflective report (1,500–2,000 words) addressing design decisions, data limitations, and audience considerations.
