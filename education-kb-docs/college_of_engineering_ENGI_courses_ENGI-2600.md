### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the principles of engineering dynamics, covering the motion of particles and rigid bodies under the influence of forces and moments. Students will develop foundational competency in kinematics and kinetics, applying Newton's laws, energy methods, and momentum principles to real-world mechanical systems. Emphasis is placed on developing systematic problem-solving strategies and translating physical scenarios into precise mathematical models.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyse the kinematics of particles and rigid bodies in two and three dimensions, including rectilinear, curvilinear, and rotational motion.
- Apply Newton's second law and the equations of motion to solve kinetics problems involving particles, systems of particles, and rigid bodies.
- Utilise work-energy and impulse-momentum methods to evaluate the dynamic behaviour of mechanical systems, including collision and impact scenarios.
- Model and analyse planar motion of rigid bodies, incorporating both translational and rotational degrees of freedom.
- Formulate and solve engineering dynamics problems using free-body diagrams, kinematic diagrams, and computational tools with professional rigour.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolment:

- **ENGI-1200: Engineering Statics** — foundational understanding of forces, equilibrium, and free-body diagrams is assumed throughout this course.
- **MATH-1310: Calculus II** — proficiency in differential and integral calculus, including basic ordinary differential equations, is essential for kinematic and kinetic analysis.
- **MATH-1420: Introduction to Linear Algebra** *(recommended)* — familiarity with vectors and matrix operations is strongly advised.

Students who have not satisfied the listed prerequisites must obtain written permission from the Department of Engineering before registering.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through a combination of the following components:

| Assessment | Weighting |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination I | 20% |
| Midterm Examination II | 20% |
| Final Examination | 30% |
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 20% |
| Laboratory / Computational Project | 10% |

**Problem Sets:** Six graded assignments will be distributed throughout the term, each requiring students to solve multi-part dynamics problems drawn from lecture material. Collaboration is permitted on a conceptual level; all submitted work must be independently written.

**Laboratory / Computational Project:** Students will complete one applied project in which a dynamic system — such as a vehicle suspension, projectile mechanism, or rotating linkage — is analysed using engineering software. A written technical report conforming to departmental style guidelines is required.

**Examinations:** All examinations are closed-book; one double-sided formula reference sheet (student-prepared) is permitted. Examinations assess both analytical problem-solving and conceptual understanding.

### Required Materials

Students are required to obtain the following materials before the first week of class:

- **Kopman, R-R. & Runningen, E. (Eds.).** *Foundations of Engineering Dynamics: Particles, Rigid Bodies, and Beyond* (4th ed.). Peculiar University Press. — This is the primary course text and is referenced directly in lectures and assignments.
- **Greenbank, L.** *Applied Kinetics: A Problem-Solving Workbook for Engineering Students* (2nd ed.). Crestline Academic Publishing. — A supplementary workbook providing structured practice problems with worked solutions.
- **Mahl, H. & Mai, R-I.** *Dynamics Computation Guide: MATLAB Applications for Mechanical Systems* (1st ed.). Ironbridge Technical Publishers. — Required for the computational project component; covers relevant MATLAB workflows and simulation techniques.

Access to **MATLAB** (student licence) is required and is available at no cost through the Peculiar University Software Portal. Graph paper, engineering paper, and a scientific calculator approved by the Department of Engineering are also required for examinations.
