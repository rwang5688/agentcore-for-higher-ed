### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the fundamental principles and methodologies underlying mathematical modeling and computer-based simulation as applied to engineering systems. Students will explore a range of deterministic and stochastic modeling techniques, with emphasis on translating real-world physical and engineering problems into tractable computational frameworks. Practical laboratory components reinforce theoretical concepts through hands-on implementation of simulation tools and analysis of system behavior under varying conditions.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Construct and validate mathematical models representing physical, mechanical, and systems-level engineering phenomena using both deterministic and probabilistic approaches.
- Design, implement, and execute discrete-event and continuous-time simulations using industry-standard software environments.
- Apply statistical analysis techniques to interpret simulation outputs, assess model accuracy, and quantify uncertainty in system predictions.
- Critically evaluate the assumptions, limitations, and appropriate scope of application for a given modeling or simulation methodology.
- Communicate modeling approaches and simulation results clearly and professionally through written technical reports and oral presentations.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-5400 must have successfully completed the following, or obtained instructor permission:

- **ENGI-3100: Numerical Methods for Engineers** — or equivalent coursework demonstrating proficiency in numerical analysis and computational techniques
- **MATH-3210: Differential Equations and Linear Algebra** — providing foundational mathematical tools essential for dynamic system modeling
- **STAT-2850: Probability and Statistics for Engineers** — establishing the statistical background required for stochastic simulation and output analysis
- **ENGI-2200: Introduction to Programming for Engineers** — or demonstrated competency in at least one high-level programming language (e.g., Python, MATLAB, or C++)

Students who have not completed all listed prerequisites are encouraged to consult with the course coordinator prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written examination covering theoretical foundations of modeling methodologies and analytical simulation concepts |
| Final Examination | 25% | Comprehensive written examination assessing mastery of all course content, including model formulation, simulation design, and result interpretation |
| Laboratory Assignments (×5) | 25% | Structured computational exercises requiring students to build, run, and analyze simulations using designated software tools; submitted individually |

*Note: A minimum passing grade of 50% must be achieved on the combined examination components in order to pass the course, regardless of overall weighted average.*
