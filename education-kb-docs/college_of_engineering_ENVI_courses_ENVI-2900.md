## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides an advanced interdisciplinary examination of the legal frameworks, regulatory structures, and policy mechanisms governing renewable energy systems and alternative fuel technologies. Students will critically analyse international, federal, and sub-national energy law as it pertains to solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, and emerging fuel alternatives, with particular attention to the intersection of environmental regulation and energy transition policy. The course draws on comparative legal analysis and real-world policy case studies to equip students with the tools necessary to evaluate and contribute to contemporary energy governance debates.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the principal legal instruments and regulatory frameworks governing renewable energy development and alternative fuel deployment at domestic and international levels
- Critically evaluate the policy trade-offs inherent in energy transition initiatives, including economic, environmental justice, and geopolitical considerations
- Analyse landmark legislation, administrative rulings, and treaty obligations shaping the renewable energy sector, applying relevant interpretive and comparative legal methodologies
- Assess the role of governmental, intergovernmental, and non-governmental actors in shaping and enforcing energy law and policy
- Construct well-reasoned, evidence-based legal and policy arguments addressing current challenges in renewable energy regulation, including grid integration, permitting reform, and fuel standard compliance

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolment:

- **ENVI-1100: Foundations of Environmental Law and Policy** or equivalent
- **ENVI-1250: Energy Systems and Sustainability** or equivalent
- **POLS-1080: Introduction to Public Policy and Governance** or equivalent (may be taken concurrently with instructor approval)

Students are also expected to possess a working knowledge of statutory interpretation and basic regulatory theory. Students without the listed prerequisites who believe they have equivalent preparation should consult with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources to seek a prerequisite waiver.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Closed-book examination testing statutory knowledge, case law, and regulatory frameworks covered in the first half of the course |
| Policy Brief | 25% | A 2,000–2,500 word professional policy brief addressing a current regulatory challenge in the renewable energy sector, prepared individually and submitted at Week 8 |
| Comparative Legal Analysis Paper | 30% | A 3,500–4,500 word research paper comparing the renewable energy or alternative fuels regulatory regimes of two or more jurisdictions, submitted at Week 13 |
| Group Regulatory Simulation | 15% | Teams of three to four students will simulate a regulatory hearing or legislative drafting exercise, presenting findings and fielding questions from the instructional panel |
| Participation and Reading Responses | 10% | Regular contributions to seminar discussion, supported by three short (400–600 word) critical reading responses submitted throughout the semester |

All written submissions must conform to the Peculiar University Academic Writing Standards and must employ a recognised legal citation format (e.g., OSCOLA or Bluebook) as directed by the supervising instructor for the relevant module.
