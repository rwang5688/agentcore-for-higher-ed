This course provides a rigorous introduction to statistical theory and applied data analysis using the R programming language, equipping students with the quantitative tools necessary for evidence-based decision making in data science contexts. Students will explore foundational and intermediate statistical concepts—including probability distributions, hypothesis testing, regression modelling, and data visualisation—implemented directly through hands-on programming exercises in R and RStudio. Emphasis is placed on the translation of real-world data problems into appropriate statistical frameworks, fostering both analytical fluency and practical computational skill.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a working knowledge of core statistical concepts, including descriptive statistics, probability theory, inferential testing, and common parametric and non-parametric methods
- Write, debug, and interpret R scripts and RMarkdown documents to perform statistical analyses, manipulate datasets, and produce publication-quality visualisations
- Select and justify appropriate statistical models and tests for a given dataset and research question, critically evaluating assumptions and limitations
- Communicate statistical findings clearly and accurately through written reports and visual outputs, tailored to both technical and non-technical audiences
- Apply regression and correlation techniques—including simple, multiple, and logistic regression—to analyse relationships within structured datasets

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following prior to enrolment:

- **DATA-2200: Foundations of Data Science** or equivalent, with a minimum grade of C+
- **MATH-2150: Probability and Statistical Reasoning** or equivalent
- Basic familiarity with at least one programming or scripting language is strongly recommended; students with no prior coding experience are advised to complete the self-paced R orientation module available through the College of Engineering learning portal before the first session

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly Lab Assignments | 25% | Ten graded lab exercises completed in RStudio, submitted as RMarkdown documents; assessed on correctness, code quality, and interpretation |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | In-class written and computational exam covering statistical theory and applied R through Module 4 |
| Statistical Analysis Project | 30% | Individual or paired project requiring students to source or obtain a real-world dataset, conduct a full statistical analysis in R, and submit both a technical report and a 10-minute presentation |
| Final Examination | 20% | Comprehensive written examination; students may bring one double-sided handwritten formula sheet |
| Participation and Peer Review | 5% | Active engagement in lab sessions and constructive written peer review of one classmate's project draft |

Late submissions will be penalised at 10% per calendar day unless an extension has been agreed in writing with the supervising instructor prior to the deadline.
