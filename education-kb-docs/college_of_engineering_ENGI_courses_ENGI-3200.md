## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-3200: Fields and Waves provides a rigorous introduction to the fundamental principles governing electromagnetic fields and wave propagation, with emphasis on their applications in engineering systems. Students will develop a thorough understanding of Maxwell's equations, boundary conditions, and the behavior of electric and magnetic fields in various media. The course bridges theoretical electromagnetics with practical engineering applications, including transmission lines, waveguides, and antenna fundamentals.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply Maxwell's equations in both integral and differential form to analyze static and time-varying electromagnetic fields in free space and material media
- Evaluate the behavior of electric and magnetic fields at material boundaries using appropriate boundary conditions and constitutive relations
- Analyze the propagation, reflection, and transmission of plane electromagnetic waves through various media, including lossy and lossless dielectrics and conductors
- Model and solve transmission line problems using the telegrapher's equations, Smith charts, and impedance matching techniques
- Demonstrate proficiency in applying vector calculus and phasor analysis to solve complex field and wave engineering problems

**Examinations:** Three closed-book examinations (one formula sheet permitted) assessing analytical problem-solving across all major course topics. Examinations are administered in-person under timed conditions.

**Problem Sets:** Weekly or biweekly written assignments requiring derivation, numerical solution, and physical interpretation of fields and wave phenomena. Late submissions are subject to a penalty of 10% per calendar day.

**Laboratory / Simulation Project:** A culminating individual or paired project utilizing industry-standard electromagnetic simulation software (e.g., ANSYS HFSS or equivalent) to model a practical device such as a transmission line junction, waveguide section, or simple patch antenna. A written technical report and brief oral presentation are required for full credit.
