### Course Description

Technical Support Fundamentals provides an advanced examination of the theoretical frameworks, methodologies, and professional practices underlying enterprise-level technical support operations, with particular emphasis on systems troubleshooting, incident management, and service delivery within complex organizational environments. Students will engage critically with the full lifecycle of technical support processes, from initial fault diagnosis through escalation protocols and post-resolution analysis, drawing upon current industry standards and best practices. This course also explores the intersection of human factors, organizational behavior, and technical infrastructure as they relate to the design and optimization of support systems at scale.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of COMP-16800, students will be able to:

- **Analyze and apply** advanced diagnostic and troubleshooting methodologies to resolve complex hardware, software, and network-related incidents in enterprise environments, demonstrating systematic and evidence-based reasoning throughout the support lifecycle.
- **Evaluate and implement** industry-standard incident management frameworks, including ITIL-aligned processes, to design, assess, and continuously improve technical support workflows and service desk operations.
- **Design and critically assess** escalation pathways, knowledge base architectures, and documentation strategies that enhance organizational support capacity and reduce mean time to resolution (MTTR) across distributed technical teams.
- **Integrate principles** from human-computer interaction, organizational communication, and customer experience theory to develop support strategies that address both technical and interpersonal dimensions of end-user service delivery.
- **Demonstrate professional and ethical competency** in the handling of sensitive user data, security incidents, and compliance-sensitive support scenarios, in alignment with current regulatory standards and institutional policy frameworks.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-16800 must have completed the following, or obtained written departmental consent prior to registration:

- **COMP-55200** – Systems Administration and Infrastructure Management, or equivalent
- **COMP-54100** – Networking Principles and Protocols
- **COMP-53800** – Operating Systems: Architecture and Management
- Completion of a minimum of **60 credit points** at the 400-level or above within the Computer Science program, or a closely related discipline as approved by the Department of Computer Science
- Demonstrated proficiency in at least one enterprise operating system environment (Windows Server, Linux/Unix, or macOS administration); proficiency may be established through prior coursework, professional certification documentation, or departmental skills assessment

*Note: Students with significant relevant professional experience in technical support or IT service management roles may petition the Department for prerequisite waiver consideration. Such petitions must be submitted no later than two weeks prior to the commencement of the term.*

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-16800 will be evaluated through a combination of theoretical and applied assessment instruments, designed to reflect both the academic rigor and the practical demands of the discipline:

- **Diagnostic Case Study Analysis (20%)** – Students will complete two written case study analyses over the course of the term, each requiring the systematic diagnosis and resolution of a complex, multi-layered technical support scenario. Each submission should demonstrate mastery of diagnostic frameworks, documentation standards, and escalation decision-making. Cases will be distributed in Weeks 4 and 9; submissions are due two weeks after distribution. *(Individual; approximately 1,500–2,000 words each)*

- **Service Desk Process Design Project (25%)** – Working in assigned groups of three to four, students will develop a comprehensive technical support process design proposal for a fictional mid-to-large enterprise organization. The project deliverable must include a fully documented incident management workflow, a knowledge base structure plan, and a staffing and escalation model, supported by reference to relevant theoretical literature and industry standards.
