## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to neural networks and their applications in modern machine learning systems. Students will explore the theoretical foundations of artificial neural networks, including feedforward architectures, backpropagation, and optimization techniques, progressing through advanced topics such as convolutional neural networks, recurrent architectures, and regularization strategies. Emphasis is placed on both mathematical rigor and practical implementation, equipping students with the skills to design, train, and evaluate neural network models for real-world problem domains.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the mathematical foundations of neural network architectures, including activation functions, loss functions, gradient descent, and the backpropagation algorithm
- Design and implement feedforward, convolutional, and recurrent neural networks using contemporary deep learning frameworks
- Apply appropriate regularization, optimization, and hyperparameter tuning strategies to improve model performance and generalization
- Critically evaluate the strengths and limitations of various neural network architectures in the context of classification, regression, and sequence modeling tasks
- Communicate technical findings effectively through written reports and reproducible code, demonstrating an understanding of responsible and ethical AI development

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-11200: Introduction to Programming and Data Structures** — foundational programming competency required
- **COMP-12450: Principles of Machine Learning** — familiarity with supervised and unsupervised learning paradigms
- **MATH-11300: Linear Algebra for Computing** — essential for understanding matrix operations underlying neural computations
- **MATH-11500: Probability and Statistics for Engineers** — required for understanding probabilistic models and loss function theory

Students without these prerequisites must obtain written instructor approval before registering.

Programming assignments must be submitted via the course platform as documented Jupyter notebooks. All submitted work must adhere to the Peculiar University Academic Integrity Policy. Late submissions will be penalized at a rate of 10% per calendar day unless an extension is approved in advance.

### Required Materials

Students are expected to obtain the following materials before the first week of instruction:

- **Vanthorpe, C. & Delacroix, S. (2021).** *Deep Learning Architectures: Principles and Practice* (3rd ed.). Harwick Academic Press. — Primary course textbook; covers all major topics from perceptrons through transformer-based models
- **Orimoto, K. & Fjelstad, P. (2020).** *Applied Neural Computation: A Hands-On Approach with Python* (2nd ed.). Meridian Computing Press. — Practical companion text used throughout lab sessions and programming assignments
- **Rashidova, N. (2022).** *Mathematics for Deep Learning: A Concise Review*. Saltbrook University Press. — Recommended supplementary reference for students seeking additional mathematical support

Access to a GPU-enabled computing environment is strongly recommended for completing programming assignments efficiently. The university's **Peculiar U High-Performance Computing Cluster (PU-HPC)** is available to enrolled students at no additional cost; setup instructions are distributed during the first lab session. All software used in this course is open-source and freely available.
