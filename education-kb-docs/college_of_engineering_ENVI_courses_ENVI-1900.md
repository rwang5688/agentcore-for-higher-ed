## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides students with a comprehensive foundation in the principles and processes that govern Earth's natural systems, including atmospheric, hydrological, terrestrial, and biological cycles. Students will examine the interactions between human activity and environmental systems, exploring issues such as climate change, resource depletion, pollution, and biodiversity loss within scientific, social, and policy frameworks. Emphasis is placed on developing critical thinking skills necessary for analysing contemporary environmental challenges and contributing to evidence-based solutions.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and explain the fundamental physical, chemical, and biological processes that sustain Earth's major environmental systems.
- Analyse the causes, mechanisms, and consequences of key environmental problems, including anthropogenic climate change, habitat fragmentation, and water scarcity.
- Apply quantitative and qualitative methods to evaluate environmental data, trends, and risk assessments.
- Critically assess the role of policy, technology, and human behaviour in shaping environmental outcomes at local, regional, and global scales.
- Communicate scientific findings clearly and responsibly to both specialist and non-specialist audiences, demonstrating awareness of ethical considerations in environmental decision-making.

---

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (Level 735), students are expected to have completed the following or equivalent:

- **ENVI-3100** Foundations of Ecological Systems or **BIOL-3200** Principles of Ecology
- **CHEM-3050** Environmental Chemistry I
- **MATH-2800** Statistics for Natural Sciences or equivalent quantitative methods coursework
- Demonstrated competency in scientific writing at the undergraduate level

Students lacking formal prerequisites may seek written permission from the course coordinator or departmental advisor prior to enrolment. Relevant professional or research experience may be considered in lieu of specific prerequisite courses.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Closed-book written examination covering core concepts from Weeks 1–6 |
| Research Paper | 30% | A 3,500–4,500 word original analytical paper on a chosen environmental issue, submitted Week 11 |
| Group Policy Brief | 20% | Collaborative project requiring teams to prepare a professional policy brief addressing a real-world environmental challenge, presented in Week 13 |
| Laboratory & Field Reports | 20% | Three structured reports based on scheduled laboratory sessions and one mandatory field excursion |
| Participation & Seminars | 10% | Active contribution to weekly seminar discussions, peer critique sessions, and in-class case studies |

A minimum grade of 50% must be achieved in the final examination or research paper to pass the course. Late submissions will be penalised at 5% per calendar day unless an extension is formally granted.
