### Course Description

This course introduces students to Blender, an industry-standard open-source 3D creation suite, with a focus on foundational skills in modelling, texturing, lighting, and rendering within a computer science context. Students will explore the full 3D design pipeline — from conceptual mesh construction through to final rendered output — while developing an appreciation for the mathematical and computational principles that underpin three-dimensional graphics. Emphasis is placed on practical, project-driven learning that equips students with transferable skills applicable to fields such as game development, simulation, visualisation, and digital media.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate proficiency in Blender's core interface and toolset, including object and edit mode operations, modifiers, and scene management
- Construct and refine three-dimensional models using standard polygon modelling techniques, including extrusion, loop cutting, subdivision, and boolean operations
- Apply materials, UV unwrapping, and texture mapping to 3D objects to achieve realistic or stylised surface appearances
- Configure lighting rigs and camera settings and produce high-quality renders using both Cycles and EEVEE rendering engines
- Develop and present a complete 3D design project that demonstrates an integrated understanding of the design pipeline and creative problem-solving within computational constraints

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-2500 must have successfully completed the following, or obtain instructor permission:

- **COMP-1100: Introduction to Computing and Digital Environments** — foundational understanding of file systems, software interfaces, and digital workflows
- **MATH-1210: Applied Mathematics for Computer Science** — basic proficiency in vectors, coordinate geometry, and spatial reasoning is strongly recommended
- Students with prior experience in any 2D digital design or image editing application are encouraged to enrol but this is not formally required

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly Lab Exercises | 20% | Short, structured exercises submitted each week reinforcing tools and techniques covered in lectures |
| Midterm Practical Examination | 20% | Timed, supervised in-lab assessment requiring students to produce a specified 3D scene under defined constraints |
| Project 1 — Object Modelling Portfolio | 20% | A curated portfolio of five modelled and textured objects, submitted with written reflections on design decisions |
| Project 2 — Final Scene Design | 30% | A fully realised, rendered 3D scene incorporating original modelling, texturing, lighting, and composition, accompanied by a short design rationale document (500–700 words) |
| Peer Review Participation | 10% | Structured written peer critique of classmates' Project 1 submissions, assessed for quality of constructive feedback |

*All project work must be submitted as both the native Blender (.blend) file and rendered output files in the specified format. Late submissions will be subject to the standard Peculiar U penalty schedule as outlined in the Student Academic Policy.*
