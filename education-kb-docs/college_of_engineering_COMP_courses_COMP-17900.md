### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the principles and techniques underpinning vision-based autonomous navigation for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and other flying robotic platforms. Students will explore the intersection of computer vision, robotics, and control theory, examining how aerial robots perceive, interpret, and respond to their environments using optical sensor data. Emphasis is placed on both theoretical foundations and practical implementation, equipping students with the skills necessary to design and evaluate visual navigation pipelines for real-world flight systems.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the fundamental principles of image formation, camera geometry, and optical flow as they apply to aerial navigation systems
- Implement and evaluate core visual odometry and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) algorithms in simulated and physical UAV environments
- Analyze the performance trade-offs between different visual sensing modalities, including monocular, stereo, and RGB-D systems, in the context of flight constraints such as weight, power, and latency
- Design and integrate visual perception pipelines with flight control systems, including the application of filtering techniques such as extended Kalman filters for state estimation
- Critically assess current literature in the field of visual navigation for flying robots and identify open research challenges and emerging directions

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-17900 should have successfully completed the following, or obtained explicit instructor approval:

- **COMP-11400: Introduction to Robotics and Autonomous Systems** or equivalent
- **COMP-13250: Computer Vision Fundamentals**
- **MATH-12100: Linear Algebra and Its Applications**
- **MATH-13050: Probability and Statistical Methods for Engineers**

A working knowledge of at least one programming language commonly used in robotics (Python or C++ strongly recommended) is expected. Prior experience with ROS (Robot Operating System) is advantageous but not required.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written in-class exam covering theoretical foundations from the first half of the course, including camera models, optical flow, and visual odometry |
| Final Examination | 25% | Comprehensive written examination covering all course material, with emphasis on integration of concepts across the full navigation pipeline |
| Programming Assignments (×4) | 30% | Four structured coding assignments requiring students to implement visual navigation algorithms in simulation, including feature tracking, depth estimation, and SLAM |
| Group Design Project | 20% | Teams of 3–4 students will develop and demonstrate a complete visual navigation solution for a simulated or physical UAV platform, culminating in a live demonstration and technical report |
| Participation & Quizzes | 5% | In-class engagement and periodic short quizzes assessing comprehension of assigned readings and lecture content |

*Note: The group design project requires submission of both a written technical report (minimum 2,500 words) and a recorded or live system demonstration. Peer evaluation components are included in the project assessment.*
