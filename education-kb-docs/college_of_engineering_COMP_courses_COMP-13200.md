### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous foundation in the mathematical structures and techniques essential to the study and practice of computer science, with emphasis on discrete mathematics, logic, and algorithmic reasoning. Students will explore topics including propositional and predicate logic, set theory, combinatorics, graph theory, and introductory probability as they apply to computational systems and problem-solving. By the conclusion of the course, students will be equipped with the formal mathematical language and reasoning skills necessary to succeed in advanced coursework in algorithms, data structures, and theoretical computer science.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of COMP-13200, students will be able to:

- Construct and evaluate formal logical arguments using propositional and predicate logic, including proof techniques such as direct proof, proof by contradiction, and mathematical induction.
- Apply set theory, relations, and functions to model and analyze computational problems and data structures.
- Utilize combinatorial principles and counting techniques, including permutations, combinations, and the pigeonhole principle, to assess algorithmic complexity and problem feasibility.
- Analyze graphs and trees as mathematical objects, applying relevant theorems to problems in network design, traversal, and optimization.
- Apply basic probability theory and discrete distributions to reason about randomized algorithms and computational outcomes.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-13200 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment | Weight |
|---|---|
| Homework Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 25% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Logic and Proof Written Assignment | 15% |
| Graph Theory Applied Project | 20% |
| Final Examination | 20% |

**Homework Problem Sets:** Six graded problem sets distributed throughout the semester, designed to reinforce lecture material and develop independent problem-solving skills. Collaboration is permitted but all submitted work must be original.

**Midterm Examination:** A closed-book, in-person examination covering logic, proof techniques, set theory, and relations. Students are permitted one hand-written reference sheet.

**Logic and Proof Written Assignment:** A formal written assignment in which students construct and present original mathematical proofs, evaluated on correctness, clarity, and rigor.

**Graph Theory Applied Project:** A group or individual project requiring students to apply graph-theoretic concepts to a real-world or simulated computational scenario, submitted as a written report with supporting diagrams.

**Final Examination:** A comprehensive closed-book examination covering all course material, administered during the official examination period.
