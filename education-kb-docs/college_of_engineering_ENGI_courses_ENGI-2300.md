### Course Description

This course provides engineering students with a comprehensive introduction to the fundamental principles and practical applications of electronic circuits and systems as they relate to engineering practice. Students will develop hands-on proficiency in analysing, designing, and testing analogue and digital electronic circuits, with an emphasis on real-world engineering contexts. Topics covered include circuit theory, semiconductor devices, operational amplifiers, signal analysis, and introductory digital logic systems.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply fundamental circuit analysis techniques, including Kirchhoff's Voltage and Current Laws, Thévenin's theorem, and Norton's theorem, to solve both series and parallel circuit problems
- Identify and characterise the behaviour of core electronic components — including resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, and transistors — within engineering circuit applications
- Design and analyse basic analogue circuits, including amplifier and filter configurations using operational amplifiers
- Interpret and construct schematic diagrams, breadboard prototypes, and simulation models using industry-standard electronic design tools
- Evaluate circuit performance through laboratory measurement, data collection, and critical comparison against theoretical predictions

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following courses prior to enrolment:

- **ENGI-1100: Introduction to Engineering Principles** — foundational understanding of engineering systems and problem-solving methodology
- **MATH-1210: Calculus I** — proficiency in differential and integral calculus as applied to engineering problems
- **PHYS-1150: Physics for Engineers I** — grounding in classical mechanics and introductory electrostatics

Students who have not completed the above prerequisites must obtain written departmental approval before registering for this course.
