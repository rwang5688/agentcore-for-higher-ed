## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-4200: Image Processing provides a comprehensive introduction to the theoretical foundations and practical techniques used in the acquisition, transformation, and analysis of digital images. Students will explore core algorithms for image enhancement, restoration, segmentation, and compression, with emphasis on both spatial and frequency domain methods. The course integrates hands-on programming projects to develop proficiency in applying image processing techniques to real-world engineering problems.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the fundamental principles of digital image formation, sampling, and quantization, and apply these concepts to evaluate image quality and system design
- Implement and compare spatial and frequency domain techniques for image enhancement and noise reduction, including convolution, filtering, and Fourier-based methods
- Apply segmentation algorithms and feature extraction methods to isolate and identify meaningful structures within digital images
- Analyze and apply standard image compression techniques, including lossless and lossy schemes, with reference to industry standards and their trade-offs
- Design and evaluate complete image processing pipelines for practical engineering applications, critically assessing algorithm performance using appropriate quantitative metrics

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **ENGI-2100: Signals and Systems** – foundational understanding of signal theory, convolution, and frequency analysis
- **ENGI-2250: Introduction to Digital Systems** – basic knowledge of digital data representation and binary operations
- **MATH-2310: Linear Algebra for Engineers** – proficiency in matrix operations, vector spaces, and transformations
- **COMP-2050: Programming for Engineers** – working knowledge of a structured programming language (Python or MATLAB preferred)
- Students must be at academic level 3035 or above, or obtain written permission from the course instructor
