## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-17400 introduces students to the foundational principles of computer programming through the lens of program structure, abstraction, and interpretation. Students will explore core computational concepts including recursion, higher-order functions, data abstraction, and the design of interpreters, with an emphasis on developing rigorous, elegant solutions to complex problems. Drawing on functional and procedural programming paradigms, this course equips students with the analytical and technical skills necessary for advanced study in computer science.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate proficiency in applying recursive and iterative problem-solving strategies to design and implement correct, efficient programs
- Construct and utilize abstractions, including higher-order functions and abstract data types, to manage complexity in software systems
- Analyze the behavior of programs by developing and applying substitution and environment models of evaluation
- Design and implement basic interpreters, including parsing and evaluation components, for simple programming languages
- Apply principles of modularity and decomposition to produce well-structured, maintainable code across a variety of computational problems

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-10100: Introduction to Computational Thinking** or equivalent foundational computing experience
- **MATH-10200: Discrete Mathematics I** (may be taken concurrently with instructor approval)

Students who have not completed the above prerequisites but believe they have equivalent preparation are encouraged to consult with the course instructor or the Department of Computer Science undergraduate advisor before enrolling.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-17400 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Programming Assignments (6–8 over the semester) | 40% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Lab Participation and Weekly Problem Sets | 10% |
| Interpreter Design Project (final weeks) | 5% |

**Programming Assignments** are submitted electronically and evaluated on correctness, code style, and efficiency. Late submissions are subject to a penalty of 10% per day unless an extension is formally requested in advance.

**Examinations** are conducted in-person and are closed-book, though students are permitted one hand-written reference sheet (both sides, letter-sized) for each exam.

**Interpreter Design Project** requires students to collaboratively design and implement a minimal working interpreter for a simplified language specification provided by the instructor, demonstrating cumulative mastery of course material.
