### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous treatment of the mathematical foundations of information theory and thermodynamic entropy, exploring their deep theoretical connections and practical engineering applications. Students will investigate Shannon entropy, channel capacity, data compression, and the relationship between information-theoretic and physical entropy as formulated in statistical mechanics. Emphasis is placed on applying these concepts to the design and analysis of communication systems, coding schemes, and computational processes within modern engineering contexts.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Define and apply Shannon entropy, mutual information, and related information-theoretic measures to quantify uncertainty and information content in discrete and continuous systems
- Analyse the fundamental limits of data compression and channel transmission using source coding and channel coding theorems, including Shannon's landmark results
- Articulate the mathematical and conceptual relationship between thermodynamic entropy (Boltzmann–Gibbs formulation) and information entropy, and critically evaluate their implications for physical computing systems
- Design and evaluate lossless and lossy compression algorithms and basic error-correcting codes, assessing their efficiency relative to theoretical bounds
- Apply information-theoretic tools to contemporary engineering problems including signal processing, cryptographic systems, and the analysis of complex networked systems
