## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-11000: Introduction to Information Studies provides a foundational exploration of the principles, structures, and societal implications of information as a core subject within the computational and engineering sciences. Students will examine how information is created, organized, stored, retrieved, and communicated across a variety of digital and institutional contexts. This course situates information studies within the broader landscape of computer science, establishing conceptual frameworks that underpin advanced coursework in data systems, human-computer interaction, and information architecture.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Articulate core theoretical frameworks governing the nature, lifecycle, and classification of information in digital and analog environments
- Evaluate the ethical, legal, and social dimensions of information access, ownership, and dissemination in contemporary technological contexts
- Apply foundational principles of information organization and retrieval to the design and critique of basic information systems
- Analyze the relationships between users, information needs, and the systems developed to address those needs within engineering and computing disciplines
- Synthesize interdisciplinary perspectives drawn from computer science, library science, and cognitive studies to produce coherent analyses of information problems

---

### Prerequisites

Given the graduate-level designation of this course (Level 590), students are expected to have completed the following prior to enrollment:

- Completion of an undergraduate degree in Computer Science, Information Technology, Library and Information Science, or a closely related field
- COMP-30200: Foundations of Data and Systems Management, or equivalent coursework demonstrating familiarity with database concepts and data structures
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or written permission of the supervising instructor
- Demonstrated proficiency in academic writing at the post-baccalaureate level

Students who have not completed formal prerequisites but possess relevant professional experience in information management, systems design, or archival practice may petition the department for an enrollment waiver.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-11000 will be evaluated through a combination of the following assessment components:

- **Participation and Weekly Reading Responses (15%):** Students will submit brief written responses (300–500 words) to assigned readings prior to each seminar session. Active, substantive in-class participation is expected and factored into this component.
- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written examination covering theoretical frameworks, key vocabulary, and foundational concepts from the first half of the course. The exam will include short-answer and essay-format questions.
- **Annotated Bibliography (15%):** Students will compile a curated, critically annotated bibliography of twelve to fifteen scholarly sources on a self-selected information studies topic, demonstrating engagement with peer-reviewed literature.
- **Systems Analysis Paper (25%):** A research paper of 4,000–5,500 words in which students critically analyze an existing information system or platform, applying course frameworks to assess its organizational structure, user experience considerations, and broader sociotechnical implications.
- **Final Group Project and Presentation (25%):** Working in teams of three to four, students will design a conceptual framework for a novel information system addressing a defined institutional or community need. Teams will deliver a formal 20-minute presentation and submit a written proposal of 3,000–4,000 words.
