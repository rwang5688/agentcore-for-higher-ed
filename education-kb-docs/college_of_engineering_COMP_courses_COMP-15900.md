### Course Description

This advanced course provides a rigorous and comprehensive introduction to the theory, architecture, and practical implementation of quantum computing systems, bridging foundational principles of quantum mechanics with real-world computational problem-solving. Students will explore quantum algorithms, quantum circuit design, error correction, and the emerging landscape of quantum hardware platforms, with an emphasis on developing genuine hands-on proficiency using current quantum programming frameworks. The course title reflects its commitment to equipping only the most motivated students with the intellectual tools necessary to navigate one of the most demanding and consequential frontiers in modern computer science.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **Understand and apply core quantum mechanical principles** — including superposition, entanglement, and quantum interference — as they pertain to computational models and algorithm design.
- **Design and analyze quantum circuits** using standard gate sets, evaluate their complexity, and implement them using industry-standard quantum programming environments such as simulated or cloud-accessible quantum processors.
- **Implement and critically assess major quantum algorithms**, including Shor's algorithm, Grover's search algorithm, and variational quantum eigensolvers, evaluating their advantages and limitations relative to classical counterparts.
- **Apply quantum error correction techniques**, including stabilizer codes and fault-tolerant computation strategies, to mitigate the effects of decoherence and noise in realistic quantum systems.
- **Evaluate the current state and near-term trajectory of quantum computing hardware**, including superconducting qubits, trapped-ion systems, and photonic platforms, and articulate the key engineering challenges that remain unsolved.

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed all of the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-3210: Algorithm Design and Analysis** or equivalent
- **MATH-3450: Linear Algebra for Engineers** or equivalent (a strong working knowledge of vector spaces, matrix operations, and eigendecomposition is essential)
- **MATH-3620: Probability and Stochastic Processes**
- **PHYS-2300: Introduction to Modern Physics** or **COMP-4710: Theoretical Foundations of Computing** (either accepted)
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 3.0 is strongly recommended; instructor consent may be required for students who do not meet this threshold

*Note: This course moves at a substantial pace. Students who are not fully comfortable with complex-number arithmetic, Dirac notation, or computational complexity theory are strongly encouraged to complete supplemental preparation before the first week of instruction.*

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 total, bi-weekly) | 25% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Quantum Algorithm Implementation Project | 25% |
| Final Examination (comprehensive) | 20% |
| Research Paper & Presentation | 10% |

- **Problem Sets:** Six rigorous assignments distributed throughout the semester, covering theoretical derivations, circuit construction, and short programming exercises using quantum simulation tools.
- **Midterm Examination:** A closed-book written examination covering quantum mechanical foundations, qubit formalism, and foundational algorithms addressed in the first half of the course.
- **Quantum Algorithm Implementation Project:** A substantial individual or paired project in which students select, implement, document, and benchmark a quantum algorithm of approved complexity on a simulated or real quantum backend. A written technical report is required.
- **Final Examination:** A comprehensive closed-book examination covering the full scope of the course, with emphasis on synthesis and comparison of methods studied.
- **Research Paper & Presentation:** A 3,000–4,500 word research paper surveying a current open problem or recent breakthrough in quantum computing, accompanied by a 15-minute in-class presentation delivered during the final two weeks of the semester.
