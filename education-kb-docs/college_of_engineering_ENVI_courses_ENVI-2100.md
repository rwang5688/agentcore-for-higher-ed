### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the economic principles underlying environmental and natural resource management, with a strong emphasis on the design and evaluation of environmental policy instruments. Students will examine how markets succeed and fail in allocating environmental goods, and how regulatory frameworks, incentive-based mechanisms, and international agreements are developed in response to these challenges. The course integrates quantitative analysis with real-world policy case studies to develop both theoretical understanding and applied competency.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and critically analyse the causes of market failure as they relate to environmental externalities, public goods, and common-pool resources, applying relevant microeconomic theory
- Compare and evaluate policy instruments including Pigouvian taxes, cap-and-trade systems, subsidies, and direct regulation in terms of efficiency, equity, and enforceability
- Apply cost-benefit analysis and valuation methodologies — including contingent valuation and hedonic pricing — to assess environmental projects and policy proposals
- Interpret and critically engage with empirical evidence from environmental economics literature to inform evidence-based policy recommendations
- Demonstrate an understanding of the intersection between environmental economics and broader sustainability frameworks, including climate policy, biodiversity governance, and resource extraction regulation

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-2100 must have completed the following, or received written permission from the course coordinator:

- **ECON-1200** Principles of Microeconomics *or* **ECON-1150** Foundations of Economic Analysis
- **MATH-1100** Introductory Calculus and Quantitative Methods *or* equivalent demonstrated quantitative proficiency
- **ENVI-1000** Introduction to Environmental Systems is strongly recommended but not strictly required

Students should be comfortable with basic algebraic reasoning and graphical economic analysis prior to enrolment.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 25% | In-person, closed-book examination covering Weeks 1–6; includes short-answer and analytical problem-solving sections |
| Policy Analysis Paper | 30% | Individual written assignment (2,500–3,500 words) requiring students to evaluate a real or hypothetical environmental policy using course frameworks; submitted in Week 10 |
| Group Case Study Presentation | 20% | Teams of 3–4 students present a comparative analysis of environmental policy instruments applied to an assigned regional or sectoral context; presented in Weeks 11–12 |
| Final Examination | 25% | Cumulative, closed-book examination; includes multiple choice, short-answer, and one extended analytical response |

*Students must achieve a passing grade in both the final examination and the written policy analysis paper in order to pass the course overall.*
