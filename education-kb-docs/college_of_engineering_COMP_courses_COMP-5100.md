### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous examination of the principles, methodologies, and best practices underlying the design and implementation of modern database systems. Students will explore relational and non-relational database architectures, data modeling techniques, query optimization, transaction management, and the theoretical foundations of data integrity and concurrency control. Emphasis is placed on applying these concepts to real-world scenarios, equipping students with the technical proficiency and critical thinking skills necessary to design scalable, efficient, and secure database solutions in professional and research contexts.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Design and implement normalized relational database schemas using entity-relationship modeling and advanced normalization techniques, including Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF) and higher normal forms.
- Construct complex SQL queries and stored procedures, demonstrating proficiency in data manipulation, aggregation, and optimization strategies for high-performance database environments.
- Evaluate and apply appropriate NoSQL and distributed database paradigms—including document, key-value, columnar, and graph models—to address the demands of large-scale, data-intensive applications.
- Analyze and implement transaction management protocols, including ACID compliance, concurrency control mechanisms, and recovery strategies to ensure data consistency and fault tolerance.
- Critically assess security vulnerabilities in database systems and apply industry-standard techniques for access control, encryption, and the prevention of injection-based attacks.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Database Design Project (Three Phases) | 30% |
| Lab Assignments & Problem Sets (6 total) | 15% |
| Research Paper & Presentation | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** will assess theoretical knowledge, schema design problems, SQL query writing, and conceptual analysis of database architectures. Both examinations are closed-book unless otherwise specified by the instructor.

**Database Design Project** is a semester-long, multi-phase project in which students design, implement, and optimize a fully functional relational database system for a realistic use case of their choosing. Phase One covers conceptual modeling and ER diagrams; Phase Two covers schema implementation and query development; Phase Three covers performance tuning, security hardening, and final presentation. Projects may be completed individually or in pairs.

**Lab Assignments & Problem Sets** consist of structured programming and design exercises submitted through the course's designated learning management platform. These assignments reinforce lecture material and provide hands-on experience with industry-standard database tools and environments.

**Research Paper & Presentation** requires each student to investigate an emerging topic in database systems—such as distributed consistency models, time-series databases, or AI-driven query optimization—and deliver a formal written paper (2,500–3,500 words) accompanied by a 12–15 minute in-class presentation.
