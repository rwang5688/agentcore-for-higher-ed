## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-4100 Humanitarian Engineering equips students with the theoretical frameworks and practical tools necessary to apply engineering principles in service of underserved communities and global development challenges. Drawing on interdisciplinary perspectives from social science, public health, and sustainable development, students critically examine the role of engineers as agents of positive social change. Emphasis is placed on culturally responsive design, ethical decision-making, and the development of context-appropriate technological solutions for communities facing resource constraints.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Evaluate engineering projects through humanitarian, ethical, and sustainability lenses, accounting for the social, cultural, and environmental contexts of affected communities
- Apply participatory design methodologies to co-develop solutions with community stakeholders rather than imposing externally driven technical interventions
- Analyse case studies of humanitarian engineering initiatives to identify success factors, failures, and lessons applicable to future projects in low-resource settings
- Demonstrate competency in needs assessment, project scoping, and the iterative design process as applied to real-world humanitarian challenges
- Communicate engineering proposals effectively to both technical and non-technical audiences, including community partners, NGOs, and funding bodies

---

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolment:

- **ENGI-2200: Engineering Design and Systems Thinking** – foundational principles of design methodology and systems analysis
- **ENGI-2350: Applied Engineering Ethics** – introduction to ethical frameworks within engineering practice
- **SOCI-2100: Global Development and Society** *(recommended co-requisite)* – sociological perspectives on inequality, development, and community engagement

Students must also have achieved a minimum cumulative GPA of 2.5 and completed a minimum of 60 credit points. Department approval is required for students who do not meet all prerequisites.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Community Needs Assessment Report | 25% | A structured written report in which student teams conduct and document a needs assessment for a hypothetical or partner community, identifying key engineering challenges and proposing a project scope |
| Design Project and Prototype | 30% | A semester-long team project requiring students to design, prototype, and present a context-appropriate engineering solution addressing a humanitarian challenge identified in their needs assessment |
| Reflective Journal Portfolio | 15% | A series of ongoing written reflections submitted throughout the semester exploring students' evolving understanding of engineering ethics, positionality, and community-centred practice |
| Midterm Examination | 15% | A written examination covering course frameworks, key humanitarian engineering concepts, case study analysis, and ethical decision-making models |
| Final Presentation and Peer Review | 15% | A formal presentation of the design project to a panel including faculty and, where available, external community or NGO partners, followed by structured peer evaluation |

*Grading rubrics for all assessments are available on the course portal. All team project components include individual accountability measures to ensure equitable contribution.*
