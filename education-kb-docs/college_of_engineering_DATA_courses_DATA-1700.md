## Course Description

---

This course provides an advanced examination of the principles, techniques, and tools used to communicate complex data findings through effective visual representation. Students will explore both the theoretical underpinnings of human visual perception and the practical application of modern visualization frameworks to transform raw datasets into clear, compelling, and analytically rigorous graphics. Emphasis is placed on the critical evaluation of visualization design choices and their impact on interpretation, decision-making, and the communication of uncertainty within professional and research contexts.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Design and implement multi-layered, interactive data visualizations using industry-standard programming libraries and tools, applying advanced techniques for handling high-dimensional and time-series datasets
- Critically evaluate visualization outputs for accuracy, accessibility, and ethical representation, identifying common sources of visual bias and distortion in data communication
- Select appropriate visualization strategies for diverse data types — including geospatial, network, and hierarchical data — based on audience, medium, and analytical purpose
- Integrate principles of visual cognition and perceptual psychology to optimize the clarity and interpretability of graphical displays in technical and non-technical reporting contexts
- Develop and present a portfolio-quality data visualization project that demonstrates end-to-end proficiency, from data acquisition and wrangling through to final narrative-driven visual output

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment:

- **DATA-1400: Foundations of Data Science** or equivalent, with a minimum grade of B
- **DATA-1550: Statistical Methods for Data Analysis** or equivalent
- **COMP-1310: Intermediate Programming for Data Applications** or demonstrated proficiency in Python or R at an intermediate level
- Graduate standing (Level 500 or above) or written permission from the course coordinator

Students without the programming prerequisite are strongly encouraged to complete the department's self-directed programming review modules before the first week of instruction.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Visualization Portfolio (cumulative, 4 submissions) | 35% |
| Midterm Examination (written + applied) | 20% |
| Group Research Project & Presentation | 25% |
| Weekly Lab Exercises | 10% |
| Critical Review Paper (1,800–2,200 words) | 10% |

**Visualization Portfolio:** Students will develop and iteratively refine a portfolio of four visualization projects throughout the semester, each targeting a different data domain. Submissions are evaluated on technical execution, design rationale, and quality of written reflection.

**Group Research Project:** Teams of three to four students will identify a real-world dataset of sufficient complexity, perform exploratory and confirmatory analysis, and produce an interactive visual narrative to be presented to the class during the final two weeks of the semester.

**Critical Review Paper:** Students will author an individual analytical paper evaluating a published data visualization — from journalism, scientific literature, or policy reporting — assessing its methodological soundness, perceptual effectiveness, and ethical implications.
