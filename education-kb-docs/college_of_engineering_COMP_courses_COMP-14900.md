## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-14900: Programming Languages Part B is an advanced continuation of the Programming Languages sequence, providing students with an in-depth exploration of language design paradigms, type systems, and formal semantics. Students will examine the theoretical and practical underpinnings of functional, logic, and concurrent programming languages, with emphasis on how language features influence software correctness, expressiveness, and performance. This course bridges foundational theory with applied implementation, equipping students to critically evaluate and leverage diverse programming language ecosystems in professional and research contexts.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and compare advanced programming language paradigms, including functional, logic-based, and concurrent models, evaluating their suitability for various computational problems
- Demonstrate proficiency in formal language semantics, including operational, denotational, and axiomatic approaches, and apply these frameworks to reason about program correctness
- Design and implement interpreters or compilers for small domain-specific or pedagogical languages, incorporating lexical analysis, parsing, and semantic evaluation stages
- Evaluate advanced type systems — including polymorphic, dependent, and gradual typing — and apply type-theoretic reasoning to program verification and language design decisions
- Synthesize knowledge of language runtime environments, memory management strategies, and concurrency models to assess their practical implications in real-world language implementations

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through a combination of theoretical and applied assessments:

- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written examination covering formal semantics, type systems, and language paradigms addressed in the first half of the course. Includes both conceptual short-answer questions and formal proof exercises.
- **Final Examination (25%):** A comprehensive written examination spanning all course material, with emphasis on synthesis and comparative analysis of programming language concepts.
- **Programming Assignments (30%):** A series of four to five incremental implementation assignments requiring students to build and extend components of an interpreter or compiler, including a lexer, parser, type-checker, and evaluator. Assignments are completed individually unless otherwise specified.
- **Research Paper (15%):** A formal academic paper (2,500–3,500 words) in which students investigate a contemporary or historical programming language, analyzing its design philosophy, type system, and semantic model in relation to course themes. Proper citation and adherence to departmental style guidelines are required.
- **Participation and In-Class Exercises (10%):** Active engagement in lecture discussions, structured in-class problem-solving sessions, and periodic unannounced quizzes assessing comprehension of assigned readings.

Late submissions are subject to a penalty of 10% per calendar day unless a prior extension has been approved in writing by the supervising instructor.
