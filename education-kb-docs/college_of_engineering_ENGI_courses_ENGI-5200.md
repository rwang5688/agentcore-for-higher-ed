This course provides a rigorous introduction to the mathematical foundations and engineering applications of linear dynamical systems, with emphasis on state-space representations, system stability, and input-output behavior in both continuous and discrete time domains. Students will develop analytical and computational tools for modelling, analysing, and designing feedback control systems encountered across mechanical, electrical, and interdisciplinary engineering contexts. The course bridges foundational mathematical theory with practical engineering problem-solving, preparing students for advanced study in control systems, signal processing, and systems engineering.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Formulate and analyse linear time-invariant (LTI) systems using state-space representations, transfer functions, and matrix exponential methods
- Apply eigenvalue analysis, Lyapunov stability theory, and related techniques to determine the stability properties of linear dynamical systems
- Evaluate controllability and observability of state-space systems and design state feedback controllers and observers accordingly
- Solve system response problems in both the time domain and frequency domain using Laplace transforms and Fourier analysis
- Apply computational tools and simulation environments to model, simulate, and validate the behaviour of linear dynamical systems in engineering applications

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations are closed-book and will assess students' ability to apply analytical techniques covered in the course, including stability analysis, state-space formulation, and system response calculations. A formula sheet approved by the course coordinator will be provided.

**Problem Sets:** Regularly assigned problem sets will reinforce lecture material and develop students' facility with both theoretical derivations and numerical problem-solving. Collaboration is permitted for discussion purposes; however, all submitted work must be independently written.

**Computational Project:** Students will undertake an individual or paired computational project requiring the modelling and analysis of a linear dynamical system drawn from a chosen engineering application area. A written technical report and brief oral presentation are required. Full project guidelines are distributed in Week 3.

**Participation and Quizzes:** Short in-class quizzes administered periodically throughout the semester will assess ongoing comprehension of lecture material. Active participation in discussions and laboratory sessions will also contribute to this component.
