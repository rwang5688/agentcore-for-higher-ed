### Course Description

This advanced course examines the intersection of economic principles and environmental management, equipping students with the analytical tools necessary to evaluate the costs, benefits, and trade-offs inherent in environmental and natural resource policy decisions. Students will critically explore market failures, externalities, and regulatory frameworks as they apply to contemporary environmental challenges including climate change, biodiversity loss, and resource depletion. Drawing on interdisciplinary perspectives, the course bridges engineering, economic, and policy domains to prepare students for professional engagement with complex environmental governance problems.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **Apply core economic concepts** — including cost-benefit analysis, externalities, public goods theory, and market-based instruments — to real-world environmental and natural resource management scenarios.
- **Critically evaluate environmental policy instruments**, distinguishing between command-and-control regulations, carbon pricing mechanisms, tradeable permit systems, and voluntary agreements in terms of efficiency, equity, and enforceability.
- **Analyse the role of institutions and governance structures** in shaping environmental outcomes at local, national, and international scales, with particular attention to stakeholder dynamics and political economy.
- **Construct and defend evidence-based policy recommendations** using quantitative and qualitative methodologies, integrating engineering feasibility considerations with economic and social impact assessments.
- **Engage with current literature and emerging debates** in environmental economics, including discussions of ecological economics, green growth, degrowth, and the valuation of ecosystem services.

Students without a formal economics background are advised to consult with one of the course instructors during the enrolment period to assess readiness.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Policy Brief (Individual) | 20% | A 2,000-word written policy brief addressing a current environmental issue, demonstrating command of course concepts and policy analysis skills. Due Week 6. |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book in-class examination covering Weeks 1–7 material, including short-answer and extended response questions. |
| Group Policy Simulation | 25% | Teams of 4–5 students participate in a structured negotiation exercise simulating an international or regional environmental policy negotiation. Includes a written group report (2,500 words) and a 20-minute in-class presentation. |
| Research Paper (Individual) | 25% | A 4,000–5,000 word original research paper applying economic analysis to a self-selected environmental policy question. Must include quantitative or formal modelling components where appropriate. |
| Participation & Weekly Seminars | 10% | Active, informed engagement in weekly seminar discussions, including two short reading response submissions (400 words each). |

All written submissions must adhere to the Peculiar U Academic Integrity Policy. Late submissions will incur a 5% penalty per calendar day unless an extension has been formally approved.
