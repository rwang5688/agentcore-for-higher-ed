## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-0200: Advanced Visualization provides students with a comprehensive foundation in engineering visualization techniques, including the creation, interpretation, and communication of complex technical drawings, 3D models, and spatial representations used in modern engineering practice. Students will develop proficiency in both manual drafting principles and industry-standard computer-aided design (CAD) software, with an emphasis on translating abstract engineering concepts into precise visual formats. This course bridges theoretical geometric principles with practical application, preparing students for the visualization demands encountered across all major engineering disciplines.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Produce accurate multi-view orthographic projections, sectional views, and auxiliary views conforming to current ANSI/ISO drafting standards
- Construct and manipulate three-dimensional solid models using parametric CAD tools, demonstrating control over features, constraints, and assembly relationships
- Interpret and critically evaluate complex engineering drawings, identifying potential design ambiguities, tolerancing issues, and manufacturing implications
- Apply principles of descriptive geometry to solve spatial problems involving intersections, developments, and true-shape determinations
- Effectively communicate design intent through the selection of appropriate visualization methods, including exploded views, rendering techniques, and annotated technical illustrations

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-0200 must have successfully completed the following:

- **ENGI-0101: Introduction to Engineering Design** or equivalent introductory engineering course
- **MATH-0110: Calculus I** or concurrent enrollment
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.0 is recommended

Students with no prior exposure to technical drawing or drafting are strongly encouraged to review foundational geometry concepts before the first session. Prior experience with any CAD software, while not required, is considered an asset.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| CAD Modelling Projects (3) | 30% |
| Weekly Drawing Assignments | 15% |
| Design Documentation Portfolio | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** assess students' ability to interpret and produce technical drawings under timed conditions, including freehand sketching, multi-view projection problems, and spatial reasoning exercises.

**CAD Modelling Projects** are completed individually and require students to produce fully constrained parametric models and formal drawing packages for progressively complex components and assemblies. Projects are evaluated on accuracy, adherence to drafting standards, and clarity of design communication.

**Weekly Drawing Assignments** consist of short exercises reinforcing topics covered in lecture and laboratory sessions, including sectioning, dimensioning, and geometric tolerancing.

**Design Documentation Portfolio** requires students to compile and reflect upon their semester work, demonstrating growth in technical visualization skills and professional presentation standards.
