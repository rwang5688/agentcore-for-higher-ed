## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced course examines the communication technologies and protocols that underpin the Internet of Things (IoT) ecosystem, providing students with a comprehensive understanding of how devices, sensors, and systems interact within networked environments. Topics include wireless communication standards, network architectures, data transmission protocols, and the security and scalability challenges inherent to large-scale IoT deployments. Students will engage with both theoretical frameworks and hands-on laboratory experiences to develop practical competencies in designing and evaluating IoT communication systems.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and compare major IoT communication protocols and standards, including MQTT, CoAP, Zigbee, Z-Wave, LoRaWAN, and NB-IoT, with respect to their suitability for specific deployment contexts
- Design and implement network topologies appropriate for IoT environments, accounting for constraints such as power consumption, bandwidth, latency, and device density
- Evaluate the security vulnerabilities and mitigation strategies relevant to IoT communication layers, including encryption, authentication, and intrusion detection
- Assess edge computing and fog computing architectures as mechanisms for optimizing data flow and reducing latency in distributed IoT systems
- Synthesize technical and operational requirements to propose scalable, interoperable IoT communication solutions for real-world application scenarios

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed all of the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-6100: Computer Networks and Protocols** or equivalent
- **COMP-6250: Embedded Systems Design**

- Demonstrated proficiency in at least one programming language suitable for systems development (Python, C, or C++ strongly recommended)
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or instructor consent

Students lacking one or more prerequisites may petition the department for a prerequisite waiver, subject to review and approval by the course instructor or supervising professor.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written examination covering core communication protocols, network architectures, and theoretical foundations addressed in the first half of the course |
| Final Examination | 20% | Comprehensive written examination encompassing all course material, with emphasis on comparative analysis and applied problem-solving |
| Laboratory Assignments (×4) | 25% | Four structured lab exercises requiring students to configure, test, and evaluate IoT communication systems using physical hardware and simulation tools |
| Research Paper | 20% | An individual 3,000–4,000 word scholarly paper critically examining an emerging IoT communication technology or deployment challenge, submitted in IEEE format |
| Capstone Design Project | 15% | A semester-long group project in which teams of 3–4 students design, prototype, and present an IoT communication solution addressing a defined real-world problem |

**Note:** A minimum combined score of 50% across examination components is required to pass the course, regardless of overall weighted average. Late submissions will be subject to a penalty of 5% per business day unless an extension has been granted in writing by the supervising instructor.
