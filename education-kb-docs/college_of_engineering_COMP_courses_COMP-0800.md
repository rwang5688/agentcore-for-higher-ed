### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous and engaging exploration of computational hardness, equipping students with the theoretical tools necessary to demonstrate the inherent difficulty of algorithmic problems. Students will examine classical and modern techniques for establishing lower bounds, including reductions, NP-completeness theory, and complexity-theoretic frameworks, with an emphasis on developing intuition for why certain problems resist efficient solution. Through problem sets, proof workshops, and collaborative projects, students will practice constructing and communicating formal hardness arguments, preparing them for advanced study in algorithms, complexity theory, and related research areas.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Construct and verify polynomial-time reductions to establish NP-hardness, PSPACE-hardness, and related complexity-theoretic results for a range of computational problems
- Apply standard proof techniques — including gadget constructions, diagonalization, and adversarial arguments — to rigorously argue lower bounds on time, space, and query complexity
- Analyze problems drawn from graph theory, combinatorics, logic, and optimization and classify them within the standard complexity hierarchy (P, NP, coNP, PSPACE, EXP, and beyond)
- Evaluate and critique the completeness and correctness of hardness proofs presented in academic literature and peer submissions
- Communicate complex theoretical arguments clearly and precisely in both written and oral formats appropriate to a professional computer science audience

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following before enrolling in COMP-0800:

- **COMP-0240: Data Structures and Algorithm Design** — foundational knowledge of asymptotic analysis and algorithm paradigms is assumed throughout the course
- **COMP-0310: Theory of Computation** — familiarity with Turing machines, decidability, and the formal definition of complexity classes is essential
- **MATH-0210: Discrete Mathematics and Proof Writing** — students must be comfortable constructing formal mathematical proofs, including proofs by induction, contradiction, and construction

Students who have not completed COMP-0310 but have equivalent background may petition the department for a prerequisite waiver. Concurrent enrollment in COMP-0370: Advanced Algorithms is strongly recommended but not required.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly Problem Sets | 30% | Eight graded problem sets distributed throughout the semester, each focusing on a distinct proof technique or complexity class |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book written examination covering reduction techniques, NP-completeness, and lower bound arguments from the first half of the course |
| Hardness Proof Portfolio | 25% | A curated collection of three original hardness proofs developed iteratively across the semester, with instructor feedback incorporated between drafts |
| Final Group Project | 15% | Small teams select an open or recently resolved hardness problem from the literature, present a detailed analysis, and deliver both a written report and an in-class presentation |
| Participation & Proof Workshops | 10% | Active engagement in weekly proof workshop sessions, including peer review of classmates' reduction arguments |

Problem sets are accepted up to 48 hours late with a 15% penalty. Extensions beyond this window require documented extenuating circumstances and department approval. All submitted work must adhere to the Peculiar U Academic Integrity Policy;
