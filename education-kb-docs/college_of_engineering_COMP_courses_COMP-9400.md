## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-9400: Introduction to Computer Science: Programming Abstractions provides an advanced exploration of foundational programming concepts, with a focus on abstraction as a core principle of software design and computational thinking. Students will examine how abstraction enables the construction of complex systems through modular, reusable, and maintainable code structures, drawing upon paradigms including procedural, object-oriented, and functional programming. This course is designed to deepen theoretical and practical fluency in computational problem-solving for students pursuing advanced study in computer science and related engineering disciplines.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of abstraction principles, including data abstraction, procedural abstraction, and the role of interfaces in software design
- Design and implement modular, scalable software solutions using multiple programming paradigms, with attention to efficiency and code clarity
- Analyse and evaluate the computational complexity of algorithms and data structures, applying appropriate abstractions to optimise performance
- Apply formal reasoning and specification techniques to verify the correctness of programs and software components
- Synthesise knowledge of programming abstractions to collaboratively develop and present a substantive software project that addresses a real-world computational problem

### Prerequisites

Due to the advanced level of this course (Level 8235), students are expected to have substantial prior experience in computer science before enrolling. The following prerequisites are required:

- Successful completion of at least two prior undergraduate or postgraduate courses in computer science or software engineering, or equivalent demonstrated experience
- Proficiency in at least one high-level programming language (e.g., Python, Java, C++, or equivalent), as assessed by the Department of Computer Science
- Completion of COMP-7100: Foundations of Algorithms and Data Structures or an approved equivalent course
- A cumulative GPA of 3.0 or above, or written departmental approval from the supervising professor

Students who do not meet these prerequisites but believe they possess equivalent knowledge and experience may petition the Department of Computer Science for a prerequisite waiver prior to the start of term.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-9400 will be evaluated through a combination of formative and summative assessments designed to reflect both theoretical understanding and practical application of programming abstractions:

- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written and computational examination covering core abstraction principles, algorithm analysis, and programming paradigms introduced in the first half of the course
- **Final Examination (25%):** A comprehensive written and computational examination assessing mastery of all course material, including formal reasoning, complexity analysis, and paradigm application
- **Programming Assignments (30%):** A series of four graded programming assignments, each targeting a specific abstraction concept or paradigm. Assignments require working implementations accompanied by written reflections on design decisions
- **Research and Design Paper (15%):** An individual written paper (3,000–4,000 words) in which students critically examine a chosen abstraction paradigm or design pattern, situating their analysis within current literature in the field
- **Group Software Project (10%):** A collaborative capstone project undertaken in groups of three to four students, culminating in a live demonstration and technical report. Projects are assessed on design rigour, code quality, teamwork, and presentation

Late submissions will be subject to a penalty of 10% per business day unless an extension has been formally approved. All assessments must be submitted in accordance with Peculiar University's Academic Integrity Policy.
