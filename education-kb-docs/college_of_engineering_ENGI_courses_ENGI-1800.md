## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced engineering course, developed in the tradition of S.C. Dutta Roy's foundational contributions to signal processing and network theory, provides a rigorous examination of linear systems, filter design, and circuit network analysis at the graduate level. Students will engage with both classical and contemporary methodologies drawn from Dutta Roy's influential body of work at IIT Delhi, applying theoretical frameworks to practical engineering challenges. The course bridges analytical signal theory with real-world applications in communication systems, control engineering, and electronic network synthesis.

### Learning Objectives

- Analyze and synthesize passive and active electrical networks using advanced frequency-domain techniques and network function theory
- Apply linear systems theory to the design and evaluation of analog and digital filter architectures, including Butterworth, Chebyshev, and elliptic filter families
- Evaluate signal transmission behavior in multi-port networks using scattering parameters and transfer matrix methods
- Formulate and solve complex engineering problems in circuit optimization and approximation theory using mathematical tools from functional analysis
- Critically assess published engineering literature and apply research methodologies to original problems in network theory and signal processing

### Assessment Methods

**Analytical Problem Sets (20%)**
Five graded problem sets distributed throughout the semester, requiring detailed derivations and numerical solutions in network synthesis and filter theory. Collaboration is permitted at the discussion level; submitted work must be independently written.

**Midterm Examination (20%)**
A three-hour closed-book examination covering linear systems theory, network functions, and filter approximation methods administered at the midpoint of the semester. Students are permitted one hand-written reference sheet.

**Research Paper and Presentation (25%)**
A formal research paper of 6,000–8,000 words on an approved topic within the scope of the course, presented to the seminar in the final two weeks of term. Topics must be approved by the supervising instructor no later than Week 5.

**Laboratory Design Project (25%)**
A team-based design project requiring students to synthesize, simulate, and prototype a working filter network meeting specified performance criteria. Final deliverables include a technical report, circuit documentation, and a live demonstration session.

**Final Examination (10%)**
A comprehensive take-home examination distributed in the final week, emphasizing integrative analysis across all course modules. Submissions are subject to academic integrity review.
