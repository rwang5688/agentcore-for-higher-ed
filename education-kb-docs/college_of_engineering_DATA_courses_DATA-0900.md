---

This advanced course provides a rigorous examination of the theoretical foundations and applied frameworks that underpin modern data science practice, with particular emphasis on end-to-end pipeline construction, model validation strategies, and reproducible research methodology. Students will critically evaluate established methodological paradigms—including CRISP-DM, TDSP, and domain-adaptive variants—and assess their appropriateness across diverse analytical contexts. The course bridges the gap between technical proficiency and methodological discipline, preparing students to design and defend robust, scalable data science workflows in professional and research settings.

### Learning Objectives

- Critically analyze and compare leading data science methodological frameworks, identifying their theoretical assumptions, strengths, and limitations in applied contexts
- Design and implement complete, reproducible data science pipelines that adhere to best practices in data governance, version control, and documentation
- Apply advanced model evaluation and validation techniques, including cross-validation strategies, performance benchmarking, and uncertainty quantification
- Formulate and communicate methodologically sound data science proposals that align analytical strategy with domain-specific objectives and ethical considerations
- Synthesize literature from both technical and social science perspectives to critically assess the broader implications of data-driven decision-making systems

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Methodology Critique Papers (×2) | 25% | Two written critiques (1,500–2,000 words each) analyzing a published data science methodology or applied case study, assessing rigor, reproducibility, and ethical implications |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book written examination covering theoretical frameworks, pipeline design principles, and validation methodology |
| Group Methodology Audit Project | 30% | Teams of 3–4 students will conduct a comprehensive methodological audit of a real-world publicly available dataset and associated published analysis, culminating in a formal audit report and 20-minute presentation |
| Final Independent Research Portfolio | 20% | An individually produced end-to-end methodological design document for an original data science project, including problem framing, workflow specification, evaluation plan, and ethical review |
| Participation and In-Class Exercises | 5% | Active engagement in weekly discussion seminars and structured in-class analytical exercises |

Critique papers are due at the end of Weeks 5 and 10. The Group Audit Project presentation schedule will be distributed during Week 2. The Final Research Portfolio is due during the final examination period. Late submissions will be penalized at 5% per day unless an extension has been approved in writing by the supervising instructor.
