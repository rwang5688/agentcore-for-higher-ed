## Course Description

**COMP-14200 | Principles of Digital Communications I**
*3 Credit Points | Computer Science | College of Engineering*

---

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the fundamental principles underlying digital communication systems, including signal representation, encoding schemes, and data transmission across communication channels. Students will explore the theoretical and practical aspects of how digital information is formatted, modulated, and transmitted reliably in modern networked environments. Emphasis is placed on developing a conceptual and mathematical foundation for advanced study in communications engineering and computer networking.

### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the fundamental principles underlying digital communication systems, including signal representation, encoding schemes, and data transmission across communication channels. Students will explore the theoretical and practical aspects of how digital information is formatted, modulated, and transmitted reliably in modern networked environments. Emphasis is placed on developing a conceptual and mathematical foundation for advanced study in communications engineering and computer networking.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain core concepts of digital signal representation, including sampling, quantization, and pulse-code modulation, and apply these concepts to practical communication scenarios.
- Analyze and compare common digital modulation techniques — including ASK, FSK, PSK, and QAM — in terms of bandwidth efficiency, noise tolerance, and application suitability.
- Apply information theory fundamentals, including Shannon's capacity theorem and entropy, to evaluate the theoretical limits of communication systems.
- Describe and differentiate channel encoding and error-detection/correction schemes such as parity checking, Hamming codes, and cyclic redundancy checks (CRC).
- Demonstrate proficiency in evaluating communication system performance under various noise and interference conditions using standard analytical methods.

**Examinations** are closed-book; one double-sided formula sheet (student-prepared) is permitted. Examinations assess both conceptual understanding and quantitative problem-solving. \
**Laboratory Assignments** involve simulation and analysis tasks using industry-standard software tools to model digital communication systems. \

**Quizzes** are administered at the discretion of the instructor and cover material from recent lectures and assigned readings.
