## Course Description

### Course Description

This graduate-level course provides an advanced exploration of applied artificial intelligence with a focus on deep learning architectures and their real-world implementations. Students will examine foundational and cutting-edge neural network models, including convolutional, recurrent, and transformer-based architectures, applying these techniques to domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, and autonomous systems. Emphasis is placed on bridging theoretical understanding with practical deployment, equipping students to design, train, and evaluate deep learning solutions for complex engineering problems.

### Learning Objectives

- Design and implement deep learning models using industry-standard frameworks, demonstrating proficiency in architecture selection, hyperparameter tuning, and model optimization
- Critically evaluate the performance, scalability, and ethical implications of AI systems deployed in real-world contexts
- Apply advanced techniques such as transfer learning, regularization, and data augmentation to improve model generalization across diverse datasets
- Interpret and communicate findings from deep learning experiments through rigorous quantitative analysis and technical documentation
- Integrate deep learning pipelines into end-to-end application workflows, including data preprocessing, model training, validation, and deployment

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-620: Machine Learning Fundamentals** or equivalent graduate-level machine learning coursework
- **COMP-605: Data Structures and Algorithm Design** or equivalent
- **MATH-510: Linear Algebra and Multivariate Calculus for Engineers**
- Proficiency in Python programming, including experience with at least one scientific computing library (e.g., NumPy, pandas, or scikit-learn)
- Graduate standing in the College of Engineering or instructor approval

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Programming Assignments (×4) | 30% | Hands-on coding tasks requiring implementation of specified deep learning models and architectures |
| Midterm Examination | 15% | Written and computational exam covering theoretical foundations and model design principles |
| Research Paper Review | 10% | Critical written analysis of a peer-selected seminal or recent publication in deep learning |
| Group Project | 30% | End-to-end deep learning system designed, built, and deployed by student teams; includes a technical report and live demonstration |
| Final Examination | 15% | Comprehensive written examination addressing applied concepts, case studies, and model evaluation strategies |

Active participation in laboratory sessions and peer reviews is expected and may be factored into borderline grade determinations at instructor discretion.

### Required Materials

Students are expected to obtain the following materials prior to the first week of class:

- **Vanthorpe, D., & Osei-Kwame, L. (2021).** *Deep Learning in Practice: Architectures, Pipelines, and Deployment Strategies* (3rd ed.). Meridian Academic Press. — Primary course textbook; all chapter assignments reference this edition.
- **Hallstrom, C., Reyes, M., & Fujita, N. (2022).** *Neural Networks from the Ground Up: A Graduate Introduction* (2nd ed.). Stonebridge Publishers. — Supplementary reference for theoretical foundations and mathematical derivations.
- **Preczewski, A. (2023).** *Applied AI Engineering: Case Studies in Modern Deep Learning*. Ashford Technical Press. — Case study readings assigned throughout the second half of the course.
- Access to the **Peculiar U High-Performance Computing Cluster (HPCC)** is required for all programming assignments; students must complete the HPCC onboarding module via the university IT portal before Week 2.
- A current student license for **TensorFlow** and **PyTorch** is available at no cost through the College of Engineering software portal.

Additional journal articles, preprints, and supplementary readings will be distributed through the course management system on a weekly basis.
