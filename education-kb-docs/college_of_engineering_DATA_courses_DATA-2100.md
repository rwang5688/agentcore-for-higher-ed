## Course Description

**Department of Data Science | College of Engineering**
**DATA-2100 | Exploratory Data Analysis | 3 Credit Points**

---

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) provides students with a rigorous foundation in the systematic investigation of datasets prior to formal modelling or hypothesis testing. Students will develop proficiency in summarising data characteristics, identifying patterns, detecting anomalies, and generating actionable insights through a combination of statistical reasoning and visualisation techniques. The course emphasises both the computational and interpretive dimensions of data exploration, preparing students to approach complex, real-world datasets with critical analytical confidence.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply descriptive statistical methods — including measures of central tendency, dispersion, and distributional shape — to characterise and summarise diverse datasets accurately and efficiently.
- Design and produce effective data visualisations, selecting appropriate graphical representations to communicate patterns, relationships, and anomalies to both technical and non-technical audiences.
- Identify and address common data quality issues such as missing values, outliers, multicollinearity, and encoding inconsistencies using principled, reproducible methodologies.
- Conduct multivariate exploratory analysis, examining relationships between variables through correlation analysis, dimensionality reduction techniques, and feature interaction assessments.
- Formulate and communicate data-driven narratives by integrating statistical findings with contextual domain knowledge, laying the groundwork for informed model selection and inferential analysis.

---

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in DATA-2100 should have successfully completed the following, or demonstrate equivalent competency:

- **DATA-1050: Foundations of Data Science** or **STAT-1200: Introduction to Statistical Reasoning** — providing baseline familiarity with statistical concepts and data terminology.
- **COMP-1150: Programming for Data Applications** — ensuring proficiency in at least one relevant programming language (Python or R strongly recommended).
- **MATH-1300: Linear Algebra and Calculus for Engineers** — supporting the mathematical reasoning underlying distributional and multivariate methods.

Students without formal prerequisites may petition the Department of Data Science for a competency review in lieu of completed coursework. Instructor consent is required for all exceptions.

---

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in DATA-2100 is evaluated through a combination of formative and summative assessments designed to reflect both theoretical understanding and applied analytical skill:

- **Laboratory Assignments (25%)** — A series of six structured lab exercises completed individually, requiring students to apply EDA techniques to curated datasets using Python or R. Submissions must include annotated code, outputs, and written interpretations.
- **Midterm Examination (20%)** — A closed-book written examination assessing conceptual knowledge of descriptive statistics, data quality management, and visualisation theory. Includes short-answer and applied interpretation questions.
- **Exploratory Analysis Project — Stage 1: Dataset Audit (15%)** — Students select an approved real-world dataset and submit a formal data audit report documenting structure, quality issues, and preliminary variable profiles.
- **Exploratory Analysis Project — Stage 2: Full EDA Report (30%)** — Building on Stage 1, students deliver a comprehensive EDA report incorporating multivariate analysis, visual storytelling, and a documented analytical workflow. A short oral presentation to the instructional team is required.
- **Participation and In-Class Analysis Exercises (10%)** — Active engagement in weekly discussion sections and unannounced in-class analytical exercises evaluating readiness and critical thinking.

Late submissions are subject to a penalty of 5% per calendar day unless an extension has been granted in writing by the supervising instructor.
