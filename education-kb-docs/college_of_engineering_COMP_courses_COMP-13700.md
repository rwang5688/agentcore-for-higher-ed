## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the theory and practice of neural networks and deep learning, covering foundational architectures, training methodologies, and modern applications in artificial intelligence. Students will explore the mathematical underpinnings of multi-layer perceptrons, convolutional networks, recurrent architectures, and attention-based models, with an emphasis on both conceptual understanding and practical implementation. By the conclusion of the course, students will be equipped to design, train, and evaluate deep learning systems for a range of real-world computational problems.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of COMP-13700, students will be able to:

- Explain the biological inspiration and mathematical foundations of artificial neural networks, including activation functions, loss functions, and gradient-based optimization techniques such as backpropagation and stochastic gradient descent.
- Design and implement core deep learning architectures — including feedforward networks, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and transformer models — using contemporary programming frameworks.
- Apply regularization strategies, hyperparameter tuning, and model selection techniques to improve generalization and prevent overfitting in deep learning systems.
- Evaluate and critically analyze the performance of trained models using appropriate metrics, visualization tools, and benchmark datasets.
- Identify ethical considerations and practical limitations associated with the deployment of deep learning systems, including issues of bias, interpretability, and computational resource requirements.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following courses prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-11200: Data Structures and Algorithms** — Foundational programming competency and algorithmic reasoning are essential for implementing and analyzing neural network architectures.
- **COMP-12400: Introduction to Machine Learning** — Familiarity with supervised and unsupervised learning paradigms, model evaluation, and feature engineering is assumed throughout this course.
- **MATH-11500: Linear Algebra** — A working knowledge of vectors, matrices, matrix decomposition, and linear transformations is required, as these concepts are central to neural network operations.
- **MATH-11800: Probability and Statistics** — Students must be comfortable with probability distributions, expectation, variance, and basic statistical inference.

Students who have not completed COMP-12400 but have equivalent demonstrated experience may petition the Department of Computer Science for a prerequisite waiver.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-13700 will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Programming Assignments (35%)** — Four graded assignments distributed across the semester, each requiring students to implement and experiment with specific neural network architectures using Python-based deep learning frameworks. Assignments are submitted via the course repository portal and are assessed on correctness, code quality, and written analysis of results.
- **Midterm Examination (15%)** — A written, closed-book examination covering theoretical content from the first half of the course, including network fundamentals, optimization theory, and convolutional architectures. The exam will include a combination of short-answer and derivation problems.
- **Final Examination (20%)** — A comprehensive written examination administered during the university's official final examination period, encompassing all course material with emphasis on topics introduced in the second half of the semester.
- **Deep Learning Project (25%)** — A culminating team-based project (groups of two to three students) in which students identify a problem domain, curate or obtain a suitable dataset, design and train a deep learning model, and present their findings in both a written report and a live demonstration. Projects are assessed on technical rigor, creativity, experimental depth, and quality of communication.
- **Participation and Quizzes (5%)** — Short in-class quizzes and active participation in discussions and lab sessions will contribute to the final grade. Quizzes are unannounced and are designed to reinforce recent lecture material.
