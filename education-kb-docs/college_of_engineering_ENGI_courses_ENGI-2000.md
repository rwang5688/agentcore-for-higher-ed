## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-2000: Electronics & Communication Engineering provides an advanced and rigorous exploration of the theoretical foundations and practical applications underpinning modern electronic systems and communication technologies. Students will examine signal processing, circuit design, electromagnetic wave propagation, and the engineering principles governing contemporary analog and digital communication networks. This course bridges classical electronic theory with cutting-edge communication engineering practice, preparing students for high-level research, industry application, and interdisciplinary collaboration within the field.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyse and design complex analog and digital electronic circuits, demonstrating mastery of advanced circuit theory and component behaviour under varied operating conditions
- Apply signal processing techniques — including Fourier analysis, filtering, and modulation schemes — to the design and evaluation of communication systems
- Evaluate electromagnetic wave propagation principles and their direct implications for antenna design, transmission line theory, and wireless communication architectures
- Synthesise knowledge of communication protocols and network architectures to assess system performance, reliability, and bandwidth optimisation in real-world engineering contexts
- Critically engage with emerging technologies and current research literature in electronics and communication engineering, producing original analytical contributions appropriate to the doctoral and advanced postgraduate level

---

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (Level 835), students are expected to have substantial prior academic and/or professional preparation. The following prerequisites are required:

- Successful completion of graduate-level coursework in electrical engineering fundamentals or equivalent (minimum postgraduate standing)
- Demonstrated proficiency in circuit analysis, including experience with both linear and non-linear circuit components
- Prior study in signals and systems theory, including Laplace and Fourier transforms
- Working knowledge of electromagnetic field theory, equivalent to an advanced undergraduate or postgraduate unit in electromagnetism
- Competency in at least one relevant computational or simulation environment (e.g., MATLAB, SPICE-based platforms, or equivalent)
- Departmental approval required for students entering without a formal background in Engineering; evidence of equivalent professional experience may be considered at the discretion of the course coordinators

### Assessment Methods

Assessment in ENGI-2000 is designed to evaluate both theoretical mastery and applied problem-solving capacity at an advanced scholarly level.

| Assessment Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Written Examination | 20% | A three-hour written examination covering signal theory, circuit design principles, and electromagnetic fundamentals |
| Final Comprehensive Examination | 25% | A four-hour examination encompassing all course content, including system-level analysis and design problems |
| Research Paper | 25% | An original scholarly paper (6,000–8,000 words) critically analysing a current development or unresolved problem in electronics and communication engineering; must adhere to IEEE citation standards |
| Design Project | 20% | A team-based engineering design project requiring the proposal, simulation, and technical documentation of a functional communication system or electronic subsystem; includes a formal oral presentation |
| Weekly Problem Sets | 10% | Structured problem sets submitted throughout the semester, assessing ongoing comprehension of quantitative and theoretical content |

*Note: A minimum grade of 50% on both the Final Examination and the Research Paper is required to pass the course, regardless of aggregate score.*
