### Course Description

This course provides an advanced examination of the engineering profession, exploring its historical foundations, ethical responsibilities, regulatory frameworks, and evolving role in contemporary society. Students engage critically with professional codes of conduct, licensure requirements, interdisciplinary collaboration, and the societal implications of engineering decisions at both local and global scales. Emphasis is placed on developing the professional identity, communication skills, and leadership competencies essential for practice in modern engineering environments.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyse and apply established professional codes of ethics to complex, real-world engineering scenarios involving competing stakeholder interests and ambiguous constraints.
- Evaluate the regulatory and licensure landscape governing engineering practice across multiple jurisdictions, including the roles of professional associations and accreditation bodies.
- Critically assess the social, environmental, and economic responsibilities of engineers as they relate to sustainable development and public safety.
- Demonstrate advanced written and oral communication skills appropriate for professional engineering contexts, including technical reporting, client-facing documentation, and public discourse.
- Synthesise knowledge from technical disciplines and the humanities to formulate informed positions on emerging challenges facing the engineering profession, such as automation, globalisation, and equity in STEM.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-6900 must have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent preparation:

- ENGI-5200: Engineering Systems and Society, or equivalent upper-division survey course in engineering fundamentals
- ENGI-5410: Engineering Analysis and Design Principles
- Minimum cumulative GPA of 3.0 at the graduate or upper-division undergraduate level
- Completion of at least 90 credit points toward an accredited engineering degree programme, or current enrolment in a graduate engineering programme

Students are also expected to have prior experience with technical writing and basic project management concepts. Enrolment by permission of instructor is available for students who do not meet all listed prerequisites but can demonstrate sufficient preparation through equivalent professional or academic experience.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-6900 will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Professional Ethics Case Study Analysis (25%):** Students will complete two written case analyses (approximately 1,500–2,000 words each) examining real or simulated engineering ethics dilemmas. Analyses will be assessed on depth of ethical reasoning, application of professional codes, and quality of argumentation.
- **Licensure and Regulatory Research Paper (20%):** A formal research paper (3,000–4,000 words) investigating the professional licensure requirements and regulatory environments in at least two jurisdictions, with a comparative analysis of standards and implications for professional practice.
- **Professional Identity Portfolio (20%):** A structured portfolio documenting the student's developing professional identity, including a reflective personal statement, a curated record of competencies, and a career development plan aligned with recognised engineering frameworks.
- **Group Seminar Presentation (15%):** Student teams will research and present a 25–30 minute seminar on an assigned contemporary issue facing the engineering profession, followed by a moderated Q&A session. Peer and instructor evaluations contribute to the final score.
- **Final Integrative Examination (20%):** A closed-book written examination assessing students' comprehensive understanding of course content, including ethical theory, professional regulation, societal responsibility, and professional communication principles.
- **Participation and Engagement (10%):** Active contribution to seminar discussions, guest lecture sessions, and peer critique activities throughout the semester.
