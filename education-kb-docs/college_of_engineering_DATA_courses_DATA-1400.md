This course provides a rigorous and comprehensive foundation in the mathematical concepts and techniques essential to the practice of data science, including linear algebra, probability theory, calculus, and statistical reasoning. Students will develop the quantitative literacy necessary to understand, implement, and critically evaluate the algorithms and models central to modern data analysis. Emphasis is placed on the application of mathematical principles to real-world data problems, preparing students for advanced study and professional work in the field.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply core concepts of linear algebra, including vector spaces, matrix operations, and eigendecomposition, to data representation and transformation tasks.
- Demonstrate a working understanding of multivariate calculus, including gradients, partial derivatives, and optimization techniques, as they relate to machine learning and statistical modeling.
- Utilize probability theory and combinatorics to model uncertainty, construct distributions, and reason formally about data-generating processes.
- Perform and interpret inferential statistical analyses, including hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and regression, within a data science context.
- Translate abstract mathematical formulations into computational implementations, bridging theoretical knowledge and practical data science workflows.

### Prerequisites

Given the advanced level of this course (Level 950), students are expected to have substantial prior academic preparation. The following prerequisites are required:

- Successful completion of an advanced undergraduate or early graduate-level course in **calculus** (single- and multivariable) with a grade of B or higher.
- Demonstrated proficiency in **linear algebra** at the undergraduate level, or completion of an equivalent bridging module approved by the Department of Data Science.
- Prior coursework or demonstrable experience in **introductory statistics or probability theory**.
- Familiarity with at least one **scientific programming language** (e.g., Python or R) is strongly recommended, as computational exercises will form an integral part of course activities.

Students who do not meet these prerequisites must obtain written approval from the course coordinator prior to enrollment.
