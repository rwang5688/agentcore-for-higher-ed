## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-4700: Cybersecurity and Its Ten Domains provides a comprehensive examination of the foundational principles and advanced practices that underpin modern information security, organized through the lens of the ten core domains recognized within the cybersecurity profession. Students will develop a rigorous understanding of security architecture, cryptography, access control, network security, and related disciplines, exploring both theoretical frameworks and their practical application in real-world organizational environments. This course prepares students for professional certification pathways and equips them with the analytical tools necessary to assess, design, and defend complex information systems against evolving threats.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify, explain, and critically evaluate each of the ten domains of cybersecurity, demonstrating a holistic understanding of how these domains interact within an enterprise security posture.
- Analyze common and emerging threat vectors, vulnerabilities, and attack methodologies, and apply domain-specific countermeasures to mitigate organizational risk.
- Design and assess security policies, access control frameworks, and identity management strategies aligned with industry standards and regulatory compliance requirements.
- Apply cryptographic principles and protocols to real-world scenarios involving data confidentiality, integrity, authentication, and non-repudiation.
- Conduct structured security risk assessments and produce professional-grade documentation including threat models, incident response plans, and security audit reports.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolling in COMP-4700:

- **COMP-3100: Network Fundamentals and Protocols** — foundational knowledge of TCP/IP networking, OSI model, and network architecture is assumed throughout this course.
- **COMP-3250: Introduction to Operating Systems Security** — familiarity with OS-level permissions, process management, and system hardening is required.
- **COMP-3410: Principles of Information Assurance** — students must demonstrate baseline competency in risk management concepts and security policy frameworks.
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.5 in Computer Science coursework is recommended. Students who have not completed the above prerequisites but believe they have equivalent experience should consult with their academic advisor or the course instructor prior to registration.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Domain Mastery Examinations (30%)** — Two closed-book, in-person examinations administered at the midpoint and end of the semester. Each examination assesses comprehension and applied reasoning across the ten cybersecurity domains covered to that point in the course.
- **Threat Analysis Case Studies (20%)** — Three written case study analyses, each approximately 1,500–2,000 words, in which students apply domain-specific frameworks to dissect documented real-world or hypothetical security incidents, identifying failures, remediation strategies, and lessons learned.
- **Security Domain Research Paper (20%)** — An individual research paper of 3,500–4,500 words focusing on an emerging challenge within one of the ten cybersecurity domains. Papers must demonstrate engagement with current peer-reviewed literature and professional standards documentation.
- **Capstone Security Assessment Project (25%)** — A team-based project in which student groups of three to four conduct a simulated security assessment of a provided organizational scenario. Deliverables include a comprehensive written report and a formal presentation to the class during the final two weeks of the semester.
- **Participation and In-Class Exercises (5%)** — Active engagement in weekly discussion activities, laboratory exercises, and scenario-based workshops.
