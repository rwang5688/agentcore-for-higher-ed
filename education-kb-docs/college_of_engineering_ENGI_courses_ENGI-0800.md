## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-0800: Peter Woolf University of Michigan is an advanced undergraduate engineering course that draws upon the interdisciplinary methodologies and research frameworks pioneered by Peter Woolf at the University of Michigan, integrating computational modeling, systems biology, and data-driven engineering principles. Students will explore the intersection of machine learning, biological systems, and chemical engineering to address complex real-world problems through quantitative analysis and iterative design. This course emphasizes both theoretical foundations and practical application, preparing students to contribute meaningfully to emerging fields at the frontier of modern engineering.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply computational and data-driven methodologies to model and analyze complex engineering systems, with emphasis on biological and chemical processes
- Critically evaluate and implement machine learning algorithms within engineering contexts, assessing their appropriateness and limitations for specific problem domains
- Design and execute iterative experimental frameworks that integrate quantitative data analysis with systems-level thinking
- Synthesize interdisciplinary knowledge spanning chemical engineering, computational biology, and applied mathematics to formulate and justify engineering solutions
- Communicate technical findings clearly and rigorously through written reports, oral presentations, and collaborative team-based project work

---

---

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-0800 will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Midterm Examination (20%):** A written closed-book examination assessing students' comprehension of core theoretical frameworks, computational methods, and systems modeling concepts covered in the first half of the course
- **Final Examination (25%):** A comprehensive written examination covering all course material, with an emphasis on integrative problem-solving and application of learned methodologies to novel scenarios
- **Laboratory and Computational Assignments (20%):** A series of six structured assignments requiring students to implement computational models, perform data analysis, and document their processes and findings using standard engineering reporting conventions
- **Interdisciplinary Research Project (25%):** A semester-long team-based project in which student groups identify a real-world engineering problem, apply course methodologies to develop a solution, and present their work in both a written technical report and a formal oral presentation to the class and instructional staff
- **Participation and Weekly Reflection Submissions (10%):** Students are expected to engage actively in lectures and discussion sections and to submit brief weekly written reflections connecting course content to current developments in the field
