---

This course provides a rigorous examination of the physical and chemical processes governing the Earth's atmosphere and ocean systems, with particular emphasis on their coupled interactions and the role these systems play in driving environmental change across multiple timescales. Students will explore the mechanisms underlying climate variability, oceanic circulation, and atmospheric dynamics, drawing on principles from physics, chemistry, and earth science. The course integrates contemporary observational data and modelling frameworks to connect foundational theory with real-world environmental challenges, including anthropogenic climate forcing and its measurable consequences.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Describe the fundamental physical and chemical properties of the atmosphere and ocean, and explain how energy transfer, circulation patterns, and biogeochemical cycles link these two systems.
- Analyse the mechanisms driving short- and long-term climate variability, including natural forcing factors and anthropogenic contributions to observed environmental change.
- Interpret atmospheric and oceanographic data sets using appropriate quantitative methods, and critically evaluate evidence presented in primary scientific literature.
- Apply conceptual and numerical models to simulate and predict responses within coupled atmosphere-ocean systems under varied environmental conditions.
- Assess the broader environmental, societal, and policy implications of ongoing changes to atmospheric composition and ocean dynamics, demonstrating an ability to communicate findings to both specialist and non-specialist audiences.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-3100 are expected to have completed the following, or to have obtained written permission from the course coordinator:

- **ENVI-2010** Introduction to Earth and Environmental Systems, or equivalent
- **PHYS-2200** Fundamentals of Fluid Dynamics and Thermodynamics, or equivalent
- **MATH-2105** Calculus and Differential Equations II
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.5 is recommended

Students with prior coursework in atmospheric science, physical oceanography, or environmental chemistry are encouraged to consult with the instructors regarding course suitability.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | In-class written examination covering atmospheric and oceanic fundamentals (Weeks 1–6) |
| Final Examination | 25% | Comprehensive written examination with emphasis on synthesis and applied problem-solving |
| Laboratory Reports (×4) | 25% | Written reports corresponding to scheduled laboratory sessions; each report assesses data analysis, interpretation, and scientific communication |
| Research Paper | 20% | A 2,500–3,500 word individually authored paper examining a defined aspect of atmosphere-ocean coupling or observed environmental change, incorporating peer-reviewed literature |
| Tutorial Participation & Problem Sets | 10% | Weekly problem sets and demonstrated engagement during tutorial sessions |

Students are required to achieve a passing grade in both the final examination and the research paper independently of their overall weighted average in order to receive credit for the course.
