## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-3200: Computational Camera and Photography explores the intersection of computer science, optics, and digital imaging through the lens of modern computational photography systems. Students will examine how algorithms, hardware, and software pipelines work together to capture, process, and enhance digital images beyond the limitations of traditional photography. Topics include image sensor design, computational imaging pipelines, HDR imaging, depth estimation, image synthesis, and the mathematical foundations underlying contemporary camera systems found in consumer devices and research applications.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the fundamental principles of digital image formation, including light capture, sensor physics, and the image signal processing (ISP) pipeline
- Implement computational photography algorithms such as tone mapping, image stitching, deblurring, and multi-image fusion using appropriate programming tools and frameworks
- Analyze and evaluate the trade-offs inherent in computational imaging system design, including noise reduction, dynamic range, resolution, and computational cost
- Apply mathematical techniques from linear algebra, signal processing, and optimization to solve problems in image enhancement and reconstruction
- Design and critically assess a complete computational imaging pipeline, from raw sensor data acquisition through to final rendered output

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-3200 must have successfully completed the following:

- **COMP-2100: Data Structures and Algorithms** — foundational programming and algorithmic thinking required for implementing image processing pipelines
- **MATH-2210: Linear Algebra** — matrix operations, transformations, and decomposition methods are used extensively throughout the course
- **MATH-2310: Probability and Statistics** — probabilistic reasoning underpins noise modeling, Bayesian image reconstruction, and machine learning components
- **COMP-2400: Fundamentals of Signal Processing** — or equivalent; students should be familiar with Fourier analysis, sampling, and convolution

Students with prior exposure to introductory Python or C++ programming and a basic familiarity with digital image formats are strongly encouraged to review those topics before the term begins. Students who have not completed the listed prerequisites must obtain written instructor approval before registering.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written and computational exam covering image formation, sensor physics, and foundational algorithms |
| Final Examination | 20% | Comprehensive written examination covering all course material |
| Programming Assignments (×4) | 30% | Incremental coding projects implementing core computational photography techniques in Python using provided image datasets |
| Research Paper | 15% | A 2,500–3,500 word critical survey of a current research area in computational imaging (e.g., neural radiance fields, learned ISP pipelines, event cameras) |
| Capstone Project | 15% | A team-based (2–3 students) end-of-term project requiring the design, implementation, and documented evaluation of an original computational photography system or pipeline component |

**Note on the Capstone Project:** Teams must submit a one-page project proposal by Week 5 for instructor approval. Final deliverables include a working codebase, a written technical report, and a short recorded demonstration video. Peer evaluation will contribute up to 10% of the Capstone Project grade.

Late submissions will incur a penalty of 10% per calendar day unless an extension has been granted in writing by the supervising instructor at least 48 hours before the deadline.

### Required Materials and Textbooks

Students are expected to acquire or gain access to the following materials before the first week of instruction:

- **Swindler, R. T., & Okafor, N. P.** (4th ed.). *Pixels and Pipelines: Computational Imaging from Sensor to Screen.* Halcroft Academic Press. — Primary course textbook; covers image formation, ISP design, and core computational photography algorithms
- **Mezzetti, C., Larbalestier, F., & Vo, T. H.** (2nd ed.). *Mathematical Foundations of Digital Imaging.* Westmarch University Press. — Reference text for the mathematical underpinnings of signal processing and optimization in imaging contexts
- **Dunmore, S. E.** (1st ed.). *Modern Camera Systems: Hardware, Software, and the Space Between.* Irongate Technical Publishers. — Supplementary reading on camera hardware architecture and real-world ISP implementation case studies

In addition to the above texts, students will be provided with access to a course-specific software environment via the Peculiar U Virtual Lab Portal, which includes pre-configured Python libraries
