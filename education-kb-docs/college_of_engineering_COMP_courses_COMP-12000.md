### Course Description

This advanced graduate-level course provides a rigorous and comprehensive examination of modern information security principles, offensive and defensive strategies, and the evolving threat landscape facing contemporary organizations. Students will engage with real-world attack methodologies, penetration testing frameworks, cryptographic systems, and enterprise-level defense architectures to develop both theoretical grounding and practical competency. Emphasis is placed on the ethical responsibilities of security professionals and the application of cutting-edge countermeasures against sophisticated adversaries.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and evaluate advanced threat vectors, including social engineering, zero-day exploits, malware ecosystems, and nation-state attack patterns, applying structured frameworks such as the MITRE ATT&CK matrix to model adversarial behavior
- Design, implement, and audit multi-layered defensive architectures incorporating firewalls, intrusion detection and prevention systems, endpoint protection platforms, and Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) solutions
- Apply cryptographic principles — including symmetric and asymmetric encryption, public key infrastructure (PKI), and post-quantum cryptographic schemes — to protect data at rest, in transit, and in use within enterprise environments
- Conduct ethical penetration testing and vulnerability assessments using industry-standard toolsets, document findings according to professional reporting standards, and propose evidence-based remediation strategies
- Critically assess organizational security policies, regulatory compliance frameworks (including NIST CSF, ISO/IEC 27001, GDPR, and HIPAA), and incident response protocols, and synthesize recommendations appropriate to complex, large-scale operational contexts

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed all of the following prior to enrollment:

- **COMP-8200: Advanced Networking and Distributed Systems** or equivalent graduate-level coursework
- **COMP-8450: Introduction to Cybersecurity Principles and Risk Management**
- **COMP-8600: Operating Systems Internals and Secure Systems Design**
- **COMP-9100: Applied Cryptography and Data Integrity**
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 3.0 in the graduate Computer Science program, or written instructor consent

Students without a formal computing background must demonstrate equivalent professional experience (a minimum of three years in a relevant IT or security role) via petition to the Department of Computer Science. Prior coursework in discrete mathematics and probability theory is strongly recommended.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Threat Intelligence Report | 20% | Individual written analysis |
| Penetration Testing Lab Portfolio | 25% | Practical lab submissions |
| Midterm Examination | 15% | Closed-book written examination |
| Final Examination | 20% | Comprehensive written examination |
| Capstone Security Architecture Project | 20% | Group project and presentation |

**Threat Intelligence Report (20%):** Each student will produce a 4,000–5,000 word analytical report investigating a real or hypothetical advanced persistent threat (APT) campaign. The report must document the attack lifecycle, affected systems, threat actor attribution where applicable, and a fully developed defensive response strategy. Reports are assessed on analytical depth, technical accuracy, and quality of written communication.

**Penetration Testing Lab Portfolio (25%):** Students will complete a series of six structured laboratory exercises using isolated virtual environments. Labs will cover network reconnaissance, web application exploitation, privilege escalation, lateral movement, data exfiltration simulation, and defensive hardening. Each lab requires a professional-format write-up detailing methodology, findings, and remediation recommendations. Submissions are evaluated on technical execution, documentation clarity, and adherence to ethical guidelines.

**Midterm Examination (15%):** A two-hour, closed-book written examination covering course content from Weeks 1 through 7. Questions will include multiple choice, short answer, and one extended response item requiring applied analysis.

**Final Examination (20%):** A three-hour comprehensive closed-book examination covering all course material. The examination will incorporate scenario-based questions requiring students to demonstrate integration of knowledge across the full breadth of the course.

**Capstone Security Architecture Project (20%):** Working in groups of three to four, students will design a complete enterprise security architecture for a fictional organization provided by the instructional team. Deliverables include a written technical proposal (minimum 8,000 words), network and systems diagrams, a risk register, an incident response
