This course introduces students to the foundations of mathematical reasoning and formal logic as applied within computer science contexts. Students will develop rigorous proof-writing skills across a variety of proof techniques, including direct proof, proof by contradiction, and mathematical induction, while exploring propositional and predicate logic. Emphasis is placed on cultivating the analytical precision and abstract thinking essential for success in advanced coursework in algorithms, theory of computation, and software verification.

### Learning Objectives

- Construct and evaluate formal logical arguments using both propositional and predicate calculus, correctly applying truth tables, logical equivalences, and quantifiers.
- Demonstrate proficiency in a range of proof techniques, including direct proof, proof by contrapositive, proof by contradiction, and proof by mathematical induction.
- Apply set theory concepts — including unions, intersections, complements, power sets, and Cartesian products — within formal mathematical arguments.
- Analyze the validity and soundness of logical arguments, identifying common errors in reasoning and distinguishing between rigorous and informal proof structures.
- Translate real-world computational problems and natural-language statements into formal logical notation and develop structured solutions accordingly.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed **MATH-10200: Pre-Calculus Mathematics** or equivalent with a grade of C or better, or have demonstrated equivalent competency through the university's mathematics placement assessment. Concurrent enrollment in **MATH-11500: Calculus I** is strongly recommended. No prior programming experience is required, though familiarity with basic algebraic reasoning is expected.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Homework Problem Sets (weekly) | 25% |

| Quizzes (four throughout semester) | 15% |

**Homework Problem Sets:** Weekly assignments require students to complete formal proofs and logical reasoning exercises, submitted in writing with full justification for all steps.

**Proof Portfolio:** Students will compile and revise a portfolio of six to eight original proofs developed throughout the semester, demonstrating growth in rigor, clarity, and technique. A brief written reflection accompanies the final submission.

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations are closed-book and held in-person. Students will be assessed on their ability to construct original proofs, evaluate logical arguments, and apply course concepts under timed conditions.

**Quizzes:** Four short in-class quizzes will assess comprehension of recently covered material and provide formative feedback to students and instructors.

Students are also required to maintain a dedicated course notebook for in-class proof exercises. Supplementary readings and practice problem sets will be distributed through the course's online learning portal at no additional cost.
