This course provides a rigorous foundation in probability theory and its applications within the context of data science, equipping students with the mathematical tools necessary to model uncertainty, analyse stochastic processes, and make principled inferences from data. Topics covered include discrete and continuous probability distributions, expectation, variance, conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, and the Law of Large Numbers, with particular emphasis on their computational implementation and relevance to real-world data problems. Students will develop both theoretical fluency and practical proficiency, preparing them for advanced study in statistical modelling, machine learning, and data analysis pipelines.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of DATA-1000, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of foundational probability theory, including sample spaces, events, probability axioms, and combinatorial methods, and apply these concepts to solve complex analytical problems.
- Characterise and work fluently with common discrete and continuous probability distributions — including binomial, Poisson, normal, and exponential distributions — selecting appropriate models for a variety of data science contexts.
- Apply conditional probability, independence, and Bayes' theorem to construct and evaluate probabilistic reasoning frameworks, including introductory Bayesian inference.
- Compute and interpret key summary statistics such as expectation, variance, covariance, and correlation, and use these quantities to describe and compare probabilistic models.
- Implement probability-based methods computationally using standard data science tools, translating theoretical constructs into simulations, estimations, and data-driven conclusions.

Students enrolling in DATA-1000 must have completed the following or demonstrate equivalent competency:

- **DATA-0200: Foundations of Data Science** or departmental equivalent

- A minimum grade of C+ in all prerequisite courses is required. Students with strong mathematical backgrounds from related disciplines may petition the Department of Data Science for prerequisite waiver consideration.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in DATA-1000 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Sets (×6) | 25% | Bi-weekly written assignments covering theoretical derivations and applied probability problems |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | Written closed-book examination covering material from Weeks 1–7 |
| Computational Lab Assignments (×4) | 20% | Guided programming labs requiring implementation of probabilistic models and simulations |
| Research Application Paper | 15% | Individual paper (1,500–2,500 words) applying a probability framework to a chosen data science problem |
| Final Examination | 20% | Comprehensive written examination; may include both closed-book theoretical questions and an open-reference applied section |

*A minimum combined score of 50% is required to pass this course. Students must sit both examinations to be eligible for a passing grade regardless of performance on other components.*
