### Course Description

This advanced course examines the environmental history of Australia from pre-colonial Indigenous land stewardship through the present day, tracing the ecological transformations brought about by European settlement, industrialisation, and contemporary conservation movements. Students will engage critically with the interplay between human societies and Australian ecosystems, including the management of arid landscapes, coastal systems, and unique biodiversity. Drawing on interdisciplinary methodologies from environmental science, history, and Indigenous studies, the course situates Australia's environmental trajectory within broader global narratives of ecological change and sustainability.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyse the long-term ecological impacts of Indigenous land management practices, including fire regimes and seasonal resource use, and evaluate their relevance to contemporary environmental policy in Australia.
- Critically assess the environmental consequences of European colonisation, including land clearing, species introductions, and water resource exploitation, across multiple Australian biomes and timescales.
- Evaluate the development of Australian conservation legislation and environmental governance frameworks, from early colonial-era resource extraction policies to modern sustainability initiatives.
- Synthesise primary and secondary historical sources alongside scientific data to construct evidence-based arguments about environmental change and human agency in the Australian context.
- Apply interdisciplinary theoretical frameworks — including political ecology, historical ecology, and postcolonial environmental thought — to real-world Australian case studies such as the Murray-Darling Basin crisis and the decline of the Great Barrier Reef.
