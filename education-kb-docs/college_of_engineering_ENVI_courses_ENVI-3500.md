This seminar course examines the principles, technologies, and policy frameworks underpinning woody biomass and forest-derived energy systems as components of the broader renewable energy landscape. Students explore the ecological, economic, and engineering dimensions of harvesting, processing, and converting wood-based materials into usable energy, with emphasis on sustainability and carbon accounting. The course draws on interdisciplinary perspectives to evaluate the role of forest energy in regional and global transitions away from fossil fuel dependence.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Evaluate the scientific basis of woody biomass energy production, including combustion, gasification, and pyrolysis pathways, and assess their relative efficiencies and environmental impacts
- Analyse the sustainability of wood energy systems through the application of life cycle assessment (LCA) methodologies and carbon flux modelling within forested landscapes
- Critically examine land use, biodiversity, and ecosystem service trade-offs associated with commercial-scale wood energy development and forest management practices
- Interpret and compare regulatory frameworks, incentive structures, and energy policy instruments governing biomass energy at municipal, national, and international levels
- Synthesise technical and socioecological evidence to formulate evidence-based recommendations regarding forest energy development in diverse regional contexts

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolment:

- **ENVI-2100: Foundations of Environmental Systems** or equivalent
- **ENVI-2210: Ecology and Natural Resource Management**
- One of: **ENGI-2050: Introduction to Energy Systems** or **ENVI-2300: Renewable Energy and the Environment**

Students lacking formal prerequisites but possessing equivalent documented experience may petition the Department of Environment and Natural Resources for enrolment consideration. A minimum cumulative GPA of 2.5 is recommended.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Seminar Participation & Discussion Leadership | 15% | Active engagement in weekly seminars; each student leads one discussion session |
| Critical Reading Responses | 10% | Six short responses (400–600 words each) to assigned readings submitted prior to seminar sessions |
| Midterm Policy Analysis Paper | 20% | A 2,000-word analysis of a current wood energy policy instrument, evaluating its scientific basis and practical effectiveness |
| Group Case Study Project | 30% | Teams of 3–4 students conduct an in-depth investigation of a real-world wood energy system, culminating in a written report (5,000–6,000 words) and a 20-minute in-class presentation |
| Final Individual Research Essay | 25% | A 3,000-word essay in which students develop and defend an original position on a contested issue in forest energy sustainability, incorporating peer-reviewed sources and policy literature |

All written submissions must adhere to the citation conventions outlined in the *Peculiar U Academic Writing Standards Guide*. Late submissions are subject to the College of Engineering's standard penalty policy unless prior accommodation has been arranged.
