## Course Description

**COMP-8500 | Introduction to AI Robotics**
*Credit Points: 3 | Department: Computer Science | College of Engineering*

---

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the principles and practices underlying artificial intelligence as applied to autonomous robotic systems. Students will examine the integration of machine learning algorithms, sensor fusion, and decision-making frameworks within physical and simulated robotic platforms. Emphasis is placed on both theoretical foundations and hands-on implementation, preparing students to design, analyze, and evaluate intelligent robotic systems in real-world contexts.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the fundamental architectures of AI-driven robotic systems, including perception, planning, and actuation pipelines
- Design and implement basic autonomous behaviors using established machine learning and control algorithms
- Evaluate and compare competing approaches to robotic navigation, manipulation, and environmental interaction
- Apply sensor fusion techniques to enable robust real-time decision-making in dynamic environments
- Critically assess the ethical, safety, and societal implications of deploying intelligent robotic systems in professional and public settings

---

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-8500 must have satisfactorily completed the following, or demonstrate equivalent competency as approved by the course instructor:

- **COMP-6210 – Foundations of Machine Learning** or equivalent graduate-level exposure to supervised and unsupervised learning methods
- **COMP-6150 – Algorithms and Computational Complexity** with a minimum grade of B-
- **COMP-5320 – Introduction to Robotics Systems** or demonstrated familiarity with robotic kinematics and control fundamentals
- Proficiency in Python and/or C++ programming; familiarity with the Robot Operating System (ROS) framework is strongly recommended
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or instructor permission required for all other students

---

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through a combination of theoretical assessments and applied project work, as follows:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Assignments (×4) | 25% |
| Capstone Design Project | 25% |
| Participation & Peer Review | 5% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** will assess conceptual understanding of AI robotics theory, algorithm analysis, and system design principles. Both examinations are closed-book with an approved reference sheet permitted.

**Laboratory Assignments** are completed individually or in pairs and require students to implement and test AI algorithms on simulated and/or physical robotic platforms available in the Peculiar U Robotics Engineering Lab.

**Capstone Design Project** requires student teams of three to four members to propose, design, implement, and present an original AI robotics application. A written technical report and live demonstration are required. Project proposals must be submitted and approved by Week 4 of the term.
