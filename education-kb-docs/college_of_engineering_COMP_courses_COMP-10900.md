## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-10900: Introduction to Formal Systems & Computation provides an advanced examination of the theoretical underpinnings of computer science, with particular emphasis on formal languages, automata theory, computability, and complexity. Students will rigorously investigate the mathematical structures that define the limits and capabilities of computational models, from finite automata to Turing machines. This course synthesizes foundational theoretical frameworks with practical reasoning skills, preparing students for research-level engagement with open problems in computational theory.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Construct and analyze formal language models, including regular, context-free, and recursively enumerable languages, and apply the appropriate computational models to each class
- Demonstrate a working understanding of computability theory, including the Church-Turing thesis, decidability, and the halting problem, and assess the theoretical limits of algorithmic problem-solving
- Apply reduction techniques and complexity-theoretic reasoning to classify computational problems within established complexity classes, including P, NP, and PSPACE
- Design and evaluate formal proofs pertaining to the properties of automata, grammars, and abstract machines with mathematical rigor and precision
- Critically engage with seminal and contemporary literature in theoretical computer science, situating course concepts within broader ongoing scholarly discourse

### Prerequisites

Students must have completed the following before enrolling in COMP-10900:

- COMP-30200: Algorithms and Data Structures (or equivalent)
- COMP-31500: Discrete Mathematics for Computer Science (or equivalent)
- MATH-20100: Logic and Proof Methods (or equivalent)
- A minimum cumulative GPA of 3.0 in Computer Science coursework, or instructor permission

Students are additionally expected to have substantial programming experience in at least one high-level language and demonstrated proficiency in formal mathematical reasoning. Undergraduate students must obtain written departmental approval prior to enrollment.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem Sets (6 assignments) | 30% |
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Research Paper & Presentation | 25% |
| Final Examination | 20% |
| Participation & Seminar Discussion | 5% |

**Problem Sets:** Six written assignments distributed throughout the semester will require students to construct formal proofs, design automata, analyze language properties, and solve complexity problems. Collaboration is permitted on conceptual discussion but all written submissions must be individual.

**Midterm Examination:** A closed-book, in-person examination covering formal language theory, automata models, and early computability material. A formula and notation reference sheet will be provided.

**Research Paper & Presentation:** Students will produce a 4,000–6,000 word original research paper engaging with an advanced topic in formal systems or computation theory. Topics must be approved by the instructional team by Week 5. Students will present findings in a seminar format during the final two weeks of the term.

**Final Examination:** A comprehensive closed-book examination covering all course material, with emphasis on integration of concepts across modules. The final examination will include both short-answer proof questions and extended analytical problems.

**Participation & Seminar Discussion:** Active, informed engagement in weekly seminars and discussion sections is expected at the 490-level. Students will be evaluated on the quality and consistency of their contributions.
