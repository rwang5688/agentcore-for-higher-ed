## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the mathematical foundations of information theory and its applications in modern communication and data systems. Students will explore entropy, channel capacity, data compression, and error-correcting codes, examining how information can be reliably transmitted and stored in the presence of noise and uncertainty. Emphasis is placed on both theoretical derivations and practical coding schemes used in contemporary engineering systems.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Define and apply fundamental concepts of information theory, including entropy, mutual information, and channel capacity, to analyze communication systems
- Design and evaluate lossless and lossy data compression algorithms based on source coding theorems
- Construct and analyze error-detecting and error-correcting codes, including Hamming, cyclic, and convolutional codes, for use in noisy channel environments
- Apply Shannon's theorems to determine theoretical performance limits of communication channels and assess the efficiency of practical coding strategies
- Evaluate the trade-offs between coding rate, redundancy, and reliability in real-world data transmission and storage applications

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Problem Sets (6–8 assignments) | 25% |
| Coding Implementation Project | 20% |
| In-Class Quizzes and Participation | 10% |

**Problem Sets:** Weekly or biweekly assignments requiring students to solve analytical and computational problems covering entropy calculations, channel modeling, and code construction.

**Coding Implementation Project:** A semester-long project in which students design, implement, and benchmark a compression or error-correction coding scheme of their choosing. A written technical report and an oral presentation to the class are required components. Projects may be completed individually or in pairs with prior instructor approval.

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Closed-book written examinations covering theoretical derivations, proofs, and applied problem solving. A formula sheet provided by the instructor will be permitted.

**Quizzes and Participation:** Short in-class assessments administered at the discretion of the supervising instructor to reinforce key concepts and encourage consistent engagement with course material.
