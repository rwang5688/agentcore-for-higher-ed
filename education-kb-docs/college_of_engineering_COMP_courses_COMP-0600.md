### Course Description

This course provides a rigorous examination of the principles underlying the design, analysis, and optimization of algorithms, with emphasis on correctness proofs, asymptotic complexity, and the selection of appropriate algorithmic strategies for complex computational problems. Students engage with advanced topics including graph algorithms, dynamic programming, randomized algorithms, approximation schemes, and complexity theory, situating these within both theoretical and practical problem-solving contexts. The course equips graduate-level students with the analytical frameworks necessary to evaluate algorithmic trade-offs and develop efficient, scalable solutions in research and professional engineering settings.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of COMP-0600, students will be able to:

- **Analyze and prove** the correctness and computational complexity of algorithms using formal techniques including loop invariants, recurrence relations, and asymptotic notation (Big-O, Θ, and Ω).
- **Design and implement** advanced algorithmic strategies — including divide-and-conquer, greedy methods, dynamic programming, and network flow — and justify the appropriateness of each approach for a given problem class.
- **Evaluate algorithmic trade-offs** with respect to time complexity, space complexity, and practical performance, including the analysis of best-, average-, and worst-case scenarios.
- **Classify computational problems** within the framework of complexity theory, demonstrating understanding of the classes P, NP, NP-complete, and NP-hard, and applying reduction techniques to establish problem hardness.
- **Develop and critically assess** approximation algorithms and randomized algorithms for problems where exact or deterministic solutions are computationally intractable.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in COMP-0600 is evaluated through the following components:

**Problem Sets (25%)** — Six written problem sets distributed throughout the semester, requiring students to analyze algorithms, construct formal proofs of correctness and complexity, and solve novel algorithmic problems. Collaboration is permitted within the boundaries outlined in Peculiar U's Academic Integrity Policy; all written work must be independently produced.

**Midterm Examination (20%)** — A closed-book, in-class examination covering algorithmic design strategies, asymptotic analysis, sorting and graph algorithms, and introductory complexity theory. Students are permitted one handwritten reference sheet (both sides, letter size).

**Algorithm Design Project (30%)** — A semester-long project completed in teams of two to three students. Teams will identify a computationally challenging real-world problem, design and implement one or more algorithmic solutions, perform empirical and theoretical analysis of performance, and present findings in both a written technical report (minimum 15 pages, ACM format) and a 20-minute oral presentation delivered to the class during the final two weeks of the semester.

**Final Examination (25%)** — A comprehensive take-home examination administered during the final examination period, covering all course material with emphasis on NP-completeness, approximation algorithms, and randomized methods. Students have 72 hours to complete and submit the examination independently.
