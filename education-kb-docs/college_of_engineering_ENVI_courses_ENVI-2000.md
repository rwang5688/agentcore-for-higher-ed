## Course Description

### Course Description

ENVI-2000 provides a comprehensive introduction to the science of seismology, examining the geological processes that generate earthquakes and the wide-ranging consequences these events have on natural and human systems. Students will explore plate tectonics, fault mechanics, and seismic wave propagation alongside the social, structural, and environmental impacts of seismic activity. The course draws on real-world case studies to connect theoretical principles with observable phenomena, equipping students with a foundational understanding of one of Earth's most consequential natural processes.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the geological and tectonic mechanisms responsible for earthquake generation, including fault types, stress accumulation, and the role of plate boundaries
- Describe the properties and behaviour of seismic waves and interpret basic seismograph data to determine earthquake magnitude and location
- Evaluate the physical, environmental, and societal impacts of seismic events, including ground rupture, liquefaction, tsunamis, and infrastructure failure
- Analyse historical and contemporary earthquake case studies to identify patterns of risk, vulnerability, and community resilience
- Critically assess current earthquake monitoring technologies, early warning systems, and mitigation strategies in both developed and developing regional contexts
