This course provides students with a comprehensive grounding in the fundamental principles, methodologies, and professional practices that define the engineering discipline at an advanced undergraduate level. Students will examine the full engineering design cycle, from problem identification and systems analysis through to prototype development and iterative refinement, with emphasis on interdisciplinary collaboration and ethical responsibility. The course situates engineering practice within broader societal, environmental, and economic contexts, equipping students to approach complex real-world challenges with rigorous analytical and creative thinking.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Apply systematic engineering design processes to identify, define, and formulate solutions to open-ended technical problems within given constraints
- Demonstrate proficiency in the use of quantitative and qualitative analytical methods relevant to core engineering sub-disciplines, including structural, mechanical, and systems analysis
- Evaluate engineering solutions critically with respect to safety, sustainability, ethical standards, and socioeconomic impact
- Communicate technical findings and design decisions effectively through written reports, oral presentations, and visual documentation appropriate to professional engineering standards
- Work productively within multidisciplinary teams to produce integrated engineering outputs, demonstrating an understanding of collaborative project management principles

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENGI-4800 must have successfully completed the following, or equivalent courses approved by the Department of Engineering:

- **ENGI-2200** – Fundamentals of Engineering Mathematics II
- **ENGI-2450** – Engineering Statics and Dynamics
- **PHYS-2310** – Physics for Engineers II
- **ENGI-3100** – Systems Thinking and Engineering Analysis

Students who have not completed the above prerequisites but believe they hold equivalent preparation are encouraged to consult the Department of Engineering's undergraduate academic advisor prior to enrollment. Exceptions require written departmental approval.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-4800 will be evaluated through the following components:

- **Midterm Examination (20%)** – A written examination covering foundational concepts in engineering analysis, design methodology, and problem-solving frameworks addressed in the first half of the course. The examination includes both short-answer and extended-response questions.
- **Final Examination (25%)** – A comprehensive written examination administered during the official final assessment period, covering all course content with emphasis on integration across topics.
- **Design Project – Phase I: Problem Definition and Feasibility Report (15%)** – Students working in assigned teams will select a real-world engineering challenge and submit a formal written feasibility report detailing problem scope, constraints, stakeholder analysis, and preliminary design directions.
- **Design Project – Phase II: Prototype and Technical Presentation (25%)** – Teams will develop and present a working prototype or detailed technical model of their proposed solution, accompanied by an oral presentation and supporting technical documentation. Evaluation criteria include design quality, technical rigor, teamwork, and communication clarity.
- **Weekly Problem Sets (10%)** – Assigned weekly exercises reinforcing quantitative and analytical skills covered in lectures and readings. Problem sets are submitted electronically and graded on accuracy and methodology.
- **Participation and Professional Conduct (5%)** – Active and constructive engagement in lectures, workshops, and team activities, assessed on a continuous basis by instructors throughout the semester.

*Note: Students must achieve a passing grade in both the written examination component and the design project component independently in order to pass the course overall.*
