## Course Description

*Department of Aeronautics, College of Engineering*

---

This introductory course provides students with a foundational understanding of the principal systems that constitute a modern aircraft, including propulsion, avionics, hydraulics, electrical systems, and structural components. Students will explore how these systems function independently and interact as an integrated whole to ensure safe, efficient flight operations. The course emphasizes real-world applications and industry standards to prepare students for further study in aeronautical and aerospace engineering disciplines.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Identify and describe the major systems of a fixed-wing aircraft, including propulsion, flight control, landing gear, hydraulic, and electrical systems
- Explain the fundamental physical and engineering principles that govern the operation of core aircraft systems
- Recognize how individual aircraft systems are integrated and how failures in one system may affect others
- Interpret basic technical diagrams, schematics, and system documentation commonly used in aviation engineering contexts
- Demonstrate awareness of aviation safety regulations and the role of airworthiness standards in aircraft systems design

### Prerequisites

There are no formal prerequisites for AERO-0100. However, students are strongly encouraged to have completed secondary-level coursework in Physics and Mathematics prior to enrollment. A foundational interest in mechanical or engineering principles is recommended.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 25% |
| Final Examination | 30% |
| Systems Analysis Lab Reports (×3) | 20% |
| Group Design & Integration Project | 15% |
| Participation & In-Class Quizzes | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations** consist of multiple-choice, short-answer, and diagram-labelling questions covering lecture content and assigned readings.

**Lab Reports** require students to document and analyse the operation of assigned aircraft systems using provided schematics and case study materials, submitted following each designated laboratory session.

**Group Design & Integration Project** tasks small student teams with producing a systems overview report for a simplified aircraft concept, demonstrating understanding of how multiple systems must be coordinated to meet performance and safety requirements. An oral presentation is required.
