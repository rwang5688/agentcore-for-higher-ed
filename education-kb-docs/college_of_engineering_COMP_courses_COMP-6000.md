## Course Description

### Course Description

COMP-6000: Design in Computing is an advanced graduate-level course that explores the theoretical foundations and practical methodologies of computational design, bridging the gap between human-centered principles and complex software and systems engineering. Students will critically examine design paradigms as they apply to algorithms, interfaces, architectures, and sociotechnical systems, developing a sophisticated understanding of how purposeful design decisions shape the functionality, usability, and ethical implications of computing artifacts. This course prepares students to lead design-driven initiatives in professional and research contexts, integrating emerging technologies with rigorous design thinking frameworks.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically evaluate and apply established design methodologies — including user-centered design, participatory design, and agile design frameworks — within the context of complex computing systems
- Synthesize knowledge from software engineering, human-computer interaction, and systems thinking to produce coherent, well-justified design solutions for real-world computational problems
- Analyze the ethical, social, and cultural dimensions of design decisions in computing, articulating the broader impact of technological artifacts on diverse populations
- Design, prototype, and iteratively refine a significant computing artifact or system, demonstrating mastery of professional design documentation and evaluation practices
- Communicate design rationale and technical specifications effectively to both technical and non-technical stakeholders through written reports, visual documentation, and formal presentations

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-6000 must have satisfied the following prerequisites prior to registration. Exceptions require written departmental approval.

- **COMP-4200: Advanced Software Engineering** or equivalent graduate-level software development coursework
- **COMP-4150: Human-Computer Interaction** or demonstrated proficiency in interaction design principles
- **COMP-3800: Algorithms and Computational Theory** or equivalent
- Graduate standing within the College of Engineering or a related discipline, or explicit permission of the supervising instructor
- Proficiency in at least one high-level programming language and familiarity with prototyping tools (e.g., wireframing software, simulation environments)

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through a combination of individual and collaborative assessments designed to reflect both theoretical understanding and applied competency.

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Design Portfolio Project | 35% | A semester-long, iterative design project in which students conceptualize, develop, document, and evaluate a computing artifact or system. Deliverables include a design brief, prototype, evaluation report, and final presentation. |
| Critical Design Review Paper | 20% | An individually authored academic paper (5,000–7,000 words) critiquing an existing computing system through one or more design frameworks studied in the course. |
| Midterm Examination | 15% | A written examination assessing students' command of core design theories, vocabulary, and methodological frameworks covered in the first half of the course. |
| Weekly Design Critiques | 15% | Short structured written responses to assigned readings and case studies, submitted prior to each seminar session and discussed in class. |
| Collaborative Studio Workshop | 15% | A team-based design sprint culminating in a group presentation and peer-review session, assessing collaborative problem-solving and iterative design practices. |

*Note: All written submissions must adhere to the Peculiar University Academic Integrity Policy. Late submissions will incur a penalty of 5% per calendar day unless a formal extension has been granted.*
