### Course Description

This course examines the principles and practices of sustainable living through an interdisciplinary lens, integrating concepts from environmental science, engineering, economics, and social equity to address the challenges of resource consumption in contemporary society. Students will critically evaluate personal, community, and systemic approaches to reducing ecological footprints while maintaining quality of life, exploring topics such as renewable energy adoption, sustainable food systems, waste reduction, and green building design. Emphasis is placed on translating theoretical knowledge into actionable strategies applicable at individual, household, and community scales.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **Analyse** the environmental, economic, and social dimensions of sustainability and articulate how these dimensions interact within complex human-natural systems.
- **Evaluate** the lifecycle impacts of consumer choices, infrastructure decisions, and land-use practices on local and global ecological systems.
- **Apply** quantitative and qualitative tools — including carbon footprint calculators, life cycle assessment frameworks, and sustainability indicators — to assess the sustainability of real-world scenarios.
- **Design** a practical, evidence-based sustainable living plan that addresses energy use, food consumption, water management, and waste generation within a defined community or household context.
- **Communicate** sustainability challenges and proposed solutions effectively to diverse audiences, including technical stakeholders, policymakers, and the general public.

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in ENVI-3000 must have completed the following, or obtained instructor consent:

- **ENVI-1100** Introduction to Environmental Science, *or* **ENVI-1150** Foundations of Earth Systems
- **ENVI-2200** Human Impacts on the Environment *or* **SUST-2050** Principles of Sustainability
- Completion of at least 60 credit points toward any undergraduate degree programme
- A working familiarity with basic data interpretation and scientific reasoning, as demonstrated through prior coursework in any natural or applied science discipline

Students who have not completed the stated prerequisites but possess relevant professional or practical experience in environmental management, engineering, or related fields are encouraged to contact the course coordinators to discuss eligibility.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Sustainability Audit Project | 25% | A structured individual assessment in which students conduct a comprehensive audit of their own living environment, quantifying resource inputs and waste outputs across energy, water, food, and material consumption categories. A written report of 1,800–2,200 words is required. |
| Community Solutions Case Study | 20% | Working in groups of three to four, students will research and present a real-world sustainable living initiative, critically evaluating its outcomes, scalability, and transferability. Deliverables include a 15-minute oral presentation and a 1,200-word analytical summary. |
| Midterm Examination | 20% | A closed-book written examination covering core theoretical frameworks, key terminology, and applied concepts from the first half of the course. The examination includes short-answer and extended-response questions. |
| Sustainable Living Design Portfolio | 25% | A culminating project in which students develop a detailed, evidence-based sustainable living plan for a specified household or small community scenario. The portfolio must integrate energy, food, water, and waste strategies and include supporting data, cost-benefit analysis, and implementation timelines. Approximately 3,500–4,000 words. |
| Participation and Weekly Reflections | 10% | Students are required to submit eight brief weekly reflection entries (300–400 words each) responding to designated readings and current events in sustainability. Active participation in seminars and workshop discussions is also assessed. |

*All written work must adhere to the Peculiar University Academic Integrity Policy. Late submissions will be penalised at 5% of the total mark per calendar day unless an extension has been approved in advance.*
