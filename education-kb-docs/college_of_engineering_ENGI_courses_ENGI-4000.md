## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-4000: High-Speed Digital Design provides a rigorous examination of the principles, methodologies, and practical techniques required to design reliable digital systems operating at high frequencies. Students will investigate signal integrity, transmission line effects, electromagnetic compatibility, and advanced logic design strategies as they apply to contemporary high-speed digital circuits and systems. Through a combination of theoretical analysis and hands-on laboratory work, students will develop the competencies necessary to engineer robust, high-performance digital solutions for real-world applications.

---

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyze and mitigate signal integrity issues including reflections, crosstalk, ground bounce, and power supply noise in high-speed digital systems
- Apply transmission line theory to model and design PCB traces, interconnects, and termination schemes for high-frequency digital signals
- Evaluate electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) requirements and implement design strategies to ensure compliance in high-speed digital environments
- Design and simulate high-speed digital circuits using industry-standard electronic design automation (EDA) tools, interpreting simulation outputs to validate design performance
- Develop and apply testing and measurement methodologies, including the use of oscilloscopes, time-domain reflectometry (TDR), and spectrum analyzers, to characterize high-speed digital systems

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrollment in ENGI-4000:

- **ENGI-3210: Digital Systems Design** – Foundational knowledge of digital logic, sequential and combinational circuits, and hardware description languages (HDLs)
- **ENGI-3150: Electromagnetics and Field Theory** – Understanding of electromagnetic wave propagation, Maxwell's equations, and field behavior in transmission media
- **ENGI-3320: Electronic Circuits II** – Proficiency in analog and mixed-signal circuit analysis, including transistor-level design and frequency-domain methods
- **MATH-2410: Differential Equations** – Mathematical foundations for analyzing time-varying signals and system behavior

Students who have not completed the above prerequisites must obtain written permission from the course instructor or department advisor prior to registration. This course requires registration approval; students should contact the Department of Engineering directly to initiate the registration process.

### Assessment Methods

Student performance will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% |
| Final Examination | 25% |
| Laboratory Reports (6 submissions) | 20% |
| Design Project (individual) | 25% |
| Problem Sets / Homework Assignments | 10% |

**Examinations:** Two written examinations will assess students' theoretical understanding of high-speed digital design principles, signal integrity analysis, and transmission line behavior. Examinations are closed-book; one double-sided formula reference sheet is permitted.

**Laboratory Reports:** Students will complete six structured laboratory sessions using EDA simulation tools and physical prototyping equipment. Each session culminates in a formal written report documenting methodology, results, and critical analysis.

**Design Project:** Students will undertake an individual capstone design project requiring the complete design, simulation, and documentation of a high-speed digital subsystem meeting a defined set of specifications. A written technical report and oral presentation are required at project completion.

**Problem Sets:** Weekly or bi-weekly assignments reinforce lecture material and develop quantitative problem-solving skills in circuit analysis and signal integrity modeling.
