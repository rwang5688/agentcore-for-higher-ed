This course provides a foundational introduction to the principles and applications of electronic circuits, components, and systems. Students will explore the behavior of passive and active electronic elements, including resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, and transistors, within both direct current (DC) and alternating current (AC) contexts. Emphasis is placed on the analysis, design, and practical implementation of basic electronic circuits, preparing students for advanced study in electrical and electronic engineering disciplines.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyse and solve DC and AC circuit problems using fundamental laws, including Kirchhoff's Voltage and Current Laws and Ohm's Law, applied to resistive and reactive networks
- Identify and describe the operating characteristics of core electronic components such as diodes, bipolar junction transistors (BJTs), and field-effect transistors (FETs), and apply these to basic circuit configurations
- Design and evaluate simple amplifier, rectifier, and filtering circuits, demonstrating an understanding of gain, frequency response, and signal conditioning
- Apply systematic methods of circuit analysis, including nodal analysis, mesh analysis, and Thévenin/Norton equivalent techniques, to solve multi-element electronic networks
- Conduct hands-on laboratory experiments to construct, test, and troubleshoot electronic circuits, interpreting measurement data using standard instrumentation such as oscilloscopes and multimeters

### Assessment Methods

Student performance in ENGI-2400 will be evaluated through the following components:

| Assessment | Weight |
|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 25% |
| Final Examination | 30% |
| Laboratory Reports (×6) | 20% |
| Circuit Design Project | 15% |
| Problem Sets / Assignments (×5) | 10% |

**Midterm and Final Examinations:** Both examinations are closed-book and will assess students' ability to apply theoretical concepts and analytical techniques to unseen circuit problems. The final examination is cumulative and will be held during the official examination period.

**Laboratory Reports:** Six supervised laboratory sessions are scheduled throughout the semester. Students are required to submit a formal lab report for each session within one week of the practical. Reports must include data tables, circuit diagrams, analysis, and a critical discussion of results.

**Circuit Design Project:** Working individually or in pairs, students will design, simulate, and present a functional electronic circuit addressing a specified engineering problem. A written technical report and a brief oral presentation to the class are required. Further details will be provided in the project brief issued in Week 4.

**Problem Sets:** Five graded assignment sets will be distributed at regular intervals throughout the semester. These are to be completed individually and are intended to reinforce lecture content and prepare students for examinations.
