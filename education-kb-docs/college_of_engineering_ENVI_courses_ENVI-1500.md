## Course Description

### Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive examination of the science, data, and projections underlying contemporary global warming, equipping students with the analytical tools necessary to critically evaluate climate forecasts and their societal implications. Drawing on atmospheric science, thermodynamics, and environmental systems theory, students will explore the physical mechanisms driving climate change alongside the computational and observational methods used to model future conditions. Emphasis is placed on bridging scientific literacy with real-world policy and engineering contexts, preparing students to engage meaningfully with one of the defining challenges of the modern era.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Explain the fundamental physical and chemical processes that drive global warming, including greenhouse gas dynamics, radiative forcing, and feedback mechanisms within Earth's climate system.
- Critically evaluate the methodologies, assumptions, and uncertainties inherent in climate modelling and long-range forecasting, including the interpretation of IPCC scenario pathways.
- Analyse observational datasets — including temperature records, sea-level measurements, and atmospheric CO₂ concentrations — using appropriate quantitative and statistical techniques.
- Assess the projected regional and global impacts of continued warming across ecosystems, infrastructure, and human populations, with particular attention to differential vulnerability.
- Communicate complex climate science findings clearly and accurately to both technical and non-technical audiences through written and oral formats.

### Prerequisites

Students must have successfully completed the following prior to enrolment:

- **ENVI-2210: Introduction to Environmental Systems** or equivalent
- **MATH-2150: Statistics and Data Analysis for Scientists** or equivalent
- **PHYS-2010: Fundamentals of Atmospheric Physics** or consent of the department

Students without a formal background in physical sciences are encouraged to consult with the course instructors before registering. A working familiarity with spreadsheet software or introductory programming (e.g., Python or R) is strongly recommended, as data analysis components of the course will require basic computational competency.

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Midterm Examination | 20% | In-class written exam covering weeks 1–6; includes short-answer and analytical questions |
| Final Examination | 25% | Comprehensive; includes data interpretation, scenario analysis, and essay-format questions |
| Climate Data Analysis Project | 25% | Individual project requiring students to analyse a real-world observational dataset, identify trends, and produce a written technical report (2,500–3,500 words) |
| Policy Brief Assignment | 15% | Group assignment in which teams of 3–4 students prepare a 1,500-word policy brief addressed to a specified governmental or engineering stakeholder body |
| Weekly Participation & Response Papers | 15% | Eight short response papers (400–600 words each) submitted throughout the semester, engaging critically with assigned readings and current events in climate science |

Late submissions are subject to a deduction of 5% per business day unless an extension has been formally approved by the relevant instructor. Academic integrity standards of Peculiar U apply to all submitted work.
