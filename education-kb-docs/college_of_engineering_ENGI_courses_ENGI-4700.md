## Course Description

### Course Description

ENGI-4700 provides a comprehensive introduction to the design, analysis, and implementation of digital integrated circuits, with emphasis on CMOS technology and logic families. Students will explore the fundamental principles governing the behaviour of digital circuits at the transistor level, including timing, power dissipation, noise margins, and signal integrity. The course bridges theoretical circuit analysis with practical design methodologies relevant to modern semiconductor applications.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Analyse the electrical characteristics and switching behaviour of CMOS and bipolar transistor-based digital circuits at the device and gate level
- Design and evaluate combinational and sequential digital circuits, assessing performance trade-offs in speed, power, and area
- Apply established methodologies for logic synthesis, circuit simulation, and verification using industry-standard electronic design automation (EDA) tools
- Interpret and utilise timing diagrams, propagation delay models, and fan-out constraints in practical circuit design scenarios
- Demonstrate a working understanding of standard cell libraries, interconnect effects, and the impact of process variation on integrated circuit performance

### Assessment Methods

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Laboratory Reports (6 submissions) | 20% |
| Midterm Examination | 22% |
| Design Project (group, 2–3 students) | 28% |
| Final Examination | 30% |

**Laboratory Work:** Six structured laboratory sessions require students to simulate, implement, and critically evaluate digital circuit designs using EDA software. Each lab report must include simulation outputs, analytical commentary, and a reflection on observed versus theoretical performance.

**Midterm Examination:** A closed-book examination covering Weeks 1–7 of course material, including transistor-level circuit analysis, logic families, and static/dynamic characteristics of CMOS gates. A single hand-written formula sheet (one side, A4) is permitted.

**Design Project:** Teams of two to three students will design, simulate, and present a functional digital integrated circuit subsystem of moderate complexity (e.g., an arithmetic logic unit, memory array interface, or clock distribution network). Deliverables include a written technical report and a formal oral presentation to the instructional team during the final two weeks of the semester.

**Final Examination:** A comprehensive closed-book examination covering all course material. Students must achieve a minimum grade of 50% on the final examination to pass the course, regardless of cumulative standing.
