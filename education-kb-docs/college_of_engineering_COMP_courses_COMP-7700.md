## Course Description

### Course Description

This advanced seminar provides an in-depth exploration of the theoretical foundations and applied methodologies underlying human-computer interaction, with emphasis on user-centered design, cognitive ergonomics, and emerging interface paradigms. Students will critically engage with current research literature, examining how computational systems are designed, evaluated, and iteratively refined to enhance usability, accessibility, and user experience across diverse populations. The course situates HCI within broader sociotechnical contexts, encouraging students to consider ethical, cultural, and psychological dimensions of interactive system design.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Critically analyze and synthesize primary research literature in human-computer interaction, identifying methodological strengths, limitations, and theoretical contributions
- Design and conduct rigorous usability evaluations using both quantitative and qualitative methods, including heuristic analysis, think-aloud protocols, and controlled user studies
- Apply established and emerging HCI frameworks — including activity theory, distributed cognition, and value-sensitive design — to the analysis and critique of interactive systems
- Develop prototypes and interface designs informed by iterative, user-centered design principles, incorporating accessibility standards and inclusive design practices
- Communicate original research findings and design recommendations effectively through written scholarship and formal oral presentation to a professional audience

---

### Prerequisites

Students enrolling in COMP-7700 must have successfully completed the following, or receive explicit written approval from the supervising instructor:

- **COMP-5210: User Interface Design and Evaluation** or equivalent with a grade of B or higher
- **COMP-5340: Research Methods in Computing** or demonstrated proficiency in qualitative and quantitative research methodologies
- **COMP-4480: Software Engineering Principles** or equivalent foundational software development coursework
- Graduate standing in Computer Science, Information Systems, or a closely related discipline, or departmental consent

Students are strongly encouraged to have prior exposure to introductory statistics and basic psychological principles of cognition and perception before enrolling.

### Assessment Methods

| Assessment Component | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Seminar Participation & Discussion Leadership | 15% | Active, substantive engagement in weekly seminar discussions; each student leads at least one session |
| Critical Reading Responses | 10% | Short written responses (500–700 words) submitted before eight designated sessions, demonstrating critical engagement with assigned readings |
| Usability Study & Report | 20% | A structured usability evaluation of an existing system, including study design, data collection, analysis, and a formal written report |
| Research Seminar Paper | 30% | An original, peer-review-quality research paper (6,000–8,000 words) presenting a novel investigation or theoretical contribution within HCI |
| Design Prototype Project | 20% | A team-based iterative design project culminating in a functional prototype, documented design rationale, and a formal in-class presentation |
| Final Oral Presentation | 5% | Presentation of the research seminar paper findings to the class and invited faculty |

Late submissions will be penalized at a rate of 5% per calendar day unless a documented accommodation has been arranged in advance with the course instructor.
